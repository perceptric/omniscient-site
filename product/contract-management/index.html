Approvers, CC and viewers

Every recipient has a role. Signers fill in their fields and sign. An approver, like the client's finance lead, approves first or declines with a reason, and the signers get their links once every approver has approved. CC gets the signed copy and never a link. A viewer gets a link that only shows the document. A new version keeps the same approvers, CC and viewers.

Questions

Questions

Something else? hello@tryomniscient.com

Can I bring my PandaDoc documents?

Yes. Connect PandaDoc in Import, filter your documents by status and pick the ones you want, or take every document with a status, up to 500 at a time. Each one comes in with its status, dates, recipients, owner, folder, tags and value. Completed documents bring their signed PDF, and Otto reads them for their dates and terms. A document that's already in Omniscient is skipped, and imported history never starts a workflow or sends an email.

Which files can I upload?

PDF or Word (.docx) files up to 25 MB each. Upload one, or drop in many at once, even as a .zip, and give each file its status, Draft or Signed, with its signed date and expiration date. Each upload to a contract becomes a new version, and earlier versions stay as they were. Otto reads the signed ones.

Does Omniscient write or draft contracts?

No. Omniscient has no contract templates and never writes, drafts or suggests contract text. Your contracts are the files you upload or import, and only people on your team edit them. Omniscient reads them to find where people sign, and Otto reads them to suggest dates and terms, each with its quote, for a person to confirm.

Can I edit the contract in Omniscient?

Yes, before it goes out. A Word file opens in an editor with a toolbar for text styles, fonts, colors, lists and links, and the + adds text, an image, a table, a page break or a signature block. A PDF keeps its pages, and you add text boxes, images and white covers or turn, move, delete and add pages. The words under a white cover stay in the PDF. When you're done, save your edits as a new version. Your upload stays version 1. A contract that's already out goes back to a draft with Edit document. Otto never writes or suggests contract text.

How does Omniscient find where people sign?

It reads the tags other tools put in contracts: DocuSign-style tags like \s1\, Dropbox Sign and Adobe Sign tags, and {{sig:1}}. Without tags, it reads the signature blocks, such as "By:", "Name:" and "Date:" lines, in one column or several. Scanned PDFs are read too. Signers are filled in from the opportunity's people and you, and you review each field before you send.

How does signing work?

Send in three steps: recipients, fields, then delivery and settings. Send to up to ten people, in order or all at once. Each signer opens a private link, confirms their name and email, agrees to sign electronically and types or draws a signature. Reminders go out on day 2 and day 5, and links expire after 30 days unless you change that. When everyone has signed, you get a signed PDF with a certificate page and an audit trail.

What are Approver, CC and Viewer?

They're recipient roles, picked in the first step of sending. A Signer fills in their fields and signs. An Approver is someone outside your team, like the client's finance lead, who approves the document before anyone signs or declines with a reason. The signers get their links once every approver has approved. CC gets the signed copy and never a link. A Viewer gets a link that only shows the document. Only signers have fields.

What if the other side wants changes?

They can comment on their link or send back a changed file. A Word file's tracked changes show with who made each one and when. The new file becomes the next version and goes back to review. When you send a version back, they see which sections were added, taken out or changed.

Who can see a contract?

Everyone in the workspace, unless you change it. A contract can be private to its owner and the people you add, or shared with a group like Legal or Finance. Workspace owners and admins see every contract. Signing and review links are stored only as hashes, and you can ask signers for a one-time code by email first.

What does Otto read?

Parties, effective date, term, end date, auto-renewal, notice period, renewal notice date, value, payment terms, termination rights and key obligations, each with a quote and a page from the file. A suggestion whose quote isn't in the file is dropped, and nothing is saved until a person confirms it.
