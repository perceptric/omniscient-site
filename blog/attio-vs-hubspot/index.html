If you're weighing Attio vs HubSpot, you're choosing between a CRM you shape yourself and a platform that already has a hub for each team. Both start free, and both put email sequences on their second paid plan.

We build Omniscient, a CRM with LinkedIn outreach, and we wrote the importers that bring data over from both tools. We read both pricing pages on November 3, 2026, plus the sites of the LinkedIn tools each one leans on. We haven't run either tool with a live sales team, so the features here come from what each vendor publishes.

Here's our short answer:

  • Pick HubSpot if marketing brings in most of your leads and you want forms, ads, email campaigns and the sales pipeline on one shared record.
  • Pick Attio if your process doesn't fit a standard pipeline and you want to design your own objects, or if you sell mostly by email to people you know.
  • Look at Omniscient if most of your deals start in LinkedIn conversations, since neither Attio nor HubSpot sends LinkedIn messages for you.

What Attio does well

Attio is a flexible CRM that builds records from your email and calendar and lets you shape objects and fields around how your business works.

These parts stood out to us on its pricing page:

  • Email and calendar sync come on every plan, and even Free fills in first interaction, last interaction and connection strength for each contact.
  • Objects grow with the plan, from 3 on Free to 5 on Plus and 12 on Pro, and custom objects and formula attributes start on Pro.
  • Workflows, AI agents, the API, webhooks, an App SDK and an MCP server come on every plan, Free included.

Honestly, Attio is where we'd point a team whose process doesn't look like a standard sales pipeline, such as an investor tracking deal flow. That freedom works best when someone on your team enjoys designing objects, so plan for their time.

What HubSpot does well

HubSpot is a customer platform that runs marketing, sales and service tools on one shared CRM. Its pricing page lists over 2,000 apps you can connect.

On the Sales Hub pricing page, these are the parts we'd look at first:

  • Starter adds 500 calling minutes, HubSpot's Notetaker for meetings and up to 50 workflows with limited triggers and actions.
  • Professional adds sequences, forecasting, conversation intelligence, lead scoring and up to 300 workflows.
  • The mobile app comes on every plan, and teammates who only need to look can have view only seats at no extra cost.

In our view, HubSpot's biggest strength is that marketing and sales share one record. Each contact carries an Original Traffic Source, the first known web source that brought them in, and our HubSpot importer keeps it as the lead's source.

Admittedly, the bill climbs at Professional, where a required $1,500 onboarding fee comes on top of the seats.

Data model: custom objects in Attio and HubSpot

Attio treats objects as something you design, starting from people, companies and deals. You then add your own, such as investors or partners, and link them the way your business works.

HubSpot starts from fixed objects that most sales teams share, like contacts, companies and deals. That shape fits a standard sales process from the first day.

We saw this difference up close when we wrote importers for both tools:

  • HubSpot's deal stages come with a win probability for each stage, which a forecast can use right away.
  • Attio's deal stages arrive as a list of statuses without probabilities, so our importer reads the won stage from Attio's celebration setting and the lost one from its name.
  • HubSpot keeps leads and customers in one contact list sorted by lifecycle stage, so our importer brings contacts at a lead stage over as leads.

Frankly, if your reps live on the phone, HubSpot has the edge here, since calling minutes start on Starter and Attio's pricing page doesn't list a dialer.

LinkedIn outreach in Attio and HubSpot

Neither Attio nor HubSpot sends LinkedIn invitations or messages for you, which matters if LinkedIn is where your first conversations happen. Each one leans on a LinkedIn tool you buy separately, and these are the ones you'll meet first:

HubSpot puts its LinkedIn steps inside sequences on Professional and Enterprise, and they need a Sales Navigator subscription of their own. Its knowledge base calls them task reminders, and completing one opens Sales Navigator on the contact's record so you can send the InMail or connection request yourself.

LinkedIn Sales Navigator is a prospecting software from LinkedIn that adds advanced search filters and InMail to your account. Core and Advanced both offer a free trial to members without a paid LinkedIn plan who haven't had a free trial in the past year.

Attio teams that prospect on LinkedIn connect a separate tool through Attio's app marketplace instead.

lemlist is a sales engagement software that runs email and LinkedIn steps in one campaign. Attio built the lemlist app, which adds Attio people to a campaign, and LinkedIn steps start on lemlist's Multichannel plan.

HeyReach is a LinkedIn outreach software that sends from many accounts at once and charges per sender. Its Attio app, built by Neon Deer Data Labs, pushes Attio people into HeyReach campaigns. Helpfully, it can also log accepted invitations and replies on each person's timeline.

Whichever you pick, the LinkedIn tool is a second bill, and its pacing settings shape how safe your account stays. We built our own sending engine this year and spent most of our care on pacing, because LinkedIn's invitation limit page doesn't publish an exact number.

At the normal pace, our engine follows these rules for each sender:

  • A new sender starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day until it reaches your limit.
  • Each day's total lands somewhere between 80% and 100% of that limit.
  • It sends only in the sender's working hours, with a lunch break and a random gap of 3 to 12 minutes between actions.
  • It views a person's profile 1 to 4 hours before it sends the invitation.
  • It drops to half pace for 3 days when LinkedIn rate limits the sender or the past week's acceptance falls under 20%, then eases back over 3 more days.

Pacing lowers the risk without removing it, since LinkedIn's prohibited software page says automation tools can get an account restricted. Our guide to automating LinkedIn outreach without getting restricted goes deeper.

HubSpot's credits help page adds that free users can't use credits. Without extra capacity, features that use credits pause until your next reset date.

Thankfully, both let you put limits on credit use. HubSpot sets a limit per feature, and Attio's credits guide lets admins cap Ask Attio's share of the pool.

Attio vs HubSpot pricing

Both charge per seat, with lower prices for yearly billing. These are the plans Attio's pricing page and HubSpot's Sales Hub pricing page listed on November 3, 2026:

What moves the bill more than the headline price:

  • HubSpot's $7 and $10 Starter prices are new customer discounts on a listed $20, so ask what a seat costs at renewal.
  • HubSpot Professional asks for a yearly commitment even when you pay monthly, plus the $1,500 onboarding fee.
  • Attio's Plus plan stops at 10 seats, so a team of 11 or more starts on Pro.

Moving between Attio and HubSpot

Happily, Attio's migration guide moves HubSpot data into any paid Attio plan through Import2, which it says is free for Attio users. It covers companies, people, deals, custom objects, notes, tasks and files.

Going the other way, HubSpot's CRM page lists CRM import on its free plan. Two lessons from building our own importers carry over to either move:

  • Our importer matches deal owners by email address, so we'd invite your team with the same emails before any import.
  • If your Attio workspace relies on custom objects, check your HubSpot plan first, since HubSpot lists them on Enterprise.

Attio vs HubSpot: which one to choose

Start with where your first conversation with a buyer usually happens:

Choose HubSpot if:

  • Your leads come from forms, ads and email campaigns, and marketing and sales should share one record.
  • Your reps make calls and want calling minutes and transcripts in the CRM.

Choose Attio if:

  • Your process needs objects of your own, like investors or partners.
  • You sell mostly by email to people you know and want sequences on Pro.

Choose Omniscient if:

  • Most of your deals start in LinkedIn conversations.
  • You want a teammate to approve LinkedIn messages before they go out.

FAQs

Is Attio cheaper than HubSpot?

At the first paid plan, HubSpot costs less for new customers: Sales Hub Starter starts at $7 a seat billed yearly and lists at $20, while Attio Plus costs $35 billed yearly. At the plans with sequences, Attio Pro costs $79 billed yearly and HubSpot Professional costs $90, plus a $1,500 onboarding fee paid once.

Does Attio have a free trial?

Yes. Attio's pricing page offers a 14 day trial of its Pro plan, and its Free plan covers up to 3 seats.

Which is better for a startup, Attio or HubSpot?

Attio fits a startup that wants to shape its own data model, and its startup program offers up to 80% off for eligible teams. HubSpot fits a startup whose leads come from marketing, and it runs its own HubSpot for Startups program.
