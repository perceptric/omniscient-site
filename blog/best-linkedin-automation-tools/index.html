Sending LinkedIn invitations by hand eats the hours you meant to spend on calls, and the wrong automation tool can get your account restricted. Here are the 13 best LinkedIn automation tools for 2026, with what each one does well and where it falls short, so you can pick before you pay for a seat.

We built the LinkedIn sending engine inside Omniscient, so we judge every tool on the two things that decide how an account holds up: how it paces your sending, and what happens after someone replies.

If you're short on time, here's where we'd start:

  • Pick Omniscient if you want LinkedIn outreach and a CRM that fills itself in, in one place.
  • Pick HeyReach if you run many LinkedIn accounts for clients and want sending rotated between them.
  • Pick GetSales.io if you run LinkedIn and email for many clients and want to pay per account.
  • Pick SalesRobot if you want an AI to answer first replies and book meetings for you.
  • Pick Meet Alfred if you also reach buyers on X.
  • Pick lemlist or La Growth Machine if you want LinkedIn and cold email in the same sequence.
  • Pick Waalaxy if you're a solo seller trying automation for the first time.
  • Pick Linked Helper if you want the lowest price for a single account and don't mind a desktop app.

How we chose these LinkedIn automation tools

A LinkedIn automation tool sends invitations and messages from your account on a schedule, then follows up when people accept. We judged each one on the same settings we had to decide for our own engine:

  • How much control you get over pacing, such as daily ranges, random gaps, working hours and a warmup for new accounts.
  • Where the tool runs, and whether your schedule keeps going with your laptop closed.
  • Whether email can sit in the same sequence, for teams that need both channels.
  • What one LinkedIn account costs, and how the price grows with seats.
  • Where replies go, and how they reach your CRM.

Honestly, every one of these tools carries some risk, ours included. Section 8.2 of the LinkedIn User Agreement bans "bots or other unauthorized automated methods" for adding contacts or sending messages, and members who use them "risk having their accounts restricted or shut down" (LinkedIn Help).

We left out tools built mainly for pulling data out of LinkedIn, since this list is about sending.

Where LinkedIn automation tools run: cloud, desktop or browser

Start here with any tool, because it decides when your account is active and from which machine.

  • Cloud tools sign in as you from the vendor's servers, so your schedule keeps running with your computer off. Ten of the 13 tools here work this way, and Dux-Soup sells it as one of its two plans.
  • Desktop apps run on your own computer and send only while that machine is on. Linked Helper works like this, and so does Dux-Soup's local plan.
  • Browser extensions work through your own browser and LinkedIn session, and LinkedIn bans extensions that automate activity on its site.

lemlist is the one tool here that sits in the browser: its LinkedIn steps need its Chrome extension. Interestingly, Waalaxy went the other way and now runs 100% in the cloud, with its Chrome extension gone from the Chrome Web Store.

Whichever kind you pick, make sure it sends in your own working hours and time zone.

01Omniscient

Omniscient is a CRM with LinkedIn outreach built for small B2B sales teams that want prospecting and their pipeline in one place. Otto, our AI agent, writes a message for each lead, and nothing goes out until your team approves it. The CRM fills itself in from your email and calendar, so every reply lands next to a full record.

Try Omniscient free →

Key features

  • Approval before sending: Approve each person's messages yourself, or let going live approve everyone who's ready in one step.
  • Human pacing: Actions go out in working hours that shift up to 20 minutes a day, with a lunch break and random 3 to 12 minute gaps.
  • Automatic warmup: A new account starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 more each day until it reaches your limit.
  • Daily totals that move: Each day's real total lands between 80% and 100% of your limit, which keeps the daily count from looking scripted.
  • Slowdown on warning signs: Low acceptance or a LinkedIn rate limit drops the account to half pace for 3 days, then it eases back over 3 more.
  • Scout lead search: Describe your buyer in a sentence, and Scout finds matching people and scores each one out of 100 on fit and timing.
  • A reason to write: Otto builds each message from your template and a current signal, such as hiring, a new role, a recent post or funding.
  • Fact flags on drafts: A draft that adds a number, money or a past conversation the evidence doesn't back goes to review before anyone can approve it.

Pros

  • Outreach and CRM together: Replies land in one Inbox with the person's CRM record beside the thread, so your team stops copying conversations between tools.
  • Careful from day one: Warmup and automatic slowdowns are on by default, and a brand new account starts slowly without any setup on your side.
  • Replies stop everything: A reply ends that person's outreach in every campaign, and opt outs stay out of every later import.
  • Low entry price: Seats that can send start at $19 a month billed yearly, below most tools on this list.

Cons

  • LinkedIn only: There are no cold email steps, so teams that live in email will be happier with lemlist or La Growth Machine.
  • Lower ceilings: Each account is capped at 40 invitations a day and 200 a week, which agencies pushing volume will outgrow.
  • No phone dialer: Calls aren't a step in the sequence, and teams that sell by phone will need a separate dialer.
  • Newer product: Admittedly, Omniscient is younger than most tools here and has a shorter public track record.

Price and plans

You can try Omniscient free for 14 days with no credit card. Paid seats, billed yearly (monthly billing costs 20% more):

  • Free: $0 for teammates who view and share records.
  • Starter: $19 per seat a month.
  • Pro: $49 per seat a month.
  • Business: $99 per seat a month.

02HeyReach

HeyReach is a cloud LinkedIn automation tool built for agencies and sales teams that send from many LinkedIn accounts at once. It rotates sending between as many senders as you connect, and every reply lands in one inbox. You pay per sender, and teammates, VAs and clients cost nothing.

Key features

  • Sender rotation: Connect many LinkedIn accounts to one campaign, and HeyReach spreads the sending across them automatically.
  • Unified Inbox: Replies from every sender land in one inbox, where you can answer for a colleague or send a voice note.
  • Hard weekly cap: Each sender tops out at 200 invitations a week, and sending pauses until Monday once LinkedIn's limit is hit.
  • Email through partners: Native Instantly, Smartlead and EmailBison integrations add email steps to a LinkedIn campaign.
  • HubSpot sync: Connections, messages and replies sync to HubSpot automatically, while Clay, RB2B and Sales Navigator feed in leads.
  • Proxy per sender: On Growth, each sender gets a dedicated residential proxy that no other account shares.

Pros

  • Scales with accounts: On Growth, adding a sender is the only thing that raises the bill, which suits agencies adding clients.
  • Firm limits: The weekly cap is a hard stop, so an eager campaign can't burn through an account's invitations.
  • Useful trial: 14 days with no credit card covers up to 3 LinkedIn accounts, enough to test rotation properly.

Cons

  • Email lives elsewhere: Email steps need an Instantly, Smartlead or EmailBison account on top of HeyReach.
  • Bring your own proxies: On Agency and Unlimited, sourcing and managing proxies becomes your job.
  • Calendar week reset: Once LinkedIn's limit is hit, sending waits for Monday, while our rolling seven day count gives room back a little each day.

Price and plans

You can try HeyReach free for 14 days with no credit card, on up to 3 LinkedIn accounts. Paid plans:

  • Growth: $79 per sender a month, or $64 a month billed yearly.
  • Agency: $999 a month, with white label and your own proxies.
  • Unlimited: $2,999 a month for up to 300 senders under a fair use policy.
  • Done for you: priced on a call with their sales team.

03Expandi

Expandi is a cloud LinkedIn automation tool built for sales teams and lead generation agencies that want careful pacing out of the box. Each account gets its own IP address and a warmup, and smart sequences mix LinkedIn actions with email follow ups that react to what each person does.

Key features

  • Dedicated IP per account: Every LinkedIn account sends from its own country based IP address instead of a shared pool.
  • Profile warmup: New profiles start with a small number of actions, and you can set your own limits during the warmup.
  • Limit ranges: Daily limits run as ranges instead of fixed numbers, which varies your activity from day to day.
  • Smart sequences: An if/action builder branches on behavior and mixes connection requests, messages and email follow ups in one flow.
  • Global Smart Inbox: Replies from every account sit in one inbox you can sort with labels, tags and notes.
  • CRM and webhooks: Native CRM integrations and webhooks push your LinkedIn outreach data into the tools you already use.

Pros

  • Safety without extras: Every Business seat includes its own IP address and a warmup, with no proxy to buy separately.
  • Email in the same flow: Email follow ups sit inside the LinkedIn sequence, and one tool covers both channels.
  • Ready for agencies: The Agency plan adds white label, client reports and roles for coworkers and clients.

Cons

  • Pricier single seat: $99 a month costs more than Waalaxy, Dripify or Meet Alfred for one account.
  • Short trial: Frankly, 7 days is tight for judging a warmup, since our own engine takes 5 days to climb from 5 to 15 invitations a day.
  • Paid visuals: Video and GIF personalization run through paid integrations such as Sendspark and Hyperize, on top of the seat.

Price and plans

You can try Expandi free for 7 days with every feature included. Paid plans:

  • Business: $99 a month billed monthly, or $79 a month billed yearly.
  • Agency: priced on a call with their sales team, for 10 or more seats.

Price and plans

Every Waalaxy plan comes with a free trial of 14 days. Prices per user:

What happens when someone replies

Surprisingly, handling a reply took us as much care as sending, and it ended up as five steps. A follow up that lands after someone already answered reads as automated, and it gives that person a reason to report you.

These are the questions about replies we'd ask in any trial:

  • Does a reply stop that person in every campaign, or only in the one they answered?
  • Does a reply that arrives just before a scheduled message stop that message?
  • Does an opt out keep them out of next month's import too?
  • Does the reply reach your CRM on its own, or does someone copy it over?

When your outreach tool and CRM are separate, test the sync before you launch. HeyReach, Dripify and Waalaxy sync with HubSpot, while Dux-Soup, Linked Helper, GetSales.io and SalesRobot's Advanced plan connect to HubSpot, Salesforce and Pipedrive.

How to choose a LinkedIn automation tool

LinkedIn keeps its invitation limit private. Reaching it may restrict your invitations, and that restriction typically lasts one week. That makes a tool's settings matter more than any number on a pricing page.

Here's what we'd test in any free trial:

  1. Set a daily range instead of one fixed number, and confirm the weekly cap is a hard stop.
  2. Connect a fresh account and see whether it warms up on its own.
  3. Set your working hours and time zone, and confirm nothing goes out overnight.
  4. Look for an automatic slowdown when acceptance drops or LinkedIn pushes back.
  5. Ask what happens when a send times out. Our engine never retries a send whose outcome is unknown, because a missing message is easy to fix by hand and a doubled one can't be taken back.
  6. Decide whether someone on your team approves messages before they go out.

FAQs

What is the best LinkedIn automation tool in 2026?

For outreach and a CRM in one place, pick Omniscient, and for agencies with many accounts, HeyReach or GetSales.io. lemlist suits teams mixing LinkedIn with email, and Linked Helper has the lowest price for a single account.

Is LinkedIn automation against LinkedIn's rules?

Yes. Section 8.2 of LinkedIn's User Agreement bans bots and other unauthorized automated methods that add contacts or send messages. Careful pacing lowers the risk of a restriction, but no tool can remove it.

How many connection requests can I send per day with a LinkedIn automation tool?

LinkedIn keeps its number private. Omniscient defaults to 15 invitations a day and 80 a week, and lemlist recommends about 20 a day for a healthy account, with roughly 100 per rolling week as the ceiling.

Are cloud LinkedIn automation tools safer than desktop apps?

A cloud tool keeps your working hours while your computer is off, and a desktop app sends only while that machine runs. LinkedIn's ban on bots covers both, so pacing settings matter more than where the tool runs.

Can a LinkedIn automation tool send cold email too?

Most tools here can. Expandi, Dripify, lemlist, La Growth Machine, Skylead and GetSales.io put email in the same sequence, and HeyReach adds it through Instantly, Smartlead or EmailBison. Omniscient keeps to LinkedIn.

How much do LinkedIn automation tools cost?

Entry plans in this guide start at $15 per LinkedIn account a month for Linked Helper, with Waalaxy and Omniscient from $19. The agency plans at HeyReach and Skylead cost $999 a month, with Skylead's priced as a first year offer.
