Linked Helper is a LinkedIn automation software that runs as a desktop app on your own computer, or on a server you rent. Plenty of people search for it as "LinkedIn Helper," so if you're comparing LinkedIn Helper alternatives, this guide covers the same tool.

You probably like its low price per account and its long list of LinkedIn actions. You might be looking anyway because the computer has to stay on, or because your team has outgrown one person's desktop.

We build the LinkedIn sending engine inside Omniscient, so we had to decide where our own sending runs. This guide sorts every alternative by that same choice, with Linked Helper's own pages open next to our engine's settings.

Prices come from each vendor's own pricing page, read in November 2026. We haven't run campaigns in these tools, so we compare what vendors publish with what we learned building our own engine.

What Linked Helper does well

Here's what Linked Helper's pricing page and homepage list today, which is what you'd leave behind:

  • Standard with local data storage costs $15 per LinkedIn account a month on a monthly term, and a 12 month term takes 45% off.
  • It covers InMails, endorsements and invitations to events, groups and company pages, on free accounts as well as Sales Navigator and Recruiter.
  • A built in CRM keeps tags, notes and custom fields on each contact, and native integrations include HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Zoho CRM and Close.
  • Its AI drafts wait in a queue so you can review and edit them before they send.
  • The trial gives 14 days of full Pro access with no card, and nothing renews on its own.

Honestly, that's a lot of tool for $15 per account a month. If your computer is on all day anyway and one person runs outreach, staying with Linked Helper is a fair choice.

Why people look for a Linked Helper alternative

Each reason below comes from Linked Helper's own pages:

  • The machine has to be on with the app running to send, and its FAQ says that's true "on either version." For sending around the clock, it suggests renting a VPS, which you pay for separately.
  • Local storage keeps your data on one device, so a teammate or a second laptop needs Cloud storage, listed from $16.50 a month against $8.25 for Local. An existing workspace can't move between the two yet.
  • Standard caps advanced actions such as event invitations and sending contacts to your CRM at 20 a day, and a Standard license can move to Pro only after it expires.
  • We didn't find email steps on its pages, and its email finder hands addresses to a cold email tool such as Instantly.
  • Safety setup is left to you, including binding a proxy to each profile and ramping up a new account by hand.

Desktop or cloud: the choice behind every Linked Helper alternative

Linked Helper and STACKED Local send from a machine you keep on. lemlist needs its Chrome extension for LinkedIn steps, and the rest, ours included, send from the vendor's servers.

We see the trade like this:

On your machineOn the vendor's servers
Sends with your laptop closedOnly from a VPS you rent and keep runningYes
The network LinkedIn seesYour own connection, or a proxy you bind to each profileThe vendor's, such as a dedicated IP on Dripify and Expandi
Where your LinkedIn session livesOn your machine, and Linked Helper says "never on our servers"With the vendor, since it acts for you
Who keeps it runningYou, including restarts and updatesThe vendor

For us, the first row settled it, because our engine sends inside your working hours with a lunch break and random gaps between actions. A laptop asleep in a bag can't keep that plan, so our engine runs in the cloud.

The trade we accepted is the network: desktop tools send from your own connection, while Omniscient sends from the cloud without a dedicated IP. If keeping every action on your own network matters most to you, Linked Helper or STACKED Local is the closer fit.

LinkedIn doesn't publish an exact invitation limit. Its help page says only that an account that reaches it "may be temporarily restricted from sending invitations," and that this "typically lasts one week." Every number above is a vendor's own ceiling, ours included, and our guide to the LinkedIn connection request limit explains where the common ones come from.

The 13 best Linked Helper alternatives in 2026

01Omniscient

Omniscient is a CRM and LinkedIn outreach software that drafts a message for each lead and sends only what your team approves. It keeps sending with your laptop closed, and each CRM record fills in from your email and calendar. A LinkedIn reply then sits beside your emails and meetings with that person.

Every campaign follows these rules:

  • A new account warms up from 5 invitations a day, and a profile view goes out 1 to 4 hours before each invitation unless you switch it off.
  • A reply stops that person's outreach in every campaign, and a person can be in only one live campaign at a time.
  • You approve each person's messages, or everyone ready at once when you go live, and editing a message after approval clears that approval.
  • Otto, our agent, writes versions of your own pitch and holds any version that adds a claim you didn't make, such as a number or a result.

Admittedly, Omniscient covers far fewer actions than Linked Helper: sequences use connection requests, messages, profile views and likes, with no InMails, groups, events or email steps. If you need those, Linked Helper or STACKED will suit you better.

With one LinkedIn account per campaign and no white label, an agency running many client senders is better served by HeyReach.

Paid seats start at $19 per seat a month, billed yearly, and monthly billing costs 20% more. The free trial lasts 14 days with no credit card, and the LinkedIn Outbound and CRM pages cover the rest.

02STACKED by Dux-Soup

STACKED is a LinkedIn automation software, powered by Dux-Soup, that runs either as a desktop app on your own machine or on its own servers. Its pricing page calls it "One product, two ways to run it," which makes it the closest match to Linked Helper on this list.

  • Local costs $55 per seat a month, or $41.25 billed yearly, and runs as a desktop app for Mac and Windows.
  • Cloud costs $99 per seat a month, or $74.17 billed yearly, and "runs continuously, no need to keep a machine on."
  • Both include unlimited concurrent campaigns with up to 12 actions, such as InMails and endorsements, and reply detection that pauses a campaign.
  • Native integrations include Salesforce, Pipedrive, HubSpot and Slack, with Zapier and Make libraries.

A STACKED seat costs more than a Linked Helper license, so it fits when running in the cloud matters more to you than the lowest price. Its pricing page doesn't state a trial length, so ask before you plan a test.

03Waalaxy

Waalaxy is a LinkedIn outreach software that sends invitations and follow ups from the cloud, with monthly invitation quotas on every plan. Pro costs €19 per user a month, billed yearly, for 300 invitations a month, and Advanced costs €49 for 800.

  • Business, at €69, adds cold email sequences and 500 email finder credits a month.
  • Its inbox is an optional add on at €20 a month.
  • Every plan lists CRM sync and a 14 day free trial.
  • Its help center says Waalaxy is "now 100% cloud" and the Chrome extension is gone.

Happily, a fixed monthly quota keeps a beginner's volume modest from the first day. If Linked Helper's settings felt like more than you need, Waalaxy is the simplest cloud tool here, though email waits for Business.

04Dripify

Dripify is a LinkedIn automation software that runs drip campaigns of LinkedIn and email steps on its own servers. Pro costs $79 per user a month, or $59 billed yearly, and Advanced costs $99, or $79 billed yearly.

  • Every plan includes email steps and 100 email finder credits a month, so a found address stays in the same sequence.
  • Its FAQ lists a dedicated IP "tied to your account, in your location," and says nothing depends on "your machine being on."
  • Each seat connects one LinkedIn account, and Pro lists a HubSpot integration.
  • The free trial lasts 7 days with no card.

Email in the same sequence is the main reason to pick Dripify over Linked Helper. Its plans allow up to 75 connection requests a day, so we'd keep your daily setting well below that.

05Expandi

Expandi is a LinkedIn automation software that runs outreach sequences on its own servers, with a dedicated IP address based in your country. Business costs $99 a month, or $79 billed yearly, and includes an automatic profile warmup.

  • Sequences mix connection requests, messages and email follow ups, with If/Action conditions.
  • Its integrations page lists native HubSpot, Salesforce and Pipedrive integrations, plus webhooks, Zapier and an API.
  • The Agency plan starts at 10 seats, is priced through sales and adds white label use.
  • The free trial lasts 7 days.

Expandi suits you if you want the warmup and a dedicated IP handled for you, with no server to rent.

06HeyReach

HeyReach is a LinkedIn automation software that runs outreach from many LinkedIn accounts at once, with every reply in one unified inbox. Growth costs $79 per sender a month, or $64 billed yearly, and its FAQ says the per sender plan gives each sender a dedicated residential proxy.

  • Agency costs $999 a month for 25 senders, adds white label and asks you to bring your own proxies.
  • Email runs through native Instantly and Smartlead integrations.
  • The free trial lasts 14 days with no card and up to 3 LinkedIn accounts.

For an agency, one flat price covers 25 cloud senders, and our guide to LinkedIn automation tools for agencies compares the agency plans.

07La Growth Machine

La Growth Machine is a sales automation software that runs multichannel sequences on LinkedIn, email, calls and X. Billed yearly, Basic costs €60 per identity a month for LinkedIn and email, and Pro at €120 adds calls and one inbox for both.

  • An identity is one profile running campaigns, and you don't need one to answer replies.
  • The Ultimate plan adds X and a native HubSpot and Pipedrive sync.
  • The free trial lasts 14 days with no card and covers 1 identity.

Frankly, we'd look here first if your reps call as often as they message, since Linked Helper's sequences stay on LinkedIn.

08Skylead

Skylead is a LinkedIn automation and cold email software that runs smart sequences with if/else conditions across both channels. The All-in-one plan costs $100 per seat a month and includes unlimited email accounts with 100,000 emails a month, plus unlimited email warmup.

  • Its blog says a new account starts in warmup mode by default, which is the ramp Linked Helper asks you to plan by hand.
  • Its guide to LinkedIn automation tools calls Skylead "a cloud-based solution," so campaigns shouldn't depend on your computer staying on.
  • Agency is listed as a first year promotion at $999 a month for 25 seats, or $1,999 for unlimited seats, with white label and one inbox for client conversations.

The pricing page mentions a 7 day free trial. A Skylead seat costs far more than a Linked Helper license, and the price covers cold email at volume. That makes it a fit if you already pay for a separate email tool next to Linked Helper, since Linked Helper's email finder hands addresses to one anyway.

09lemlist

lemlist is a sales engagement software that finds leads and runs campaigns across email, LinkedIn and calls. LinkedIn steps come with the Multichannel plan at $109 per user a month, or $87 billed yearly, because the $69 Email plan has no LinkedIn automation.

  • LinkedIn steps cover profile visits, follows, invitations, text and voice messages, and InMail.
  • Multichannel adds 5 senders per user, a call dialer and SMS, with WhatsApp as an add on.
  • HubSpot, Salesforce and Pipedrive integrations and a lead database of more than 650 million contacts come with every plan.
  • The FAQ lists a 14 day trial with Multichannel features apart from in app calls, plus 200 credits.

Its help center says the Chrome extension "is required for LinkedIn automation". It doesn't say whether Chrome has to stay open, so ask before you count on sending with your laptop shut. We'd pick lemlist when email leads and LinkedIn is the second touch.

10SalesRobot

SalesRobot is a LinkedIn and cold email automation software that runs in the cloud and can answer replies with an AI appointment setter. Basic costs $59 per LinkedIn account a month, or $39 billed yearly, with one active campaign and most daily actions capped at 20.

  • Advanced costs $79, or $59 billed yearly, and adds unlimited campaigns, A/B tests and native HubSpot, Pipedrive and Salesforce sync.
  • Each LinkedIn account gets a dedicated IP, and a reply stops the automated LinkedIn messages to that lead.
  • Email automation is billed apart, at $15 per email account a month.
  • The free trial lasts 14 days with no credit card.

The AI setter answers leads for you, which is the opposite of how we built Omniscient, where nothing goes out without approval. If handing first replies to an AI is what you want, SalesRobot is built around it.

Advanced allows up to 75 connection requests a day, five times the 15 our Normal pace sends. Its own disclaimer says using it "may not comply with LinkedIn's User Agreement." That caution applies to every tool on this list, ours included.

11Meet Alfred

Meet Alfred is a LinkedIn automation software that runs campaigns across LinkedIn, email and X from its own cloud. LinkedIn Only costs $29 per user a month, billed yearly, and the FAQ says there's "no browser extension, nothing installed on your machine."

  • Multi-Channel costs $49 per user billed yearly and adds email, X, and the InMail and Sales Navigator inbox.
  • Agency is listed at $39 per user billed yearly, below Multi-Channel, for 3 or more seats, and adds white label plus native Salesforce and HubSpot integrations.
  • Campaigns run with "smart delays and randomized, human-like activity patterns," and you can change daily limits and activity windows.
  • The free trial lasts 7 days with no credit card.

The pricing page shows only yearly prices, so check the monthly rate before you plan a short test. For a Linked Helper user who wants a low price and nothing to install, LinkedIn Only is the plan to try first.

12GetSales.io

GetSales.io is a LinkedIn and email outreach software that runs each account in its own cloud session and gives every client a workspace. LinkedIn costs $69 a month for 1 account, or $195 a month for 5, with unlimited actions and a managed ISP proxy.

  • New accounts warm up gradually, and alerts flag an account whose health drops.
  • Email Outreach is a separate module at $59 a month for 100 inboxes, with a dedicated server and IP.
  • White label comes with the Business setup, listed from $500 a month in resources.

Luckily for agencies, each client keeps its own LinkedIn and email accounts and its own inbox, so one client's replies never mix with another's. Inside a workspace, every reply gets a label and a suggested next step. The homepage names HubSpot, Pipedrive and Salesforce as places to send leads, so confirm your CRM link works during the 7 day trial.

13Kanbox

Kanbox is a LinkedIn prospecting software that puts lead imports, sequences, a smart inbox and Kanban pipelines in one place. Starter costs $20 a month, or $12 billed yearly, for one campaign at a time and 300 lead imports a month.

  • Essential costs $45 per LinkedIn account a month, or $30 billed yearly, with 3 campaigns at once and team access.
  • Pro costs $80, or $50 billed yearly, and adds webhooks and the API.

Every plan is listed as cloud based, and the comparison table promises "Cloud 24/7 (runs without your computer)," though the FAQ also calls Kanbox "a Chrome extension that installs in 2 clicks." The free trial lasts 15 days with no credit card.

Of every tool here, Kanbox Starter sits closest to Linked Helper's price. Surprisingly, its Kanban board could stand in for a light CRM if you sell on your own.

We found no native CRM integrations or email steps on its site, only an email finder, so replies stay in Kanbox unless you connect the webhooks.

How to switch from Linked Helper without risking your account

Your LinkedIn account carries its pending invitations and recent activity into the new tool, so the first week needs the most care. Here's the order we'd follow:

  1. 01
    Close Linked Helper before the new tool starts

    Its safety page says LinkedIn flags a session used "from multiple locations" at once, so never run both on one account.

  2. 02
    Export what you've built

    Download your contacts to CSV, plus your messaging history if you're on Pro, so the new tool can skip people who already heard from you.

  3. 03
    Load your do not contact list

    Add current customers and anyone who asked not to hear from you before the first campaign.

  4. 04
    Withdraw only invitations you've given up on

    LinkedIn says you can't invite the same person again for up to three weeks after a withdrawal.

  5. 05
    Restart slower than you ended

    Treat the first week like a warmup even on an old account, and raise limits only while acceptance stays above 20%, the line where our own engine slows down.

  6. 06
    Test the reply path

    Ask a colleague to answer a test message and check that it lands where your team works.

Fortunately, our engine covers part of step 2 on its own. It checks the relationship on LinkedIn before every invitation, so someone still holding your old Linked Helper invitation isn't sent a second one.

LinkedIn's page on invitation restrictions names invitations "ignored, left pending, or marked as spam" as one cause and suspected automation as another, which applies to every tool here, ours included. Our guide to the safest LinkedIn automation tools covers what else to check.

FAQs

What is the best alternative to Linked Helper?

It depends on why you're leaving. Omniscient fits small teams that want outreach and a CRM together, and HeyReach suits agencies with many senders. STACKED by Dux-Soup is the closest match if you'd like a desktop and a cloud option.

Is LinkedIn Helper the same as Linked Helper?

Yes. The product's name is Linked Helper, and many people search for it as LinkedIn Helper. Its site also says it "is neither an official product of LinkedIn nor affiliated with LinkedIn Corporation."

Is there a cheaper alternative to Linked Helper?

Not on the same billing term. Linked Helper's Standard plan costs $15 per LinkedIn account a month, with 45% off a 12 month term. Kanbox comes closest at $20 a month, or $12 billed yearly, and Waalaxy and Omniscient start at €19 and $19 a month billed yearly.

Which Linked Helper alternatives keep sending with my computer off?

Every alternative in this guide except STACKED Local and lemlist runs on the vendor's servers, going by each vendor's own pages. lemlist's help center says its Chrome extension is required for LinkedIn automation, so ask lemlist whether Chrome has to stay open.

Is Linked Helper safe to use?

Linked Helper keeps your session on your own machine and lets you set limits, hourly pacing and a proxy for each profile. Its homepage also says these measures "cannot eliminate the risk of LinkedIn restrictions," which holds for every tool here, ours included.
