## What Dripify does well

Dripify keeps its [plans](https://dripify.com/pricing/) short and puts a lot into the first one:

- Pro costs $79 per user a month, or $59 billed yearly, with unlimited drip campaigns, LinkedIn and email steps in one sequence, a dedicated inbox and a native HubSpot integration.
- Advanced costs $99 per user a month, or $79 billed yearly, and adds team management, A/B tests, step analytics, lead tagging, evergreen leads and advanced LinkedIn protection.
- Campaigns run on Dripify's servers from a dedicated IP in your location, so your laptop can stay closed.
- Free LinkedIn accounts work fine, so you don't need Premium or Sales Navigator to start.
- The 7 day trial gives you Advanced features with no card, and nothing charges when it ends.

Users rate it 4.7 out of 5 across 477 reviews on [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/215082/Dripify/reviews/). Honestly, email steps on the entry plan are a real plus, since our own tool has none and Waalaxy saves cold email for its top plan. If each rep runs LinkedIn and email from one account, staying with Dripify is a fair call.

## Why teams look for a Dripify alternative

Most of the reasons come down to how Dripify bills seats and where your replies end up:

- Each seat connects one LinkedIn account, so a team of five reps pays for five seats.
- Team management, A/B tests and advanced LinkedIn protection come only with Advanced, so a growing team ends up on the higher price.
- HubSpot is the one native CRM, and that sync runs one way, from Dripify to HubSpot. Salesforce, Pipedrive and the rest connect through Zapier, Make or webhooks.
- Sequences mix LinkedIn and email steps, so a rep who also calls or reaches buyers on X keeps those in another tool.
- Its ceiling of 75 connection requests a day sits well above the 40 our own engine allows at its highest setting.

## Dripify's daily limits next to ours

Dripify publishes its [daily limits](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/8201637-how-dripify-daily-limits-safeguard-your-linkedin-account) for each LinkedIn account type, and you move them with sliders. The table puts them beside our engine's settings:

| Setting | Dripify | Omniscient |
|---|---|---|
| Connection requests a day | Up to 75 on every plan and LinkedIn account type | 15 at the Normal pace, 40 at the highest setting |
| Pending and weekly caps | Fewer than 800 pending invitations, as Dripify advises | 80 a week at the Normal pace, never more than 200 over a rolling 7 days |
| Timing | Random delays inside the working hours you set | Your hours, with the start and end moving by up to 20 minutes each day, a lunch break and 3 to 12 minute gaps at the Normal pace |
| Day to day totals | A range of 1 to 10 around your setting, so 60 with a range of 5 sends 55 to 65 | Between 80% and 100% of your limit, drawn fresh each day |
| A new account | Activity Control adjusts limits for gradual growth | Starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day |
| Slowing down on its own | Activity Control weighs your connections, pending invitations, account type and past activity | Half pace for 3 days after a rate limit, or when under 20% of a week's invitations are accepted once 20 have gone out, then back to full over 3 more days |

![Bar chart of connection requests a day: Dripify's daily quota on Pro and Advanced is 75. Omniscient's highest setting is 40, the Fast pace 25, the Normal pace and default 15, the Careful pace 10, and the first day of a warmup 5. LinkedIn doesn't publish its own limit.](/assets/blog/dripify-alternatives/daily-limits.svg)

LinkedIn keeps its invitation limit private. Reaching it can restrict your invitations, and LinkedIn says that restriction ["typically lasts one week."](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550555) Every number above is a ceiling a vendor picked for itself, ours included.

That week is why we default to 15 a day. A restriction stops every invitation until it lifts, and a slower day costs you far less than a lost week. We also capped our sliders at 40 a day and 200 a week, the top of the range our app suggests for Sales Navigator accounts.

Thankfully, Dripify's sliders go down as easily as they go up. If you stay, these are the changes we'd make first:

- Move the connection request slider well below 75, closer to a pace you'd keep by hand.
- Set lower limits yourself during the 7 day trial, since Dripify says the trial "doesn't have any restrictions on daily processing limits."
- Turn on Activity Control, which Dripify recommends "for the best account safety."
- Keep pending invitations under 800, as Dripify's [weekly limit article](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/6074973-i-am-out-of-connection-requests-on-linkedin-what-should-i-do-now) advises.

Our guide to the [LinkedIn connection request limit](/blog/linkedin-connection-request-limit/) explains where the common numbers come from.

## How to choose a Dripify alternative

Start with whatever pushed you away from Dripify, then trial the two or three tools that fix it. These questions settle most choices:

- **Count your senders first:** Per seat pricing suits a team of three, while 25 or more client accounts make flat plans like HeyReach Agency or Skylead Agency the better deal.
- **Decide where email lives:** If you liked having email in the same sequence, Expandi, Skylead, La Growth Machine, lemlist and Closely keep it there.
- **Follow a reply:** Ask where a LinkedIn reply lands and who copies it into your CRM, because a native sync or a CRM like Omniscient removes that step.
- **Read the safety defaults:** Look for a warmup that runs on its own, daily invitations under 40 and a pace that changes from day to day.
- **Use the trial on a real list:** A 7 day trial leaves little time to see who accepts, so launch your test campaign on day one.

Admittedly, no tool here fixes every reason at once, ours included, so rank your reasons before you start a trial.

## How to move off Dripify without risking your account

A LinkedIn account carries its pending invitations and recent pace into the new tool, so the first week after a switch needs the most care. We'd make the move in this order:

```steps
1. Pause every Dripify campaign: Your plan stays active until the end of the period you've paid for, so stop campaigns yourself before you cancel from Billing.
2. Save who you've contacted: Keep a list of everyone in your Dripify campaigns, so the new tool can skip people who already heard from you.
3. Load your do not contact list: Add customers and anyone who asked not to hear from you before the first campaign.
4. Withdraw only what you've given up on: Dripify suggests keeping pending invitations under 800, and LinkedIn says a withdrawn invitation can't go to the same person again for up to three weeks.
5. Restart slower than you ended: Treat the first week like a warmup even on an old account, and raise limits only once acceptance holds above 20%.
6. Test the reply path: Ask a colleague to answer a test message and confirm that it lands on the right CRM record.
```

Fortunately, a careful tool covers part of step 2. Our engine confirms the relationship on LinkedIn before every invitation, so someone still holding your old Dripify request isn't sent a second one.

LinkedIn's page on [invitation restrictions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012) names invitations left pending or ignored as one cause. It also names suspected use of an automation tool, which covers every tool on this list, ours included. Our guide to the [safest LinkedIn automation tools](/blog/safest-linkedin-automation-tools/) covers the other settings worth a look in your first month.
