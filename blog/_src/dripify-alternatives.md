## What Dripify does well

Here's what Dripify's [pricing page](https://dripify.com/pricing/) lists today, and what you'd leave behind:

- Pro costs $79 per user a month, or $59 billed yearly, with unlimited drip campaigns, LinkedIn and email steps, a dedicated inbox and a native HubSpot integration.
- Advanced costs $99 per user a month, or $79 billed yearly. It adds team management, A/B tests, step analytics, lead tagging and Activity Control, which adjusts your daily limits on its own.
- Both plans list a dedicated IP tied to your account and location, and campaigns run on Dripify's servers while your computer is off.
- The free trial lasts 7 days with Advanced features and no card, and nothing is charged when it ends.

Honestly, email steps on the entry plan are a real plus, since our own tool has none and Waalaxy saves cold email for Business. If each rep runs LinkedIn and email drips from one account, staying with Dripify is a fair choice.

## Why teams look for a Dripify alternative

Each reason comes from Dripify's own pages, so you can check it against your setup:

- Each seat covers one LinkedIn account, and the pricing FAQ says it plainly: "A team of five reps needs five seats."
- Team management, A/B tests and Activity Control come only with Advanced, so a growing team pays the Advanced price.
- HubSpot is the one native CRM on its [integrations page](https://dripify.com/integrations/), and that sync runs one way. Salesforce, Pipedrive and the rest connect through Zapier, Make or webhooks.
- Sequences cover LinkedIn and email, and we didn't find calls, SMS, X or WhatsApp steps on its pages.
- Its daily quota of up to 75 connection requests sits well above the 40 our own engine allows at its highest setting.

## Dripify's daily limits next to ours

Dripify's help center publishes its [daily limits](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/8201637-how-dripify-daily-limits-safeguard-your-linkedin-account) for each LinkedIn account type, and you move them with sliders. Here they are beside ours:

| Setting | Dripify, from its own pages | Omniscient, from our engine |
|---|---|---|
| Connection requests a day | Up to 75 on every plan and LinkedIn account type | 15 at the Normal pace, 40 at the highest setting |
| Invitations a week | No number listed; its help center suggests keeping pending invitations under 800 | 80 at the Normal pace, never more than 200 over a rolling 7 days |
| Timing | Random delays between actions | Your hours, with start and end moved up to 20 minutes each day, a lunch break and 3 to 12 minute gaps at the Normal pace |
| Day to day totals | A range of 1 to 10 around your setting, so 60 with a range of 5 sends 55 to 65 | Between 80% and 100% of your limit, drawn fresh each day |
| A new account | Activity Control, on Advanced, adjusts limits for "a gradual growth" | Starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day |
| Slowing down on its own | Activity Control weighs your connections, pending invitations, account type and past activity | Half pace for 3 days after a rate limit, or when under 20% of a week's invitations are accepted once 20 have gone out, then back to full over 3 more days |

![Bar chart of connection requests a day: Dripify's daily quota on Pro and Advanced is 75. Omniscient's highest setting is 40, the Fast pace 25, the Normal pace and default 15, the Careful pace 10, and the first day of a warmup 5. LinkedIn doesn't publish its own limit.](/assets/blog/dripify-alternatives/daily-limits.svg)

LinkedIn doesn't publish its invitation limit, and its [help page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550555) says only that reaching it may restrict your invitations. That restriction "typically lasts one week." Every number above is a vendor's own ceiling, ours included.

That week is why we default to 15 a day. A restriction stops every invitation until it lifts, and a slower day costs you far less than a lost week. We also capped our sliders at 40 a day and 200 a week, the top of the range our app suggests for Sales Navigator accounts.

Thankfully, Dripify's sliders go down as easily as they go up. If you stay, these are the changes we'd make first:

- Move the connection request slider well below 75, closer to a pace you'd keep by hand.
- Set lower limits yourself during the 7 day trial, since Dripify says the trial "doesn't have any restrictions on daily processing limits."
- Turn on Activity Control if you're on Advanced, which Dripify recommends "for the best account safety."
- Keep pending invitations under 800, as Dripify's [weekly limit article](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/6074973-i-am-out-of-connection-requests-on-linkedin-what-should-i-do-now) advises.

## The 14 best Dripify alternatives in 2026

### 1. Omniscient

Omniscient is a CRM and LinkedIn outreach software that drafts a message for each lead and sends only what your team approves. Replies land in one Inbox next to the person's CRM record, and the CRM fills itself in from your email and calendar.

Dripify fills messages with [variables](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/5029089-how-to-personalize-connection-requests) from each lead's profile, such as company and time in the current role. In Omniscient, Otto, our agent, writes up to three versions of your own pitch for each kind of signal, such as a job opening or a comment the person left.

Otto holds any version that adds a number, a name, customers or a result you didn't write. A held version never sends until someone on your team edits or approves it, and editing a message after approval clears that approval.

![Omniscient's campaign editor: the seller's own pitch and offer on the left, and on the right two versions Otto wrote for leads who are hiring. Version 1 is locked. Version 2 is held with the warning "Adds a name you didn't write: Northwind."](/assets/blog/dripify-alternatives/held-version.shot.html)

Pacing is on for every sender from day one, whatever your plan:

- A new account starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day, and the warmup starts again after a reconnect or a restriction.
- A profile view goes out 1 to 4 hours before each invitation, unless you turn that setting off.
- A reply stops that person's outreach in every campaign, and a person can be in only one live campaign at a time.
- Before a campaign goes live, Omniscient shows who it goes out to, who's left out and why.

Admittedly, Omniscient isn't the right fit if your sequences need email steps, since it sends on LinkedIn only. Each campaign also sends from one LinkedIn account, and we don't list a dedicated IP or white label. An agency rotating dozens of client senders is better served by HeyReach.

Paid seats start at [$19 per seat a month, billed yearly](/#pricing), and monthly billing costs 20% more. See the [LinkedIn Outbound](/product/linkedin-outbound/) and [CRM](/product/crm/) pages for the rest.

## How to move off Dripify without risking your account

A LinkedIn account carries its pending invitations and recent pace into the new tool, so the first week after a switch needs the most care. Here's the order we'd follow:

```steps
1. Pause every Dripify campaign: Your plan stays active until the end of the period you've paid for, so stop campaigns yourself before you cancel from Billing.
2. Save who you've contacted: Keep a list of everyone in your Dripify campaigns, so the new tool can skip people who already heard from you.
3. Load your do not contact list: Add customers and anyone who asked not to hear from you before the first campaign.
4. Withdraw only what you've given up on: Dripify suggests keeping pending invitations under 800, and LinkedIn says a withdrawn invitation can't go to the same person again for up to three weeks.
5. Restart slower than you ended: Treat the first week like a warmup even on an old account, and raise limits only once acceptance holds above 20%.
6. Test the reply path: Ask a colleague to answer a test message and check that it lands on the right CRM record.
```

Fortunately, a careful tool covers part of step 2. Our engine checks the relationship on LinkedIn before every invitation, so someone still holding your old Dripify request isn't sent a second one.

LinkedIn's page on [invitation restrictions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012) names invitations left pending or ignored as one cause. It also names suspected use of an automation tool, which covers every tool on this list, ours included. Our guide to the [safest LinkedIn automation tools](/blog/safest-linkedin-automation-tools/) covers what else to check in your first month.
