# Best Attio Alternatives in 2026

Attio is a CRM software that builds records from your email and calendar and lets you shape the data model around how your business works. If you're comparing Attio alternatives, you've probably run into one of its plan limits, or you need something it leaves to another tool, like LinkedIn outreach.

We build Omniscient, a CRM that also runs LinkedIn outreach, so we read Attio's pricing page and help center closely while planning our own. Below are the eight alternatives we'd shortlist in 2026, sorted by the reason you're leaving.

```callout
Prices come from each vendor's own pricing page, read on November 3, 2026. We haven't run every CRM here with a live sales team, so where we judge one, we compare what it publishes with what we learned building ours.
```

If you're short on time, here's where we'd start:

- Pick Omniscient if you prospect on LinkedIn and want each reply on a CRM record that fills itself in.
- Pick HubSpot if you want a free CRM now and marketing tools on the same platform later.
- Pick folk if you're a founder or an agency and your deals start in your own network.
- Pick Pipedrive if your team plans its week around pipeline stages.
- Pick Twenty if you like Attio's flexible objects and want open source software you can host yourself.
- Pick Clarify if you want the whole team on board without paying per seat.
- Pick Affinity if you set up Attio to track deal flow at a fund.
- Pick Close if your team sells by phone.

## What Attio does well

Attio still fits plenty of teams, so it helps to know what you'd give up. Its homepage now pitches "agentic revenue," with agents that follow up with leads and flag renewals, and its [pricing page](https://attio.com/pricing) lists these today:

- Email and calendar sync and automatic enrichment come with every plan, Free included.
- Objects grow with the plan, from 3 on Free to 5 on Plus, 12 on Pro and unlimited on Enterprise.
- Plus sends up to 1,000 emails a month per user, and Pro removes the cap.
- Pro adds sequences, Call Intelligence, permission controls and advanced reporting.
- Eligible startups can get up to 80% off, and the trial gives you 14 days of Pro.

Honestly, if your process doesn't fit a standard CRM and someone on your team enjoys designing objects, Attio is hard to replace. Our [Omniscient vs Attio](/blog/omniscient-vs-attio/) comparison goes through the two feature by feature.

## Why teams look for an Attio alternative

Each of these reasons comes from Attio's own pages:

- Sequences and Call Intelligence start on Pro, at $79 per user a month billed yearly, or $99 monthly.
- Sequences send email only, and Attio's [help center](https://attio.com/help/reference/automations/sequences/create-a-sequence) says they don't suit "cold or high volume sales outreach."
- Attio doesn't send on LinkedIn, so teams that prospect there add an app from its directory, such as [lemlist](https://attio.com/apps/lemlist).
- AI runs on credits: 100 to 2,500 seat credits per user a month, plus 250 to 10,000 workspace credits from Free to Pro. Extra credits start at $70 a month.
- Free stops at 3 seats and Plus at 10, so your eleventh seat means moving everyone to Pro.

These matter most for a small sales team, and for anyone who opens most conversations on LinkedIn.

## The 8 best Attio alternatives in 2026

### 1. Omniscient

Omniscient is a CRM and LinkedIn outreach software that fills in your records from email, calendar and call transcripts, and sends only the LinkedIn messages your team approves. Otto, our AI agent, drafts those messages and your follow ups, and he proposes field values with the quote behind each one.

Attio and Omniscient both read your inbox. The hardest call we made was deciding when the AI may write to a record without asking you, and each field now follows these rules:

- You set every field to off, suggest or auto.
- A value with no quote behind it, or one Otto is less than 60% sure of, is dropped.
- An auto field fills itself only when it's empty and Otto is at least 85% sure.
- A value someone on your team typed is never changed, and a private inbox never fills a field on its own.
- Everything else waits in Suggestions, where you accept or skip it.

We made suggestions wait on purpose, because one wrong amount in a forecast costs more than an empty field. Fields filled with proof start on Pro, at $49 per seat a month billed yearly.

On the LinkedIn side, Scout finds leads from a plain sentence and scores each one out of 100 for fit and timing. We built the sending engine ourselves this year, and pacing took most of our care, because LinkedIn doesn't publish its invitation limits.

Here's what the engine does for every sender at the Normal pace:

- A new sender starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day until it reaches your limit.
- It works inside the sender's hours, with a lunch break and random gaps of 3 to 12 minutes.
- It views the profile 1 to 4 hours before sending the invitation.
- It drops to half pace for 3 days when LinkedIn rate limits the account, or when fewer than 20% of a week's invitations are accepted.

Replies land in one Inbox next to the CRM record, and a reply stops that person's remaining steps by default.

Admittedly, Omniscient is narrower than Attio in a few places. We have no mobile app, and we run outreach on LinkedIn only, without sending email from your inbox.

Teammates who only view or log data are free, paid seats start at [$19 per seat a month billed yearly](/#pricing), and the trial lasts 14 days with no credit card. The [CRM page](/product/crm/) and the [LinkedIn Outbound page](/product/linkedin-outbound/) show the rest.

### 2. HubSpot

HubSpot is a customer platform software that puts marketing, sales and service tools on one CRM. Its free CRM is "100% free with no expiration date" for up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts, and it includes Breeze Assistant, which researches companies and preps you for sales calls.

- [Sales Hub Starter lists at $20 per seat a month](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/sales), with a "starts at $7" price for new customers, and it includes sequences.
- Professional starts at $90 per seat a month on a yearly commitment, plus a one time $1,500 onboarding fee.
- Paid plans include HubSpot Credits for AI, 500 a month on Starter and 3,000 on Professional, and extra credits cost $0.01 each.
- Included credits reset each month and don't roll over.

HubSpot suits a team that wants email sequences on an entry plan and expects to add marketing tools later. We compare the two head to head in [Attio vs HubSpot](/blog/attio-vs-hubspot/).

### 3. folk

folk is an AI CRM software that tracks relationships and deals for sales and agency teams. Its Chrome extension, [folkX](https://www.folk.app/products/folkx), imports single people or whole lists from LinkedIn and Sales Navigator, and it also works on Gmail, WhatsApp, Instagram and X.

- [Standard costs $24 per member a month billed yearly, or $30 monthly](https://www.folk.app/pricing), and Premium costs $48, or $60 monthly.
- Email sequences, custom objects and dashboards start on Premium.
- Standard includes 2,000 magic fields a month, which are folk's AI fields, and 1,500 for its workflow assistant.
- Every plan has unlimited members, and the trial lasts 2 weeks with no credit card.

Interestingly, folk's Premium plan gets you email sequences for less than Attio's Pro seat. It suits founders and agencies whose deals start from LinkedIn conversations and their own network.

### 4. Pipedrive

Pipedrive is a sales pipeline CRM software that helps small businesses manage leads and close deals. Its AI Sales Assistant predicts which deals you're likely to win or lose and suggests which leads to pursue.

- Nova, its meeting intelligence, writes a brief before each call, records and transcribes it, and drafts CRM updates for you to approve.
- The homepage says Nova comes with every plan at no extra charge.
- The free trial lasts 14 days with no credit card.
- Pipedrive's pricing page didn't open for our reader, so check today's plans on [its pricing page](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/pricing).

Pipedrive suits a team that wants a clear pipeline more than a custom data model. Thankfully, its CRM updates from calls wait for your approval, which is the same choice we made for suggested values in Omniscient.

### 5. Twenty

Twenty is an open source CRM software that gives you building blocks for your own data model and automations, in its cloud or on servers you run. Its site calls it "the #1 Open Source CRM on GitHub."

- [Pro costs $9 per user a month billed yearly](https://twenty.com/pricing), with email and calendar sync, a custom data model and about 100 AI tasks a week per member.
- Organization costs $39 and adds custom roles with field and row permissions, plus SSO.
- You can host the open source core yourself for free, and the cloud trial lasts 30 days.
- Cloud workspaces include an MCP server for connecting AI assistants.

Twenty is the closest match here if you love Attio's flexible objects and want to own the code and the data. In return, someone on your team builds what Attio gives you ready made, and looks after the servers if you host it.

### 6. Clarify

Clarify is an AI CRM software that calls itself "the autonomous CRM," with an agent named Rep that drafts campaigns and enrolls leads in them. It syncs with your email and calendar and records every call.

- [The Free plan costs $0](https://www.clarify.ai/pricing) with unlimited seats, 1,000 credits a month, 20,000 records and one active campaign.
- Starter costs $50 a month for the whole team, with 5,000 credits a month, 250,000 records and five active campaigns.
- A meeting summary uses 30 credits, and Starter credits don't roll over.

Luckily for small teams, adding a teammate never changes the bill. Watch the credits instead, since every summarized meeting draws from the same pool.

### 7. Affinity

Affinity is a relationship intelligence CRM software that helps private capital firms, such as venture capital and private equity funds, find deals through their network. It captures your firm's emails and meetings automatically and scores how strong each relationship is.

- [Essential costs $2,000 per user a year](https://www.affinity.co/pricing), billed annually, with relationship scoring and pipeline tracking.
- Scale costs $2,300 and adds a notetaker, AI Chat, agents and a hosted MCP server.
- Advanced costs $2,700 and adds enrichment from PitchBook, Dealroom and Crunchbase.
- We didn't see a free trial on its pricing page.

If you set up Attio to track deal flow at a fund, Affinity is built for that exact job, and it points out warm paths to decision makers. For a startup sales team, it's more CRM than you need.

### 8. Close

Close is a sales CRM software that helps teams call and text their leads, with a dialer and SMS built in. Its AI assistant, Chloe, answers questions about your pipeline and your calls on every plan.

- [Solo shows $9 or $19 a month for one user](https://www.close.com/pricing), depending on billing, and Essentials shows $35 or $49 per user.
- Growth adds workflows and the Power Dialer, and Scale adds the Predictive Dialer and live call coaching.
- Call Assistant costs $50 a month per organization plus $0.02 a minute.
- The trial lasts 14 days with no credit card and includes $5 of calling and enrichment credits.

Frankly, Close is the one we'd pick for a team on the phone all day, since Omniscient has no dialer. Once you hit dial on a saved list, Close works through it on its own.

## How to switch from Attio without losing your history

Personally, we'd run the new CRM next to Attio for a couple of weeks before moving the team over. Here's the order we'd follow:

```steps
1. Connect your inbox first: Add Gmail or Outlook and your calendar to the new CRM while Attio keeps running, so its timeline starts filling.
2. Export what an importer might skip: Save custom objects and lists as spreadsheets, since an importer may only read the standard records.
3. Import the core records: Move companies, people, deals and notes, then check that each deal kept its stage and owner.
4. Rebuild the custom parts: Recreate custom objects and fields in the new data model, then load the spreadsheets from step 2.
5. Move automations last: Rebuild workflows and sequences once the data settles, so nothing fires on a half imported record.
6. Connect a LinkedIn sender early: A new sender should start slowly, so connect it a couple of weeks before your first campaign.
```

In Omniscient, the importer reads companies, people, deals with their stages, and notes from Attio. Records already in your workspace are matched and filled in, so a contact that came in through your inbox doesn't turn into a duplicate.
