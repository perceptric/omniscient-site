## What Waalaxy does well

Waalaxy is a LinkedIn outreach tool built for founders and small teams who are new to prospecting. Since July 2026 it [runs fully in the cloud](https://intercom.help/waalaxy/en/articles/15587366-waalaxy-is-now-fully-cloud-based), so there's nothing to install and campaigns keep going with your browser closed.

Here's how the [three plans](https://www.waalaxy.com/pricing) compare, per user and per LinkedIn account:

| Waalaxy plan | Monthly | Billed yearly | Invitations | What it adds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pro | $19 | $16 | 300 a month, 11 a day | Unlimited campaigns, sequence templates, CRM sync and 25 email finder credits a month |
| Advanced | $39 | $32 | 800 a month, up to 100 a day and 200 a week | API access, Make, Zapier and n8n modules, and live chat |
| Business | $69 | $55 | The same as Advanced | Cold email sequences, LinkedIn plus email campaigns and 500 email finder credits a month |

The product is shaped around people running outreach for the first time:

- Quotas set a modest pace for you, and each day's total lands at a random point between 80% and 100% of the maximum.
- Every plan includes ready sequence templates, automated follow ups, unlimited campaigns and CSV imports and exports.
- You can import leads from LinkedIn search, Sales Navigator and Recruiter Lite, or bring them in from a CSV.
- Replies sync to HubSpot or Pipedrive, and Make, Zapier and n8n modules reach 2,000+ other apps.

Honestly, for a founder sending a few hundred invitations a month, that's a tidy package. If Pro's pace suits you and you don't need cold email, staying with Waalaxy is a sensible call.

## Why people look for a Waalaxy alternative

Most of the reasons come down to plan limits and where your replies end up:

- Pro stops at 300 invitations a month, about 11 a day, so any faster pace means moving up to Advanced.
- Cold email sequences need Business, the most expensive of the three plans.
- The LinkedIn Inbox, with reply templates, reminders and tags, is a paid add on at €20 a month once its 500 free conversations run out.
- Replies reach your CRM through a sync, so the CRM stays a second tool with its own bill.
- The [card trial](https://intercom.help/waalaxy/en/articles/6413101-what-are-the-different-subscriptions) turns into a paid plan unless you cancel in time, and no reminder arrives before the first charge.

## Waalaxy's invitation quotas next to our pacing

Waalaxy sets its [daily quotas](https://intercom.help/waalaxy/en/articles/10327318-what-are-quotas) itself and keeps them separate from LinkedIn's weekly limit, so your actions stop when you hit either one. LinkedIn doesn't publish its own number. When you reach it, LinkedIn [restricts your invitations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550555) for a period that "typically lasts one week."

![Daily invitation ceilings for one LinkedIn account. Waalaxy Pro: 11 a day, about 300 a month. Waalaxy Advanced and Business: up to 100 a day, at most 200 a week. Omniscient at the Careful pace: 10 a day and 60 a week. At the Normal pace, the default: 15 a day and 80 a week. At the Fast pace: 25 a day and 150 a week. Omniscient's highest setting: 40 a day and 200 a week.](/assets/blog/waalaxy-alternatives/daily-ceilings.svg "Waalaxy's quotas next to Omniscient's sender settings. LinkedIn publishes no number of its own.")

Interestingly, the two products meet on the weekly ceiling: Waalaxy Advanced and our highest setting both stop at 200 invitations a week. They part ways on the daily peak, where Waalaxy allows up to 100 in one day and our engine stops at 40.

Under that ceiling, our engine shapes each day on its own:

- Each day's total lands between 80% and 100% of your limit, the same range Waalaxy uses for its daily quotas.
- A new account starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day, and the warmup starts again after a reconnect or a restriction.
- Once 20 invitations have gone out in a week, an acceptance rate under 20% drops the account to half pace for 3 days, and a rate limit from LinkedIn does the same. It then climbs back to full over 3 more days.
- A profile view goes out 1 to 4 hours before each invitation, unless you turn that setting off.
- Actions keep a random gap of 3 to 12 minutes at the Normal pace, inside your working hours and around a lunch break.

We chose the lower daily peak on purpose. LinkedIn names sending ["many invitations within a short amount of time"](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012) as one cause of invitation restrictions, so our engine spreads a week's invitations across your working days.

Our sender settings recommend 100 or fewer invitations a week, or up to 150 to 200 with Sales Navigator. Admittedly, the trade is speed. At the Normal pace of 80 a week, a list of 400 people takes at least five weeks to invite, while a tool with a higher ceiling sends faster and carries more of the risk that LinkedIn steps in.

Every tool on this list automates LinkedIn, ours included, and LinkedIn warns that members who use such tools ["risk having their accounts restricted or shut down."](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387) Our guide to the [LinkedIn connection request limit](/blog/linkedin-connection-request-limit/) explains where the common weekly numbers come from.

## How to choose a Waalaxy alternative

Start with whatever pushed you away from Waalaxy, then trial the two or three tools that fix it. These questions settle most choices:

- Count your senders. Per seat pricing suits a team of three, while 25 or more client accounts make flat plans like HeyReach Agency or Skylead Agency cheaper.
- Decide where email lives. If cold email matters as much as LinkedIn, keep both in one sequence with La Growth Machine, Dripify, Skylead or lemlist.
- Follow a reply. Ask where a LinkedIn reply lands and who copies it into your CRM, since native sync or a CRM like Omniscient removes that step.
- Read the safety defaults. Look for a warmup that runs on its own, a daily ceiling you'd be comfortable hitting and a pace that changes from day to day.
- Use the trial on a real list. A 7 day trial leaves little time to see who accepts, so launch your test campaign on day one.

## How to switch from Waalaxy without losing your place

A LinkedIn account carries its history into any new tool, including pending invitations and its recent pace. Some things leave Waalaxy with you, and some you rebuild:

![What carries over when you leave Waalaxy. Prospect lists: export them as CSV before your access ends. Sequences and templates: copy your messages, then rebuild the steps. Pending invitations: they stay on LinkedIn, and a careful tool looks them up before it invites. Past conversations: they stay in LinkedIn's own messaging. CRM sync: connect the new tool, or pick one with a CRM inside. Your sending pace: warm the account up again in the new tool.](/assets/blog/waalaxy-alternatives/carry-over.svg)

Here's the order we'd follow for one account:

```steps
1. Export your prospect lists: CSV exports come with every Waalaxy plan, so save each list while your paid access lasts.
2. Copy your best messages: Save your templates in a document, since you'll rebuild each sequence in the new tool.
3. Stop campaigns, then turn off renewal: You cancel Waalaxy by turning off auto renewal, and you keep access until the end of your billing period.
4. Connect the account once Waalaxy is quiet: Two tools sending from one account at the same time add up to a pace neither one planned.
5. Restart slower than you ended: Treat the first week like a warmup, even on an old account, and raise limits once acceptance holds above 20%.
6. Withdraw old invitations with care: After you [withdraw an invitation](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a568295), you can't invite that person again for up to three weeks.
```

Fortunately, a careful tool handles part of this for you. Our engine looks up the relationship on LinkedIn before every invitation, so someone still holding your old Waalaxy request isn't sent a second one, and people you're already connected with go straight to the message.

LinkedIn can also [restrict your invitations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012) when many of them are ignored or left pending.
