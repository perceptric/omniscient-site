## What HeyReach does well

HeyReach's pricing promise is "unlimited senders, one flat monthly fee," and the Agency and Unlimited plans come close to it. You pay only for LinkedIn senders, and clients, teammates and VAs join for free. Here's what the [plans](https://www.heyreach.io/pricing) include:

- **Growth:** $79 per sender a month, or $64 billed yearly, with unlimited campaigns, the unified inbox, the API, webhooks and an MCP server. Each sender gets a dedicated residential proxy.
- **Agency:** $999 a month for 25 senders, with a 50 sender option, white label, onboarding done for you and a dedicated Slack channel. You bring your own proxies.
- **Unlimited:** $2,999 a month for up to 300 senders under a fair use policy, with migration done for you and priority support.
- **Free trial:** 14 days with no card and up to 3 LinkedIn accounts, or 30 days on request for Agency and Unlimited.

Admittedly, with 25 or more client accounts, one flat bill and one inbox across all of them is hard to beat, and HeyReach may still be your best fit. Our guide to [LinkedIn automation tools for agencies](/blog/linkedin-automation-tools-for-agencies/) compares the agency plans side by side.

## Why teams look for a HeyReach alternative

Most of the reasons come down to how HeyReach is built:

- It bills per sender until you reach the Agency plan, so a team of three or four pays the Growth rate for every account.
- It sends on LinkedIn only. Email runs through native integrations with Instantly, Smartlead and EmailBison, which means a second tool and a second bill.
- Its native CRM sync covers HubSpot, and it also connects to Attio and Breakcold, so Salesforce, Pipedrive and the rest go through Zapier, Make, n8n or the API.
- A brand new LinkedIn account has to be [warmed up by hand](https://help.heyreach.io/en/articles/10415479-can-i-use-a-free-linkedin-account-with-heyreach) until it reaches at least 100 connections before you connect it.

These matter most for a small sales team, or for anyone whose replies need to land in a CRM without somebody copying them over.

## HeyReach's safety limits next to ours

Honestly, HeyReach is upfront about its [connection request limits](https://help.heyreach.io/en/articles/15128619-how-do-connection-request-limits-work-in-heyreach), and its numbers sit close to the ones we chose for our own engine:

| Setting | HeyReach | Omniscient |
|---|---|---|
| Most invitations a day | 40, with 25 suggested once warmed up | 40, with 15 at the Normal pace |
| Most invitations a week | 200 per sender | 200 over a rolling 7 days, with 80 at the Normal pace |
| A brand new account | Warm it up by hand to 100 connections first | Starts at 5 a day and adds 2 a day on its own |
| Acceptance rate | Keep it above 20% | Half pace for 3 days when under 20% of a week's invitations are accepted, once 20 have gone out |
| LinkedIn pushes back | Waits a couple of hours and tries again, or stops until Monday in Cooldown Mode | Half pace for 3 days, then back to full over 3 more |
| Working hours | A window of 8 to 12 hours | Your hours, with a lunch break and gaps of 3 to 12 minutes at the Normal pace |

Both tools stop at 40 a day and 200 a week. LinkedIn doesn't publish its limits, so those numbers are ceilings each tool sets for itself, and our guide to the [LinkedIn connection request limit](/blog/linkedin-connection-request-limit/) explains where they come from.

The bigger difference is who acts on those numbers. HeyReach handles pushback with Cooldown Mode and Locks, while the warmup schedule and the acceptance rate stay your job. Our engine ramps a new account on its own and slows down when acceptance drops or LinkedIn rate limits it.

## How to switch from HeyReach without risking your accounts

Moving a LinkedIn account between tools is the moment we'd watch most closely, because its history comes along with it. Here's the order we'd follow, and our guide to [automating LinkedIn outreach without getting restricted](/blog/automate-linkedin-outreach-without-getting-restricted/) covers pacing in depth:

```steps
1. Pause HeyReach first: Two tools on one account can double its pace, and HeyReach itself warns against running another automation tool alongside it.
2. Save who you've contacted: Export your lead lists and replies, so the new tool skips anyone who already heard from you.
3. Bring your opt outs: Load your do not contact list before the first campaign, and it will hold across every import.
4. Restart slower than you ended: Treat the first week like a warmup, even on an old account, and raise limits once acceptance holds above 20%.
5. Withdraw only what you're done with: Clear requests unanswered for two weeks or more, and keep the ones you still hope will land.
6. Test the reply path: Have a colleague answer a test message and confirm it lands on the right CRM record.
```

LinkedIn can [restrict your invitations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012) when many of them are ignored or left pending. After you withdraw an invitation, you can't invite that person again for up to three weeks, which is why step 5 stays selective.

Thankfully, a careful tool handles part of this for you. Before every invitation, our engine confirms the relationship on LinkedIn, so someone still holding a request from your HeyReach days doesn't get a second one.

A send that times out is never retried on its own either. We chose that because a missing message is easy to fix by hand, and a doubled one can't be taken back.
