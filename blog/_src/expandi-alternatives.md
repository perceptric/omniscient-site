## What Expandi does well

Expandi built its [Business plan](https://expandi.io/pricing/) around account safety, and the rest of the plan holds up too:

- Business costs $99 a month per seat, or $79 a month billed yearly, with a dedicated IP based in your country and an automatic profile warmup.
- Sequences mix connection requests, messages and email follow ups, with If/Action conditions that branch on what each person does.
- [Native integrations](https://expandi.io/integrations/) cover HubSpot, Salesforce and Pipedrive, plus Smartlead, webhooks, Zapier and API credentials.
- Duplicate prevention, a blacklist, campaign A/B tests and a Global Smart Inbox with labels, tags and notes come with every seat.
- The Agency plan starts at 10 seats, is priced on a call and adds white label, client reports, and roles and permissions.

Honestly, that's a strong list, and the safety side reads a lot like the engine we built. If a dedicated IP and an automatic warmup matter most to you and the price works, staying with Expandi is a fair call.

## Why teams look for an Expandi alternative

Most of the reasons come down to how Expandi is priced and where your replies end up:

- Each seat runs [one LinkedIn account at a time](https://help.expandi.io/en/articles/10261458-manage-subscriptions-seats-for-your-linkedin-accounts-workspace), so every rep needs a Business seat until you reach the Agency plan's 10 seats.
- The free trial lasts 7 days, while nine tools on this list give you 14 days or more.
- Personalized images and GIFs cost extra on top of the plan, and video through Sendspark is a paid add on.
- Replies land in Expandi's inbox and reach your CRM through a sync, so the CRM stays a second tool with its own bill.

These hit hardest for a small sales team, or for anyone who wants LinkedIn replies and deals in the same place.

## Expandi's safety settings next to ours

| Setting | Expandi | Omniscient |
|---|---|---|
| Where it sends from | The cloud, with a dedicated IP in your country | The cloud, with your laptop closed |
| A new account | Automatic profile warmup, at a growth rate you set | Starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day |
| Daily totals | Limit ranges you set, with safety stops before you go over | Between 80% and 100% of your limit, different each day |
| Working hours | Your days and hours, which Expandi suggests you vary each day | Your hours, with the start and end moving by up to 20 minutes on their own |
| Between actions | Hourly invitation limits that can adjust on their own | Random gaps of 3 to 12 minutes at the Normal pace, plus a lunch break |
| Weekly invitations | "Up to 300+ connection requests weekly" | Never more than 200 over a rolling 7 days, with 80 at the Normal pace |

The biggest difference is who does the varying. Expandi's guide to [active times](https://help.expandi.io/en/articles/5616042-activity-schedule-settings-active-times) recommends "varying your hours each day," which leaves that chore with you. We had our engine move the hours on its own, because account safety shouldn't depend on someone remembering a daily task. It also drops to half pace for 3 days when under 20% of a week's invitations are accepted, once 20 have gone out.

LinkedIn keeps its exact invitation limit private. Hitting it can restrict your invitations, and LinkedIn says that restriction ["typically lasts one week."](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550555) Every weekly number on a vendor's site, ours included, is a ceiling that vendor picked for itself. Our guide to the [LinkedIn connection request limit](/blog/linkedin-connection-request-limit/) explains where the usual numbers come from.

## How to choose an Expandi alternative

Start with whatever pushed you away from Expandi, then trial the two or three tools that fix it. These questions settle most choices:

- **Count your senders first:** Per seat pricing suits a team of three, while 25 or more client accounts make flat plans like HeyReach Agency or Skylead Agency cheaper.
- **Decide where email lives:** If cold email matters as much as LinkedIn, keep both in one sequence with Skylead, La Growth Machine, lemlist or Dripify.
- **Follow a reply:** Ask where a LinkedIn reply lands and who copies it into your CRM, because native sync or a CRM like Omniscient removes that step.
- **Read the safety defaults:** Look for a warmup that runs on its own, daily invitations under 40 and a pace that changes from day to day.
- **Use the trial on a real list:** A 7 day trial leaves little time to see who accepts, so launch your test campaign on day one.

## How to switch from Expandi without risking your account

A LinkedIn account brings its pending invitations and recent pace into the new tool, so the first week after a switch needs the most care. Here's the order we'd follow:

```steps
1. Stop Expandi's campaigns first: A removed seat stays active until your billing cycle ends, so pause every campaign yourself.
2. Save who you've contacted: Export everyone in your Expandi campaigns, so the new tool can skip people who already heard from you.
3. Bring your blacklist: Load it as your do not contact list before the first campaign, so it holds across every import.
4. Remove the seat as well as the account: Disconnecting a seat from a LinkedIn account doesn't cancel the seat, so take it off your subscription too.
5. Restart slower than you ended: Treat the first week like a warmup, even on an old account, and raise limits once acceptance holds above 20%.
6. Test the reply path: Have a colleague answer a test message and confirm it lands on the right CRM record.
```
