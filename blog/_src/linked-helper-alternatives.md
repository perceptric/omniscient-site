# Best LinkedIn Helper Alternatives in 2026

You probably like its low price per account and its long list of LinkedIn actions. You might be looking anyway because the computer has to stay on, or because your team has outgrown one person's desktop.

We build the LinkedIn sending engine inside Omniscient, so we had to decide where our own sending runs. This guide sorts every alternative by that same choice, with Linked Helper's own pages open next to our engine's settings.

## What Linked Helper does well

Here's what Linked Helper's [pricing page](https://www.linkedhelper.com/pricing) and [homepage](https://www.linkedhelper.com/) list today, which is what you'd leave behind:

- Standard with local data storage costs $15 per LinkedIn account a month on a monthly term, and a 12 month term takes 45% off.
- It covers InMails, endorsements and invitations to events, groups and company pages, on free accounts as well as Sales Navigator and Recruiter.
- A built in CRM keeps tags, notes and custom fields on each contact, and native integrations include HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Zoho CRM and Close.
- Its AI drafts wait in a queue so you can review and edit them before they send.
- The trial gives 14 days of full Pro access with no card, and nothing renews on its own.

Honestly, that's a lot of tool for $15 per account a month. If your computer is on all day anyway and one person runs outreach, staying with Linked Helper is a fair choice.

## Why people look for a Linked Helper alternative

Each reason below comes from Linked Helper's own pages:

- The machine has to be on with the app running to send, and its FAQ says that's true "on either version." For sending around the clock, it suggests renting a VPS, which you pay for separately.
- Local storage keeps your data on one device, so a teammate or a second laptop needs Cloud storage, listed from $16.50 a month against $8.25 for Local. An existing workspace can't move between the two yet.
- Standard caps advanced actions such as event invitations and sending contacts to your CRM at 20 a day, and a Standard license can move to Pro only after it expires.
- We didn't find email steps on its pages, and its email finder hands addresses to a cold email tool such as Instantly.
- Safety setup is left to you, including binding a proxy to each profile and ramping up a new account by hand.

## Desktop or cloud: the choice behind every Linked Helper alternative

Linked Helper and STACKED Local send from a machine you keep on, and the rest, ours included, send from the vendor's servers.

![Where Linked Helper and its alternatives send from. On a machine you keep on, which sends only while that machine is on with the app open: Linked Helper and STACKED Local. On the vendor's servers, which keep running when your laptop is closed: Omniscient, STACKED Cloud, Waalaxy, Dripify, Expandi, HeyReach and La Growth Machine.](/assets/blog/linked-helper-alternatives/where-it-runs.svg)

We see the trade like this:

| | On your machine | On the vendor's servers |
|---|---|---|
| Sends with your laptop closed | Only from a VPS you rent and keep running | Yes |
| The network LinkedIn sees | Your own connection, or a proxy you bind to each profile | The vendor's, such as a dedicated IP on Dripify and Expandi |
| Where your LinkedIn session lives | On your machine, and Linked Helper says "never on our servers" | With the vendor, since it acts for you |
| Who keeps it running | You, including restarts and updates | The vendor |

For us, the first row settled it, because our engine sends inside your working hours with a lunch break and random gaps between actions. A laptop asleep in a bag can't keep that plan, so our engine runs in the cloud.

The trade we accepted is the network: desktop tools send from your own connection, while Omniscient sends from the cloud without a dedicated IP. If keeping every action on your own network matters most to you, Linked Helper or STACKED Local is the closer fit.

## Linked Helper's safety settings next to ours

Thankfully, Linked Helper's [safety kit page](https://www.linkedhelper.com/safety-kit) gives its defaults as real numbers, so we could set them next to our engine's:

| Setting | Linked Helper, from its own pages | Omniscient, from our engine |
|---|---|---|
| Daily ceiling | 150 actions over a rolling 24 hours by default, with separate limits for each action | 15 invitations a day at the Normal pace, with settings that stop at 40 a day and 200 a week |
| Between actions | Hourly caps you set, such as 6 actions an hour, plus random pauses inside every step | Random gaps of 3 to 12 minutes at the Normal pace |
| Day to day totals | Varied on their own, like 36, 40 and 32 invites on three days | Between 80% and 100% of your limit, drawn fresh each day |
| Working hours | Your target time zone's business hours, plus random offsets you add to launch times | Your hours, with the start and end moving by up to 20 minutes each day and a 30 to 60 minute lunch |
| A new account | 10 to 15 actions a day for two weeks, then 5 to 10 more every 10 to 14 days | Starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day, and starts over after a reconnect or a restriction |
| Slowing down | After a warning, rest the account by hand for a few days, then send about 10 connection requests a day | Half pace for 3 days after a LinkedIn rate limit, or when under 20% of a week's invitations are accepted once 20 have gone out, then back to full over 3 days |

Interestingly, the two columns agree more than they differ, and the biggest gap is who does the work: Linked Helper hands you clear guidance, and our engine applies similar rules by itself.

## The 7 best Linked Helper alternatives in 2026

- A new account warms up from 5 invitations a day, and a profile view goes out 1 to 4 hours before each invitation unless you switch it off.
- A reply stops that person's outreach in every campaign, and a person can be in only one live campaign at a time.
- You approve each person's messages, or everyone ready at once when you go live, and editing a message after approval clears that approval.
- Otto, our agent, writes versions of your own pitch and holds any version that adds a claim you didn't make, such as a number or a result.

### 2. STACKED by Dux-Soup

STACKED is a LinkedIn automation software, powered by Dux-Soup, that runs either as a desktop app on your own machine or on its own servers. Its pricing page calls it "One product, two ways to run it," which makes it the closest match to Linked Helper on this list.

- Local costs [$55 per seat a month, or $41.25 billed yearly](https://www.dux-soup.com/pricing), and runs as a desktop app for Mac and Windows.
- Cloud costs $99 per seat a month, or $74.17 billed yearly, and "runs continuously, no need to keep a machine on."
- Both include unlimited concurrent campaigns with up to 12 actions, such as InMails and endorsements, and reply detection that pauses a campaign.
- Native integrations include Salesforce, Pipedrive, HubSpot and Slack, with Zapier and Make libraries.

A STACKED seat costs more than a Linked Helper license, so it fits when running in the cloud matters more to you than the lowest price. Its pricing page doesn't state a trial length, so ask before you plan a test.

Happily, a fixed monthly quota keeps a beginner's volume modest from the first day. If Linked Helper's settings felt like more than you need, Waalaxy is the simplest cloud tool here, though email waits for Business.

### 4. Dripify

Dripify is a LinkedIn automation software that runs drip campaigns of LinkedIn and email steps on its own servers. Pro costs [$79 per user a month, or $59 billed yearly](https://dripify.com/pricing/), and Advanced costs $99, or $79 billed yearly.

- Every plan includes email steps and 100 email finder credits a month, so a found address stays in the same sequence.

Email in the same sequence is the main reason to pick Dripify over Linked Helper. Its plans allow up to 75 connection requests a day, so we'd keep your daily setting well below that.

### 5. Expandi

Expandi suits you if you want the warmup and a dedicated IP handled for you, with no server to rent.

### 6. HeyReach

HeyReach is a LinkedIn automation software that runs outreach from many LinkedIn accounts at once, with every reply in one unified inbox. ## How to switch from Linked Helper without risking your account

Your LinkedIn account carries its pending invitations and recent activity into the new tool, so the first week needs the most care. Here's the order we'd follow:

```steps
1. Close Linked Helper before the new tool starts: Its safety page says LinkedIn flags a session used "from multiple locations" at once, so never run both on one account.
2. Export what you've built: Download your contacts to CSV, plus your messaging history if you're on Pro, so the new tool can skip people who already heard from you.
3. Load your do not contact list: Add current customers and anyone who asked not to hear from you before the first campaign.
4. Withdraw only invitations you've given up on: [LinkedIn says](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a568295) you can't invite the same person again for up to three weeks after a withdrawal.
5. Restart slower than you ended: Treat the first week like a warmup even on an old account, and raise limits only while acceptance stays above 20%, the line where our own engine slows down.
6. Test the reply path: Ask a colleague to answer a test message and check that it lands where your team works.
```

Fortunately, our engine covers part of step 2 on its own. It checks the relationship on LinkedIn before every invitation, so someone still holding your old Linked Helper invitation isn't sent a second one.

LinkedIn's page on [invitation restrictions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012) names invitations "ignored, left pending, or marked as spam" as one cause and suspected automation as another, which applies to every tool here, ours included. Our guide to the [safest LinkedIn automation tools](/blog/safest-linkedin-automation-tools/) covers what else to check.
