# Best Linked Helper Alternatives in 2026

If you're comparing LinkedIn Helper alternatives, you probably like its low price per account and its long list of LinkedIn actions. What usually starts the search is the computer that has to stay on, or a team that has outgrown one person's desktop.

## What Linked Helper does well

Here's what Linked Helper's [pricing page](https://www.linkedhelper.com/pricing) and homepage list today, which is what you'd leave behind:

- Standard with local data storage costs $15 per LinkedIn account a month on a monthly term, and a 12 month term takes 45% off.
- It covers InMails, endorsements and invitations to events, groups and company pages, on free accounts as well as Sales Navigator and Recruiter.
- A built in CRM keeps tags, notes and custom fields on each contact, and native integrations include HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Zoho CRM and Close.
- Its AI drafts wait in a queue so you can review and edit them before they send.
- The trial gives 14 days of full Pro access with no card, and nothing renews on its own.

Honestly, that's a lot of tool for the price. If your computer is on all day anyway and one person runs outreach, staying with Linked Helper is a fair choice.

## Why people look for a Linked Helper alternative

Each reason below comes from Linked Helper's own pages, so you can check it against your setup:

- The machine has to be on with the app running to send, and its FAQ says that's true "on either version." For sending around the clock, it suggests renting a VPS, which you buy separately.
- Local storage keeps your data on one device, so a second laptop or a teammate means moving backup files by hand. Cloud storage starts at $16.50 a month against $8.25 for local on a 12 month term, and an existing workspace can't move between the two yet.
- Standard caps eleven advanced actions at 20 a day, sending contacts to your CRM among them, and a Standard license can move to Pro only after it expires.
- We didn't find email steps on its pages, and its email finder hands addresses to your cold email tool, such as Instantly.
- The safety setup is yours to do, from binding a proxy to each profile to ramping up a new account by hand.

If you've read reviews about Linked Helper's Chrome extension being blocked, check their date. Its homepage now reads "Desktop app, not a Chrome extension."

## Desktop or cloud: the choice behind every Linked Helper alternative

Every tool here sends either from a machine you keep on or from the vendor's servers. Linked Helper and STACKED Local run on your machine, and the rest, ours included, run in the cloud.

![Where Linked Helper and its alternatives send from. On a machine you keep on, which sends only while that machine is on with the app open: Linked Helper and STACKED Local. On the vendor's servers, which keep running when your laptop is closed: Omniscient, STACKED Cloud, Waalaxy, Dripify, Expandi, HeyReach and La Growth Machine.](/assets/blog/linked-helper-alternatives/where-it-runs.svg)

We weighed both sides when we built our engine, and this is how the trade looks to us:

| | On your machine | On the vendor's servers |
|---|---|---|
| Sends with your laptop closed | Only from a VPS you rent and keep running | Yes |
| The network LinkedIn sees | Your own connection, or a proxy you bind to each profile | The vendor's, such as a dedicated IP on Dripify and Expandi |
| Where your LinkedIn session lives | On your machine, and Linked Helper says "never on our servers" | With the vendor, since it acts for you |
| Who keeps it running | You, including restarts and updates | The vendor |

Personally, the first row decided it for us. Our engine sends inside your working hours, with a lunch break and random gaps between actions. A laptop asleep in a bag can't keep that plan, so our engine runs on our servers.

The trade we accepted is the network. Desktop tools send from your own connection, while Omniscient sends from ours and doesn't list a dedicated IP. If keeping every action on your own network matters most to you, Linked Helper or STACKED Local is the closer fit.

## The 7 best Linked Helper alternatives in 2026

### 1. Omniscient

Omniscient is a CRM and LinkedIn outreach software that drafts a message for each lead and sends only what your team approves. It sends from our servers with your laptop closed, and replies land in one Inbox next to the person's CRM record.

Linked Helper's CRM keeps tags and notes, and it can push contacts to a CRM you pay for separately. Omniscient is the CRM itself, and it fills each record in from your email and calendar, so a LinkedIn reply sits next to every email and meeting with that person.

Every campaign follows these rules from the first day:

- A new account warms up from 5 invitations a day, and a profile view goes out 1 to 4 hours before each invitation unless you turn that setting off.
- A reply stops that person's outreach in every campaign, and a person can be in only one live campaign at a time.
- You approve each person's messages, or everyone ready at once when you go live, and editing a message after approval clears that approval.
- Otto, our agent, writes versions of your own pitch and holds any version that adds a name, a number or a result you didn't write.

Admittedly, Omniscient covers far fewer actions than Linked Helper. Sequences use connection requests, messages, profile views and likes, with no InMails, groups, events or email steps, and each campaign sends from one LinkedIn account. Paid seats start at [$19 per seat a month, billed yearly](/#pricing), and monthly billing costs 20% more. The free trial lasts 14 days with no credit card, and the [LinkedIn Outbound](/product/linkedin-outbound/) and [CRM](/product/crm/) pages cover the rest.

### 2. STACKED by Dux-Soup

STACKED is a LinkedIn automation software, powered by Dux-Soup, that runs either as a desktop app on your own machine or on its own servers. Its pricing page calls it "One product, two ways to run it," which makes it the closest match to Linked Helper on this list.

- Local costs [$55 per seat a month, or $41.25 billed yearly](https://www.dux-soup.com/pricing), and runs as a desktop app for Mac and Windows.
- Cloud costs $99 per seat a month, or $74.17 billed yearly, and "runs continuously, no need to keep a machine on."
- Both include unlimited campaigns with up to 12 actions, such as InMails, endorsements and likes, and reply detection that pauses a campaign.
- Native integrations include Salesforce, Pipedrive, HubSpot and Slack, with Zapier and Make libraries.

A STACKED seat costs more than a Linked Helper license, so it fits when a cloud version matters more than the lowest price. Its pricing page doesn't state a trial length, so ask before you plan a test.

Happily, a monthly quota keeps a beginner in a modest range with no sliders to tune. If Linked Helper's settings felt like more than you need, Waalaxy is the simplest cloud tool here, though email waits for Business.

### 4. Dripify

Dripify is a LinkedIn automation software that runs drip campaigns of LinkedIn and email steps on its own servers. Pro costs [$79 per user a month, or $59 billed yearly](https://dripify.com/pricing/), and Advanced costs $99, or $79 billed yearly.

- Every plan includes email steps and 100 email finder credits a month, so a found address stays in the same sequence.

Email in the same sequence is the main reason to pick Dripify over Linked Helper. Its plans allow up to 75 connection requests a day, so we'd set that slider well below the top.

### 5. Expandi

Expandi suits you if you'd like the warmup handled for you and a dedicated IP without renting and running a server yourself.

For an agency, HeyReach moves every client sender to the cloud at one flat price, so nobody keeps a machine on for them. Our guide to [LinkedIn automation tools for agencies](/blog/linkedin-automation-tools-for-agencies/) compares the agency plans side by side.

## How to switch from Linked Helper without risking your account

A LinkedIn account carries its pending invitations and recent pace into the new tool, so the first week after a switch needs the most care. Here's the order we'd follow:

```steps
1. Close Linked Helper before the new tool starts: Linked Helper's own safety page says LinkedIn flags a session used "from multiple locations" at once, so never run both on one account.
2. Export what you've built: Download your contacts to CSV, plus your messaging history if you're on Pro, so the new tool can skip people who already heard from you.
3. Load your do not contact list: Add customers, partners and anyone who asked not to hear from you before the first campaign.
4. Withdraw only invitations you've given up on: [LinkedIn says](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a568295) you can't invite the same person again for up to three weeks after a withdrawal.
5. Restart slower than you ended: Treat the first week like a warmup even on an old account, and raise limits once acceptance holds above 20%.
6. Test the reply path: Ask a colleague to answer a test message and check that it lands where your team works.
```

Fortunately, a careful tool covers part of step 2 for you. Our engine checks the relationship on LinkedIn before every invitation, so someone still holding your old Linked Helper invitation isn't sent a second one.

LinkedIn's page on [invitation restrictions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012) names invitations that are "ignored, left pending, or marked as spam" as one cause, and suspected use of an automation tool as another. That second one applies to every tool here, ours included, and our guide to the [safest LinkedIn automation tools](/blog/safest-linkedin-automation-tools/) covers what else to check.
