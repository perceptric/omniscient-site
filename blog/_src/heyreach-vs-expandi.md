# HeyReach vs Expandi: Features, Pricing and Safety Compared

HeyReach and Expandi both run LinkedIn outreach from the cloud and both bill per LinkedIn account. So if you're weighing HeyReach vs Expandi, the choice comes down to how many accounts you run and where your cold email lives.

We build Omniscient, which has its own LinkedIn sending engine, so we read both vendors' pricing pages and help centers on November 2, 2026 with a builder's eye. We haven't run campaigns in either tool, so every claim about them below comes from what HeyReach and Expandi publish.

Here's our short answer:

- Pick HeyReach if you run many LinkedIn accounts, like an agency with client senders, and want one inbox and one flat bill for all of them.
- Pick Expandi if a small sales team wants email steps, image personalization and a built in warmup inside one LinkedIn tool.
- Look at Omniscient if you'd rather approve each message and keep every reply on a CRM record that fills itself in.

## What HeyReach does well

HeyReach is a LinkedIn automation software that runs outreach from many LinkedIn accounts at once and rotates sending between them. Its homepage promises "Unlimited senders, one fixed cost," and its plans are built around that line.

These stood out on its [pricing page](https://www.heyreach.io/pricing) and homepage:

- Growth includes unlimited campaigns, the API, webhooks and an MCP server for every sender.
- Agency costs $999 a month for 25 senders and adds one white label brand, done for you onboarding and a dedicated Slack channel.
- Unlimited costs $2,999 a month, with a fair use cap of 300 senders.
- The unified inbox lets you reply on behalf of a colleague and send voice notes from your desktop.

Interestingly, HeyReach treats every LinkedIn account the same way, whether it belongs to a rep or a client. With 25 or more senders, one published flat price and one inbox across all of them is hard for a per seat tool to match.

## What Expandi does well

Expandi is a LinkedIn automation software that runs outreach sequences from the cloud, with a dedicated IP address in your country for each account. Its [pricing page](https://expandi.io/pricing/) aims the Business plan at small sales teams.

These are the parts we'd look at first:

- Business includes unlimited campaigns and templates, tags and notes, a blacklist and duplicate checks.
- Sequences mix invitations, LinkedIn messages and email follow ups, and the If/Action builder branches on whether people accept, reply or engage.
- Email steps send from Gmail, Outlook or SMTP, and a reply pauses the lead, says Expandi's [comparison page](https://expandi.io/expandi-vs-heyreach/).
- Campaigns can enroll people who view your profile or like your posts, and the profile view signal needs LinkedIn Premium or Sales Navigator.
- Native integrations cover HubSpot, Salesforce and Pipedrive, according to its [integrations page](https://expandi.io/integrations/).
- The Agency plan starts at 10 seats, is priced on request and adds white label use and client reports.

Honestly, Expandi reads like a tool built for one rep's account and the people who engage with it. If your buyers answer email as often as LinkedIn, keeping both channels in one sequence saves you a second tool and a second bill.

## HeyReach vs Expandi pricing

Both tools bill per LinkedIn account, and both let teammates who don't send log in at no cost. The differences show up in the agency plans and in what costs extra:

- HeyReach's [pricing page](https://www.heyreach.io/pricing) lists one Growth price per sender, and its flat Agency plan starts at 25 senders.
- Expandi charges the same Business price for each seat, and its [pricing page](https://expandi.io/pricing/) sends teams of more than 10 seats to sales.
- Expandi's images, GIFs and video cost extra, and its page lists no price for them.
- HeyReach's email steps run through an Instantly, Smartlead or EmailBison plan that you pay for separately.
- HeyReach also lists an early stage program with a discounted plan for companies under $250,000 in ARR and fewer than 5 people.

## HeyReach vs Expandi safety

LinkedIn's page on [prohibited software](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387) says it doesn't permit third party tools that automate activity on its site. Members who use them "risk having their accounts restricted or shut down," it adds. That rule covers HeyReach, Expandi and every tool in this category, ours included.

Both vendors publish settings that keep an account's activity closer to a person's, and they split the work differently. Their own help articles list these:

| Setting | HeyReach | Expandi |
|---|---|---|
| A new account | Warm it up by hand to "at least 100 connections" before connecting | Built in warmup from 5 actions a day, adding 3 every two days up to 21 |
| Daily invitations | Up to 40, with 25 suggested once warm | Limits you set as ranges |
| Weekly invitations | 200 per sender, and a pause until Monday if LinkedIn's weekly limit is hit | The warmup article suggests staying within 100 actions a week |
| Working hours | A window of 8 to 12 hours | Your days and hours, varied each day as the help center suggests |
| Acceptance rate | Keep it above 20% | Not covered in the articles we read |
| LinkedIn pushes back | Invitations wait a couple of hours, then retry | Not covered in the articles we read |

Thankfully, both vendors give you a weekly number to stay under, because LinkedIn doesn't publish one. Its [help page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550555) says only that reaching the invitation limit may restrict you, and that this "typically lasts one week." Our guide to the [LinkedIn connection request limit](/blog/linkedin-connection-request-limit/) explains where the usual numbers come from.

We built these same settings into Omniscient this year, and we chose to have the software own every one of them. The chart below shows who handles each job by default in all three tools:

![Who handles five safety jobs. Warming up a new account: by you in HeyReach, automatic in Expandi and Omniscient. Varying the working day: by you in HeyReach and Expandi, automatic in Omniscient. Varying the daily total: not listed for HeyReach, automatic in Expandi and Omniscient. Watching the acceptance rate: by you in HeyReach, not listed for Expandi, automatic in Omniscient. The warmup is the biggest gap we see. HeyReach asks you to [warm up a new account by hand](https://help.heyreach.io/en/articles/10415479-can-i-use-a-free-linkedin-account-with-heyreach), starting around 5 requests a day, before you connect it. Expandi ramps the account inside the tool, and its [warmup article](https://help.expandi.io/en/articles/5405001-account-warm-up) notes that changing the settings resets progress to the start.

We went the automatic route as well, so in Omniscient these happen on their own:

- A new sender starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day until it reaches your limit.
- The warmup starts over after a reconnect or a restriction, even on an old account.
- When fewer than 20% of a week's invitations are accepted, once 20 have gone out, the sender drops to half pace for 3 days.

![Omniscient's LinkedIn senders: Sam Carter's account shows 16% of invitations accepted and is slowed until Nov 5 because few invitations were accepted, so today's caps are about half the usual, while Maya Chen's new account is warming up on day 4](/assets/blog/heyreach-vs-expandi/slowed.shot.html)

In the articles we read, neither vendor slows an account by itself when acceptance drops. With either tool, plan to check that number each week and lower your limits when it falls.

HeyReach also wrote publicly about LinkedIn this year. In August it [published a post](https://www.heyreach.io/blog/heyreach-ban) explaining that LinkedIn removed its company page and restricted its executives' profiles on March 25, 2026. HeyReach says customer accounts, campaigns and the platform kept running normally, and that a page removal is separate from an account restriction.

## Sequences, email and personalization

HeyReach keeps its sequences on LinkedIn and passes email to other tools. Its [help article on sequences](https://help.heyreach.io/en/articles/10493916-what-can-be-automated-in-heyreach-sequence) lists these building blocks:

- Two conditions, "If connected" and "Open profile check," and the second one needs Sales Navigator.
- Warmup actions before the invitation: view a profile, follow it or like a recent post, each capped at 40 a day per sender.
- Invitations with an optional note, messages with A/B variants, and InMail from a Sales Navigator account.
- A Find Email step, then Add to Instantly, Smartlead or EmailBison for the email part.
- A wait of at least 3 hours between most steps.

Expandi puts email inside the sequence itself, and its builder has more conditions to branch on. Its campaigns can also start from a signal, such as a profile view or a post like, which Expandi says syncs about once a day.

On personalization, HeyReach automates voice notes, while Expandi's comparison page says it has none. Expandi instead sells image and GIF personalization through Hyperise and video through Sendspark as paid extras.

Luckily, both trials let you run a small campaign before you pay, and HeyReach's covers up to 3 accounts if you want to test sender rotation.

## Inbox, CRM and integrations

Once a lead answers, the reply has to reach whoever owns the deal, and each tool handles that like this:

| Where replies go | HeyReach | Expandi |
|---|---|---|
| Inbox | One unified inbox across senders, with sentiment and auto tagged warm leads | A Global Smart Inbox with labels, tags and notes |
| CRMs | HubSpot sync, with Attio and Breakcold on its integrations page | Native HubSpot, Salesforce and Pipedrive |
| Everything else | Zapier, Make, n8n, Clay, the API, webhooks and an MCP server | Zapier, webhooks, API credentials and an MCP server in beta |

Expandi's integrations page says its Salesforce sync runs automatically based on sentiment, with overwrite controls, and that Pipedrive gets LinkedIn activity on the deal timeline. HeyReach's [integrations page](https://www.heyreach.io/integrations) doesn't list Salesforce or Pipedrive, so those would go through Zapier, Make, n8n or the API.

## Where Omniscient fits

Omniscient is a CRM and LinkedIn outreach software that drafts a message for each lead and sends only what your team approves. Every reply lands in one Inbox next to the person's CRM record, and the record fills itself in from your email and calendar.

We built it for small B2B teams that would rather skip the sync between an outreach tool and a CRM. Day to day, that looks like this:

- Every sender gets the pacing above from the first day, plus a profile view 1 to 4 hours before each invitation unless you turn it off.
- Each day's real total lands between 80% and 100% of your limit, inside working hours that shift by up to 20 minutes, with a lunch break.
- A person can be in only one live campaign at a time, and a reply stops their outreach in every campaign.
- Otto, our agent, drafts each message from the person's role, posts and company news, and editing a message after its approval clears it.

Admittedly, Omniscient sends on LinkedIn only, with no email steps, and we don't list a dedicated IP or white label. Each campaign also sends from one LinkedIn account, so an agency rotating client senders will be better served by HeyReach.

Paid seats start at [$19 per seat a month, billed yearly](/#pricing), and the free trial lasts 14 days with no credit card. The [LinkedIn Outbound page](/product/linkedin-outbound/) and the [CRM page](/product/crm/) show the rest.

## Which one should you choose

Here's how we'd decide, based on the team you run:

- Choose HeyReach if you're an agency with many client senders, since its Agency price is published and includes white label.
- Choose HeyReach if your cold email already runs in Instantly or Smartlead and LinkedIn only needs to feed it.
- Choose Expandi if a small sales team wants LinkedIn and email in one sequence on one bill.
- Choose Expandi if you want image or video personalization, or campaigns that start from profile views and post likes.
- Choose Omniscient if you want to approve each message and keep every reply on a CRM record without a sync.

Frankly, we'd let your email setup decide between the first two. If email already lives in another tool, HeyReach adds LinkedIn beside it, and if it doesn't, Expandi covers both channels in one place. ## Questions about HeyReach vs Expandi

```faq
Q: Is HeyReach cheaper than Expandi?
A: At list price, HeyReach costs less per account. Growth costs $79 per sender a month, or $64 billed yearly, while Expandi Business costs $99 per seat, or $79 billed yearly. HeyReach's email steps need a separate Instantly, Smartlead or EmailBison plan, and Expandi's images and video cost extra.
Q: Which is safer, HeyReach or Expandi?
A: Both send from the cloud with a separate IP or proxy for each account, and LinkedIn's rules don't permit automation tools of any kind. Expandi warms up a new account inside the tool, while HeyReach asks you to warm it up by hand and caps each sender at 200 invitations a week. Expandi's own comparison page says "No tool can promise zero risk."
Q: Does HeyReach send emails?
A: HeyReach doesn't send email itself. It can find a lead's email and pass the lead to Instantly, Smartlead or EmailBison, which send the email part of your outreach.
Q: Is Expandi good for agencies?
A: Expandi's Agency plan starts at 10 seats and adds white label use, client reports, and roles and permissions, with pricing on request. HeyReach publishes a flat $999 a month for 25 senders with one white label brand, which makes it easier to budget.
Q: How many connection requests can you send with HeyReach or Expandi?
A: HeyReach allows up to 40 a day and 200 a week per sender. Expandi's default warmup climbs to 21 actions a day, and its warmup article suggests staying within 100 a week. LinkedIn doesn't publish its own limit.
Q: Can I use HeyReach and Expandi on the same LinkedIn account?
A: HeyReach's help center advises against using several automation tools on one account, since that can cause conflicts and disconnections. Pick one tool per account and pause the other before you switch.
```
