# Waalaxy vs lemlist: Which LinkedIn Tool Fits Your Team

Waalaxy is a LinkedIn outreach software that runs invitation and message campaigns from the cloud, with a monthly invitation quota on each plan. lemlist is a sales engagement software that runs outbound sequences across email, LinkedIn, calls, WhatsApp and SMS from one place.

So the Waalaxy vs lemlist choice mostly comes down to one question: which channel books your meetings today? If it's LinkedIn, Waalaxy costs less and keeps things simple, and if it's email, lemlist gives you far more to work with.

We built the LinkedIn sending engine inside Omniscient this year. That meant working through the questions these tools answer for you, such as how many invitations are safe and where replies should land. Below, we compare Waalaxy and lemlist on price, LinkedIn limits, email and CRM, using each vendor's own pages.

```callout
Prices and limits come from each vendor's own pages, read in November 2026. We haven't run campaigns in Waalaxy or lemlist, so we compare what each one publishes with what we learned building our own engine.
```

If you're short on time, here's where we'd start:

- Pick Waalaxy if LinkedIn is your main channel and you want a low price with ready sequences.
- Pick lemlist if email books most of your meetings and LinkedIn is a second touch in the same sequence.
- Look at Omniscient if you want LinkedIn outreach you approve, inside a CRM that fills itself in from your email and calendar.

## Waalaxy vs lemlist pricing

Both tools charge per user, and both pricing pages open on yearly billing. Waalaxy's [pricing page](https://www.waalaxy.com/pricing) lists three plans and one paid extra:

- Pro, €19 per user a month: about 300 invitations a month, LinkedIn sequences, CRM sync and 25 Email Finder credits a month.
- Advanced, €49 per user a month: 800 invitations a month, plus the API and the Make, Zapier and n8n modules.
- Business, €69 per user a month: everything in Advanced, plus cold email sequences, LinkedIn and email campaigns and 500 Email Finder credits a month.
- The Inbox, with saved replies, reminders, tags and filters, is an optional extra at €20 a month.

Waalaxy's yearly tab says it saves up to 50%, and its [help center](https://intercom.help/waalaxy/en/articles/6413101-what-are-the-different-subscriptions) gives Business a standard price of €139 a month that may vary by country. So check the monthly price at checkout if you'd rather not pay for a year up front.

lemlist's [pricing page](https://www.lemlist.com/pricing) has two plans that matter here, plus two paid extras:

- Email, $69 a month or $55 billed yearly: 50,000 emails a month with unlimited users and senders, and no LinkedIn automation.
- Multichannel, $109 per user a month or $87 billed yearly: LinkedIn automation, SMS, a built in call dialer, unlimited emails and messages and 5 senders per user.
- Lead enrichment costs $20 a month, or $16 billed yearly, for 2,000 credits a month.
- Buying intent signals cost $29 a month, or $21 billed yearly, for 100 signals a month.

lemlist's FAQ describes a 14 day trial with Multichannel features, apart from calls inside the app, and 200 credits.

Honestly, the pricing tells you who each tool is for. Waalaxy's cheapest plan already includes LinkedIn, while lemlist puts LinkedIn on the plan that also carries calls and SMS. For LinkedIn and email together, the two sit much closer: €69 on Waalaxy Business against $87 on lemlist Multichannel.

## LinkedIn features in Waalaxy and lemlist

Both tools send invitations and follow up messages on a schedule, and the bigger difference is how each one connects to your LinkedIn account.

In a [help article](https://intercom.help/waalaxy/en/articles/15587366-waalaxy-is-now-fully-cloud-based) dated July 1, 2026, Waalaxy says it "is now 100% cloud" and that its Chrome extension has left the Chrome Web Store. Campaigns run from the web app in the background, so you don't need LinkedIn open while they work.

Interestingly, some comparisons updated as late as August 2026 still describe Waalaxy as an extension that needs your computer on, so check the date on anything you read.

Waalaxy's other LinkedIn features, from its homepage and help center:

- 99+ outreach sequences you can use as they are, with automated follow ups and unlimited campaigns.
- Invitations, messages, profile visits and follows, each with its own daily quota on Advanced and Business.
- Up to 50,000 prospects per account on Advanced and Business, and CSV imports and exports on every plan.
- CRM sync on every plan, with HubSpot and Pipedrive named on its homepage.

lemlist links LinkedIn through its Chrome extension instead. Its [extension guide](https://help.lemlist.com/en/articles/4452748-how-to-use-the-lemlist-linkedin-chrome-extension) says the extension "relies on your browser cookies to recognize your current LinkedIn session," so logging out or clearing cookies can break the link until you reconnect.

lemlist's LinkedIn side, from its pricing page and help center:

- LinkedIn steps cover profile visits, follows, invitations, messages, voice messages and InMail.
- Each lemlist account connects to one LinkedIn account, according to its [help article on multiple profiles](https://help.lemlist.com/en/articles/12889586-connect-and-manage-multiple-linkedin-profiles-in-lemlist), so a second profile needs its own seat.
- You can work in lemlist from inside LinkedIn and Gmail, and a unified inbox keeps replies from every channel together.
- Its help center points you to a LinkedIn Health Score, still in beta, under the Deliverability Hub settings.

## LinkedIn limits and account safety

LinkedIn doesn't publish an exact invitation limit, and its [Invitation limit reached](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550555) page only says a restriction "typically lasts one week." Its [prohibited software page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387) goes further: members who use tools that automate activity on LinkedIn risk having "their accounts restricted or shut down." That risk applies to every tool here, ours included.

Each vendor's help center sets its own numbers, and we've put our engine's settings beside them:

| Setting | Waalaxy | lemlist | Omniscient |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invitations a day | 11 on Pro, 80 to 100 on Advanced and Business | Up to 20, on working days only | 15 at the Normal pace, 40 at most |
| Invitations a week | At most 200 on Advanced and Business | About 100 a rolling week, which lemlist gives as LinkedIn's cap | 80 at the Normal pace, 200 at most |
| Messages a day | 120 to 150 on Advanced and Business | 30, voice messages included | 30 at the Normal pace |
| Profile visits a day | 120 to 150 on Advanced and Business | 30 | 40 at the Normal pace |
| A new account | Not stated in its plans article | A "LinkedIn Invite Ramp-Up per day" setting, such as 2 more a day up to 20 | 5 invitations on day one, then 2 more each day |
| After a warning | Not stated in its plans article | Pause for at least 48 hours, then restart at around 5 actions a day | Half pace for 3 days, then back to full over 3 more |

The lemlist figures come from its articles on [account limits](https://help.lemlist.com/en/articles/7210709-set-account-limits) and [connection requests](https://help.lemlist.com/en/articles/8514157-linkedin-connection-requests-limits-best-practices-avoiding-restrictions), and Waalaxy's come from its [subscriptions article](https://intercom.help/waalaxy/en/articles/6413101-what-are-the-different-subscriptions).

Surprisingly, the two vendors read LinkedIn's unpublished limit quite differently. Waalaxy's top plans allow up to 200 invitations a week, while lemlist's help center tells you to stay near 100. Both numbers are ceilings, and we'd treat the lower one as the safer guide whichever tool you pick.

We set our default below lemlist's guidance and Waalaxy's top plans, at 15 a day and 80 a week, because a restriction usually costs a full week of outreach. Our [guide to the LinkedIn connection request limit](/blog/linkedin-connection-request-limit/) explains how we picked those numbers.

The shape of the day mattered to us as much as the total, so an Omniscient sender also does this on its own:

- It sends only inside your working hours, with a lunch break of 30 to 60 minutes near the middle of the day.
- It waits a random 3 to 12 minutes between actions at the Normal pace.
- Each day's total lands between 80% and 100% of your limit, so no two days look the same.
- It views a profile 1 to 4 hours before sending that person an invitation.
- It halves its pace for 3 days when fewer than 20% of last week's invitations were accepted or LinkedIn rate limits the account.

What you give up is speed: a new account in Omniscient sends 5 invitations on its first day and reaches the Normal pace of 15 on its sixth.

## Email and other channels

Thankfully, this part is easy to call, because lemlist does far more with email than Waalaxy does, as its homepage and pricing page show:

- lemwarm, lemlist's email warmup and deliverability tool, comes with every seat.
- A database of 650M+ leads, with an email and phone number finder.
- Calls through a built in dialer and SMS, with WhatsApp as an extra on Multichannel.
- AI agents for signals, enrichment and messaging, plus lemAgent.

Its help center is careful with email volume, too. Once a mailbox finishes warming up, it starts at 10 to 15 cold emails a day through lemlist and adds 5 a week, up to 25 to 30.

Waalaxy saves email for its top plan, so cold email sequences come only with Business. Its Email Finder gives you 25 credits a month on Pro and Advanced, or 500 on Business. If you send cold email every day, lemlist is the better fit, and Waalaxy Business suits teams that add an email step to their LinkedIn campaigns.

## Where your LinkedIn replies end up

A reply is the moment a lead becomes a conversation, so it's worth knowing where each tool puts it:

- In Waalaxy, replies go to the Inbox, an optional extra at €20 a month, and leads sync to HubSpot, Pipedrive or other tools through Make, Zapier and n8n.
- In lemlist, replies from every channel share one unified inbox, and the integrations send leads to HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive or Attio.

In both cases, your CRM stays a separate app with its own login, and usually its own bill.

![Where a LinkedIn reply lands in each tool. Personally, this was the part we cared about most when we built Omniscient's Inbox. When replies live in one tool and deals in another, someone has to copy them across, and the CRM falls behind on the busiest days.

## Which one fits your team

Look at where your last ten meetings came from, because that usually settles this comparison:

- Choose Waalaxy if you're a founder or a small team doing outreach mostly on LinkedIn, and you'd like low prices and ready sequences.
- Choose Waalaxy Business if LinkedIn leads your outreach and you want an email step in the same campaign.
- Choose lemlist if email books most of your meetings, or you want calls, SMS, a lead database and lemwarm in one place.
- Look further if you want LinkedIn replies inside your CRM, or every message approved before it goes.
