## What LinkedIn says about automation tools

Section 8.2 of the [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) bans "bots or other unauthorized automated methods" used to "add or download contacts, send or redirect messages." Its [prohibited software page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387) names "browser plug-ins, or browser extensions" too, and says members who use them risk having "their accounts restricted or shut down."

LinkedIn's page on [invitation restrictions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012) lists what usually leads to one:

- You've sent many invitations in a short amount of time.
- Many of your invitations were ignored, left pending or marked as spam.
- LinkedIn suspects you're using an automation tool.

LinkedIn acts against vendors too: HeyReach [writes](https://www.heyreach.io/blog/heyreach-ban) that on March 25, 2026, LinkedIn removed its company page and restricted the profiles of four of its leaders, while customer campaigns kept running.

Honestly, that's why we read "safest" as "lowest risk." No tool can hide automation from LinkedIn for certain, so the best ones make your account act like a careful person at work and back off early when the signs turn bad.

## What makes a LinkedIn automation tool safe

We judged each tool on five checks, most of them settings we had to decide for our own engine.

| Safety check | Why it matters | How Omniscient handles it |
|---|---|---|
| Sends with your laptop closed | Actions follow your hours even when your computer is asleep | Cloud sending in each sender's own time zone |
| Its own IP or proxy | Logins from one steady place look like one person | Not listed |
| Automatic warmup | A new account at full speed on day one stands out | 5 invitations on day one, then 2 more each day |
| Daily totals that vary | The same 15 invitations every day is a pattern | Each day's cap lands between 80% and 100% of your limit |
| Backs off on its own | Falling acceptance is the earliest warning you get | Half pace for 3 days, then back up over 3 more |

Most tools on this list let you set the hours they send in, and most say they space actions out at random, so we didn't score those two. We also skipped tools built mainly for scraping, since LinkedIn's rules name scraping on its own.

We left lemlist off the list for a different reason. Its help center says its Chrome extension ["is required for LinkedIn automation,"](https://help.lemlist.com/en/articles/12889586-connect-and-manage-multiple-linkedin-profiles-in-lemlist) and LinkedIn names extensions directly.

## The 12 safest LinkedIn automation tools in 2026

### 1. Omniscient

Omniscient is a CRM and LinkedIn outreach software that paces every sender like a person at work and sends only the messages your team approves. These rules run for every sender from its first day:

- A new account starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day until it reaches your limit, and the warmup starts over after a reconnect or a restriction.
- Each day's cap lands between 80% and 100% of your limit, with random gaps of 3 to 12 minutes at the Normal pace.
- Working hours include a lunch break, and each day's start and end move by up to 20 minutes, so mornings don't open at 9:00 sharp.
- Once a sender has sent 20 invitations in a week, an acceptance rate under 20% drops it to half pace for 3 days.

Approval is our other safety layer: you approve each person's messages, or everyone ready at launch, and editing a message clears its approval. A reply stops that person in every campaign at once.

![Omniscient campaign settings: Approval with Approve each person picked, and Safety with rules that always hold, such as stop when they reply and skip people in another campaign](/assets/blog/safest-linkedin-automation-tools/safety.shot.html)

Admittedly, Omniscient doesn't list a dedicated IP or proxy for each account, and it caps every sender at 40 invitations a day and 200 a week. It sends on LinkedIn only, so if you need email steps or rotate dozens of client accounts, La Growth Machine or HeyReach will suit you better.

Paid seats start at [$19 per seat a month, billed yearly](/#pricing), with a 14 day free trial and no credit card, and the [LinkedIn Outbound page](/product/linkedin-outbound/) shows how campaigns work.

### 2. Expandi

Expandi is a LinkedIn automation software that runs outreach sequences from the cloud for sales teams, lead generation agencies and recruiters. Its Business plan costs [$99 a month, or $79 a month billed yearly](https://expandi.io/pricing/), and the free trial lasts 7 days.

Its pricing page groups these under "Account Safety":

- A dedicated IP address based in your country for each account.
- An automatic profile warmup with a growth rate you can adjust.
- Daily limit ranges for invitations, messages, InMails and event invites.

Its help center adds [active times in your own time zone](https://help.expandi.io/en/articles/5616042-activity-schedule-settings-active-times) and suggests "varying your hours each day." Expandi's safety list reads a lot like ours and adds a dedicated IP, something Omniscient doesn't mention. We didn't find an automatic slowdown for low acceptance on its pages, so keep an eye on that number yourself.

We'd point a sales team to Expandi when it wants the IP and the warmup handled from day one.

### 3. La Growth Machine

La Growth Machine is a sales automation software that runs multichannel sequences on LinkedIn, email, calls and X. Basic costs [€60 per identity a month](https://lagrowthmachine.com/pricing/) for LinkedIn and email, and the free trial lasts 14 days with no card.

Its [LinkedIn automation page](https://lagrowthmachine.com/features/linkedin-automation/) spells out more safety behavior than most vendor pages we read:

- Campaigns run on its servers with "a dedicated mobile proxy for each LinkedIn identity."
- Daily limits, sending days, hours and time zone are set for each identity.
- Invitations pause for 24 hours when LinkedIn caps them, and you're told.
- Pending invitations are withdrawn after 4 weeks by default.

The warmup is up to you, since the page says to "start low on a new or quiet account and go up step by step." Thankfully, it's also candid that "no tool can rule it out: LinkedIn doesn't allow automation." Choose it when your sequences mix LinkedIn and email steps.

### 4. GetSales.io

GetSales.io is a LinkedIn and email outreach software that runs each LinkedIn account in its own cloud session and bills by account instead of by user. One account costs [$69 a month, and 5 cost $195](https://getsales.io/pricing/), with a 7 day trial for LinkedIn and email.

Its pricing page lists more account protection than most:

- "Premium managed ISP proxy connections are included," with checks that keep "each LinkedIn account on a consistent connection."
- "A new LinkedIn account gets a gradual ramp toward its daily limits," and you can switch the ramp off if you'd rather set limits yourself.
- Its [homepage](https://getsales.io/) says "every step is paced within LinkedIn's safe range for that account."

Refreshingly, that covers both the proxy and the warmup, a pair only Expandi also lists on this page. We didn't find varied daily totals or a slowdown for low acceptance, so watch your acceptance rate in the first weeks.

The price list also bills LinkedIn accounts and email inboxes apart, so work out what your setup costs before the trial ends. For an agency running many client accounts, having the proxy handled is the main draw.

### 5. Dripify

Dripify is a LinkedIn automation software that runs drip campaigns of LinkedIn and email steps from its own servers. Pro costs [$79 per user a month, or $59 billed yearly](https://dripify.com/pricing/), and Advanced costs $99, or $79 billed yearly, with a 7 day trial that needs no credit card.

Both plans list a dedicated IP, "a consistent IP address tied to your account, in your location," and actions "spaced with randomised timing" inside your working hours. Advanced adds activity control, where "your sending pace adjusts to your account's own activity and history."

Two things we'd check in a trial:

- The daily quotas go up to 75 connection requests, well above the 15 a day our Normal pace uses, so pick your own lower number.
- We didn't find a warmup on the pricing page, so ramp a new account up by hand.

It suits a small team that wants an IP included and is happy to set its own lower limits.

### 6. HeyReach

HeyReach is a LinkedIn automation software that connects many LinkedIn accounts and rotates sending between them. The [Growth plan costs $79 per sender a month](https://www.heyreach.io/pricing), and Agency costs $999 a month for 25 senders.

Its [help article on connection limits](https://help.heyreach.io/en/articles/15128619-how-do-connection-request-limits-work-in-heyreach) is the clearest one we read:

- Each sender stops at 40 invitations a day and 200 a week, with 25 a day suggested once warmed up.
- When LinkedIn's weekly limit is hit, Cooldown Mode stops invitations until the following Monday.
- It asks you to keep your acceptance rate above 20%.
- It suggests 8 to 12 working hours a day, since its randomized delays can stop a 3 hour window from reaching the daily limit.

Growth gives each sender a dedicated residential proxy, while Agency and Unlimited ask you to bring your own. A new account needs warming up [by hand to at least 100 connections](https://help.heyreach.io/en/articles/10415479-can-i-use-a-free-linkedin-account-with-heyreach) before you connect it. Our [HeyReach alternatives guide](/blog/heyreach-alternatives/) puts its limits next to ours line by line. It's built for agencies with many senders who want a hard weekly cap on each one.

### 7. Skylead

Skylead is a LinkedIn automation and cold email software that builds sequences with if/else conditions across both channels. The All-in-one plan costs [$100 per seat a month](https://skylead.io/pricing/) with unlimited email inboxes, and the free trial lasts 7 days.

Its [guide to the connection limit](https://skylead.io/blog/linkedin-connection-limit/) describes two safety habits we'd want in any tool:

- "After signing up, Skylead puts you in warm-up mode by default," and then it "slowly increases your limits until your account is fully warmed up."
- You set a range for daily invitations instead of one fixed number, so your totals move from day to day.

Frankly, we like seeing the warmup on by default, since a brand new account is the easiest one to run too fast. We couldn't confirm how Skylead handles IPs or proxies on the pages we could read, and we didn't find a slowdown for low acceptance, so ask support about both.

At $100 a seat it's the highest starting price here, which pays off only if you use the email side too. We'd look at it if your team runs LinkedIn and cold email side by side.

### 8. SalesRobot

SalesRobot is a LinkedIn and cold email automation software that runs in the cloud and can let an AI agent answer replies for you. Basic costs [$59 per LinkedIn account a month, or $39 billed yearly](https://www.salesrobot.co/pricing), and the free trial lasts 14 days with no card.

Its [homepage](https://www.salesrobot.co/) says it "runs entirely in the cloud with dedicated IPs for each LinkedIn account," and every plan lists "human-like behavior simulation" with "smart time delays & conditions." Its pricing table caps connection requests at 20 a day on Basic and 75 on the higher plans, and the FAQ adds "real-time throttling."

We'd read the bolder numbers with care. The homepage says SalesRobot cuts ban risk "to 0.00001 %," while its own footer says using it "may not comply with LinkedIn's User Agreement." We'd go by the footer, and it holds for every tool here, ours included.

We found no warmup for LinkedIn accounts, so ramp a new one up by hand, and on a higher plan keep the daily number well under the 75 it allows. It suits a team that wants a dedicated IP and AI help on first replies, with someone reading what the AI sends.

### 9. Linked Helper

Linked Helper is a LinkedIn automation software that runs as a desktop app on your own computer or on a server you control. Standard costs [$15 per LinkedIn account a month](https://www.linkedhelper.com/pricing) on a monthly term, and the 14 day trial gives full Pro access with no card.

Its [safety kit page](https://www.linkedhelper.com/safety-kit) explains how the desktop approach works:

- You set daily limits and hourly pacing, such as 6 actions an hour at most.

Interestingly, its pricing page also says "LinkedIn may still impose account-specific limits or restrictions," which holds for every tool here, ours included. The warmup is a guideline of 10 to 15 actions a day for the first two weeks, and the computer or server has to stay on during your working hours. Pick it if you want every action to leave from your own network.

### 10. Dux-Soup

Dux-Soup is a LinkedIn automation software that runs drip campaigns as a desktop app on your computer or on its own servers. STACKED local costs [$55 per seat a month](https://www.dux-soup.com/pricing), and STACKED cloud costs $99 and keeps running with your computer off.

Its [knowledge base](https://support.dux-soup.com/article/770-default-daily-limits-and-automation-speed-in-stacked) adds profile visits at a random 25 to 125 an hour, with a 5 minute pause after every 20 visits.

We didn't find a dedicated IP or an automatic warmup, and the pricing page has a demo button where a free trial would usually sit. Dux-Soup makes sense for a team that likes careful defaults and would rather the vendor set the speed.

### 11. Waalaxy

We didn't find details on IP addresses, warmup or slowdowns on its pages, so ask support about those before you scale up. For a solo seller trying automation for the first time, that's a sensible place to start.

## What a safe tool does when LinkedIn pushes back

In our own engine, the rules that matter most cover the moments when something goes wrong. That's where an account gets hurt, and those moments rarely show up on a pricing page.

![Five moments when LinkedIn pushes back and what Omniscient's engine does: low acceptance means half pace for 3 days, too many requests means a retry after 15 then 30 minutes and a 3 day slowdown, a sign out or a refusal pauses the sender, and a send that times out is never sent again on its own](/assets/blog/safest-linkedin-automation-tools/pushback.svg)

Here's what we chose for each one, and the reasoning behind it:

- Low acceptance only counts after 20 invitations in a week, because two ignored invitations on a quiet Monday shouldn't slow an account for six days.
- A rate limit sends the action back to the queue for 15 minutes, then 30, and also slows the sender for the next 3 days.
- A sign out or a refusal pauses the sender with nothing retried around it, and the warmup starts again from 5 a day when it comes back.
- A send that times out is marked "outcome unknown" and never goes again on its own. A missing message is easy to fix by hand, while a doubled one can't be taken back.
- Work held overnight goes out at random times in the first 2 hours of the next working slot, so mornings don't open with a burst.

The random daily totals cost you some volume, since a sender's cap averages about 90% of your setting. We took that trade because identical totals every day look like a machine.

Ask any vendor how its tool handles these moments before you connect an account. The answers tell you how much of the safety work you'll be doing yourself.

## How to choose the safest LinkedIn automation tool for you

Start from how you work, then test the settings in the first week of a trial, before you load your full list:

1. Decide where it runs: cloud tools keep your hours with your laptop closed, and Linked Helper keeps every action on your own network. Dux-Soup lets you choose either one.
2. Connect one account first, set a careful pace and watch the acceptance rate for a week, slowing down if fewer than 1 in 5 people accept.
3. Check whether the warmup is automatic, and when it's by hand, start near 5 invitations a day and add a couple each day.
4. Keep the weekly cap at 100 or fewer, as our own settings recommend, and confirm the tool treats it as a hard stop.
5. Run one tool per LinkedIn account, since two tools each keep their own pace and together can send twice as much.
6. Decide who reads messages before they go, because LinkedIn counts invitations marked as spam against you.

Happily, who you invite is one safety lever every tool leaves in your hands. A shorter list of people with a real reason to hear from you leaves fewer invitations ignored, and ignored invitations are on LinkedIn's own list of triggers.
