# 13 Best folk CRM Alternatives in 2026

folk is an AI CRM software that keeps a team's contacts and pipelines in one place, with a Chrome extension that saves LinkedIn profiles as you browse. If you're comparing folk CRM alternatives, you've probably hit a plan limit, or you want a CRM that also sends your LinkedIn invitations and messages.

We build Omniscient, a CRM that sends LinkedIn invitations and messages at a human pace, which is the part folk leaves to you. This list starts from folk's own pricing and help pages and gives you the 13 alternatives we'd shortlist in 2026, sorted by the reason you're leaving.

Our short answer, by the reason you're leaving:

- Pick Omniscient if you want LinkedIn invitations and messages sent for you, with replies on a CRM record that fills itself in.
- Pick Attio if you want to design your own objects.
- Pick HubSpot if you want a free CRM now and marketing tools later.
- Pick Salesflare if you want automatic data entry with a LinkedIn sidebar and email workflows.
- Pick Breakcold if you want LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Telegram and email conversations in one inbox.
- Pick Pipedrive if your team plans its week around pipeline stages.
- Pick Copper if your agency works inside Google Workspace.
- Pick Streak if you want your pipeline inside Gmail itself.
- Pick Twenty if you want open source software you can host yourself.
- Pick Clarify if you want the whole team on board without paying per seat.
- Pick Close if your team sells by phone.
- Pick Nimble if you want one simple plan with room for 25,000 contacts.
- Pick Dex if folk was mostly where you kept up with your own network.

## What folk does well

folk still fits plenty of teams, so it helps to know what you'd give up. Its homepage calls it "the only AI CRM your team needs to close deals and find your next clients." Here's what its [pricing page](https://www.folk.app/pricing) and product pages list today:

- Its extension, [folkX](https://www.folk.app/products/folkx), imports single people or whole lists from LinkedIn and Sales Navigator, and it also works on Gmail, WhatsApp, Instagram and X.
- Standard costs $24 per member a month billed yearly, or $30 monthly, with pipelines, email campaigns, enrichment and AI fields that folk calls magic fields.
- Email, calendar and WhatsApp sync come on Standard, and no plan caps how many members you add.
- folk now has a [mobile app](https://www.folk.app/mobile) for iOS and Android, and its homepage marks phone call recording as new.
- The trial lasts 2 weeks with Premium features and no credit card.

Honestly, we like folk's design so much that Omniscient's square, black and white look takes after it. If your deals come from people you already know, folk may still be the right home for them.

## Why teams look for a folk alternative

The reasons we found sit on folk's own pricing page and in its help center:

- Email sequences, custom objects, dashboards and API access start on Premium, at $48 per member a month billed yearly, or $60 monthly.
- Sequences send email only, with up to 10 steps, and folk's [sequence help article](https://help.folk.app/en/articles/8744016-send-email-sequence) says replies land in your own email inbox, outside folk's messaging inbox.
- A sequence needs credits for every step before it starts, so folk's own example of 3 steps to 200 contacts takes 600 credits.
- folkX saves profiles, adds notes and tasks, syncs LinkedIn conversations into folk and fills in your [message templates](https://help.folk.app/en/articles/6573940-message-templates) on LinkedIn. We didn't find automated invitations or LinkedIn steps in sequences on its pages, its plans or its help center.
- Standard syncs 1 account per member and shares only the last 30 interactions with teammates, while Premium shares the full history.

### 1. Omniscient

Omniscient is a CRM and LinkedIn outreach software that fills in your records from email, calendar and call transcripts, and sends only the messages your team approves. Otto, our AI agent, drafts those messages and the follow up emails you send after a call.

Where folkX saves a profile for you to write to later, an Omniscient campaign does the sending. Its LinkedIn steps send a connection request with or without a note, and later messages go only to people who are already connections. You approve each person's messages, or the whole campaign at launch, and editing a message after approval clears it.

LinkedIn's [invitation limit page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550555) says a restriction "typically lasts one week" but gives no number for the limit itself. So at the Normal pace, which aims for 15 invitations a day and 80 a week, each sender works like this:

- A new sender starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day until it reaches the limit.
- Each day's total lands somewhere between 80% and 100% of that limit.
- It sends inside the sender's working hours, with a lunch break and random gaps of 3 to 12 minutes.
- By default, it views the profile 1 to 4 hours before sending the invitation.
- It drops to half pace when LinkedIn rate limits the account, or when fewer than 1 in 5 of a week's 20 or more invitations are accepted. After 3 days, it eases back to full pace over 3 more.

The tradeoff we accepted is speed, since a brand new sender sends 5 invitations on its first day and only reaches 15 on its sixth.

Replies land in one Inbox with the person's CRM record beside each thread, and a reply stops that person's outreach in every campaign. On Pro, Otto shows the quote behind each value he proposes, and he fills an empty field on his own only when he's at least 85% sure.

Admittedly, Omniscient is narrower than folk in a few places. We have no mobile app or browser extension like folkX, and our campaigns send on LinkedIn only, with no email steps. If you save profiles by hand and follow up mostly by email, folk Premium or Salesflare Pro will suit you better.

Paid seats start at [$19 per seat a month billed yearly](/#pricing), Pro costs $49, and teammates who only view or log data are free. The trial lasts 14 days with no credit card, and the [Outbound LinkedIn page](/product/linkedin-outbound/) and the [CRM page](/product/crm/) show the rest.

### 2. Attio

Attio is a CRM software that builds records from your email and calendar and lets you shape the data model around how your business works. Its homepage also lists a web agent that researches for you and enrichment for every record.

- [Free covers up to 3 seats](https://attio.com/pricing), with 3 objects and 200 emails a month.
- Plus costs $35 per user a month billed yearly, or $44 monthly, for up to 10 seats and 5 objects.
- Pro costs $79, or $99 monthly, and adds sequences and Call Intelligence with up to 12 objects.
- The trial gives you 14 days of Pro, and eligible startups can get up to 80% off.

Attio suits a team whose data no longer fits people and deals, like an agency that tracks projects or a fund that tracks investors. If email sequences are why you're leaving folk, plan on Pro, and our [Attio alternatives guide](/blog/attio-alternatives/) shows where its other limits sit.

### 3. HubSpot

HubSpot is a customer platform software that puts marketing, sales and service tools on one CRM. Its [free plan](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/sales) covers up to 2 users with no credit card.

- Sales Hub Starter starts at $7 per seat a month with 500 HubSpot Credits. The page also shows $20 and doesn't say which billing each price is for.
- Sequences start on Professional, according to HubSpot's [send limits article](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/connected-email/sales-email-send-limits), and a Professional seat can send up to 500 sequence emails a day.
- Professional starts at $90 per seat a month with 3,000 HubSpot Credits, plus a required $1,500 onboarding fee you pay once.

Thankfully, you can try the free plan with a copy of your folk contacts before you pay for anything.

### 4. Salesflare

Salesflare is a CRM software for B2B teams that fills itself in from your inbox, email signatures and calendar. Fittingly, its headline reads "The CRM that keeps itself up to date."

- [Growth costs $29 per user a month billed yearly, or $39 monthly](https://salesflare.com/pricing), with a LinkedIn sidebar, an email sidebar and the mobile app.
- Growth also sends personalized email campaigns and tracks emails and website visits.
- Workflows of several emails start on Pro, at $49 billed yearly or $64 monthly, along with permissions and custom dashboards.
- The trial lasts 30 days with no credit card.

Salesflare is the closest swap for a folk user who wants automatic data capture and email follow ups from one tool. Its pricing page doesn't say whether LinkedIn messages sync into the CRM, so test that during the trial.

### 5. Breakcold

Breakcold is an AI CRM software that puts your email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp and Telegram conversations in one inbox, next to your pipeline. Its homepage calls it "the AI Native CRM for modern salespeople and AI agents," and its features page says you can add leads from LinkedIn, Sales Navigator, Gmail and more.

- [X1 costs $99 a month billed monthly](https://www.breakcold.com/pricing), for 1 seat and 2 connected accounts, and X5 costs $199 for 5 seats.
- Every plan has the full CRM and its AI agent, Chief of Staff, and the plans differ by seats and by how much AI work you get each week.
- Extra seats or connected accounts cost $10 a month each, and yearly billing takes 20% off.
- The trial lasts 14 days, and Breakcold asks for a card up front.

Of everything here, Breakcold feels closest to folkX, since it also saves people from LinkedIn and keeps their chats on the record. Its pricing page promises a tasks queue where "you approve every send," but we didn't find automated LinkedIn invitations there. Since X1 costs more than a folk Standard seat, Breakcold pays off best for a small team on X5, where 5 seats share one price.

### 6. Pipedrive

Pipedrive is a sales pipeline CRM software that helps small businesses track leads and close deals. Its AI Sales Assistant predicts which deals you're likely to win or lose and suggests which leads to pursue.

- Nova, its meeting intelligence, writes a brief before each call, records and transcribes it, and drafts CRM updates for you to approve.
- The homepage says Nova "is included in all plans, at no additional cost."
- The plans are Lite, Growth, Premium and Ultimate, according to Pipedrive's [pricing help article](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/how-does-pricing-work-in-pipedrive).
- The free trial lasts 14 days with no credit card.
- Its pricing page didn't load for us on November 3, so [check today's prices there](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/pricing).

Pipedrive suits a team that wants its week organized by deal stages more than by relationships. Interestingly, Nova's CRM updates wait for your approval, which is the same choice we made for the values Otto suggests in Omniscient.

### 7. Copper

Copper is a CRM software for service businesses that sell through relationships, built to work inside Google Workspace. You can add leads, track conversations, find files and manage tasks from Gmail and Google Calendar.

- [Basic costs $23 per seat a month billed yearly, or $29 monthly](https://www.copper.com/pricing), with up to 2,500 contacts, pipelines and contact enrichment.
- Professional costs $59, or $69 monthly, with 15,000 contacts, workflow automation, bulk email and reporting.
- Email series start on Business, at $99 billed yearly or $134 monthly, with unlimited contacts.
- You can start free with no credit card.

Copper names agencies and consulting firms among the businesses it's built for. Watch the 2,500 contact limit on Basic if your folk workspace holds a big network.

### 8. Streak

Streak is a CRM software that lives inside Gmail, so each email shows the pipeline and stage it belongs to. Its homepage calls it "the Gmail CRM for high-performing teams," and it gives Claude and ChatGPT live context on each deal.

- [Pro costs $49 per user a month billed yearly, or $59 monthly](https://www.streak.com/pricing), with unlimited pipelines, records and contacts, shared pipelines and mail merge.
- Pro+ costs $69, or $89 monthly, and adds automations, integrations, advanced reports and priority support.
- The free plan keeps email tracking and 50 mail merge sends a day, without CRM pipelines.
- The trial gives you 14 days of Pro+.

Streak's mail merge sends templated emails to a list with automatic follow ups, which covers much of what folk users move to Premium for. It's built for Gmail, so a team on Outlook will do better with Nimble, which syncs Microsoft 365.

### 9. Twenty

Twenty is an open source CRM software that you run in its cloud or host on your own servers, with custom objects and fields you add from Settings without code. Its FAQ calls it "the #1 Open Source CRM on GitHub."

- [Pro costs $9 per user a month billed yearly](https://twenty.com/pricing), with email and calendar sync, call recording, enrichment and a custom data model.
- Organization costs $39 and adds custom roles with field and row permissions, SSO and audit logs.
- Each member gets about 100 AI tasks a week on Pro, and about 500 on Organization.
- Hosting the open source core yourself is free, and the cloud trial lasts 30 days with no card.

Twenty suits a team with an engineer who wants to own the data and shape the CRM around it. Every cloud workspace also includes an MCP server, so an AI assistant can read and update your records. Expect to build more yourself than you did in folk, where pipelines and the LinkedIn extension come ready.

### 10. Clarify

Clarify is an AI CRM software that builds your contacts, companies and deals from your email and calendar, and calls itself "The Autonomous CRM." It records calls and drafts follow ups, and you pay for that AI work in credits.

- [Free costs $0](https://www.clarify.ai/pricing) with unlimited seats, 1,000 credits a month, 20,000 records and one campaign.
- Starter costs $40 a month for the whole team billed yearly, or $50 monthly, with 5,000 credits, 250,000 records, 5 campaigns and the LinkedIn integration.
- A meeting summary uses 30 credits and a campaign email uses 1, and Starter credits don't roll over.
- Growth starts at $1,000 a month, billed yearly.

Happily for a growing team, no plan charges per seat, which is the opposite of folk's per member pricing. The bill moves with AI use instead, so count the meetings you summarize each month before you choose a plan. Its homepage says it runs campaigns across email and LinkedIn, so test the LinkedIn side during your first week on Starter.

### 11. Close

Close is a sales CRM software with calling, email and SMS built in, and its headline reads "The CRM that sells with you." Its AI sales agent, Chloe, comes on every plan, and Close says she can call and qualify leads and book meetings.

- [Solo costs $9 a month billed yearly, or $19 monthly](https://www.close.com/pricing), for one user and up to 10,000 leads.
- Essentials costs $35 per user billed yearly, or $49 monthly, for a team with unlimited leads.
- Workflows, bulk email and the Power Dialer start on Growth, at $99 billed yearly or $109 monthly.
- Scale, at $139 or $149 monthly, adds the Predictive Dialer and live call coaching.
- The trial lasts 14 days with no credit card.

Frankly, Close is the one we'd pick for a team that books meetings by phone. folk now records calls you make from your own number, but it has no power dialer, and Omniscient has no calling at all. In Close, one workflow can send the first email, text the lead and queue a call for a rep.

### 12. Nimble

Nimble is a relationship CRM software for small teams that syncs your email, contacts and calendars from Microsoft or Google. Its homepage says "AI does the busywork," and its Prospector finds and enriches leads on LinkedIn and other websites.

- [The one plan costs $24.90 per seat a month billed yearly, or $29.90 monthly](https://www.nimble.com/pricing/), with 25,000 contact records for the account.
- Another 10,000 records cost $10 a month, and email marketing is a $15 monthly upgrade for the whole company.
- The trial lasts 14 days with no credit card, for up to 5 licenses.

Luckily for a small budget, Nimble has a single plan, so everyone on the team gets the same features. It also lists email sequences sent from your own address, but the pricing page doesn't say whether the plan includes them.

### 13. Dex

Dex is a personal CRM software that helps you organize your contacts and stay in touch with the people you know. It pulls contacts from more than 15 sources into one list, and its reminders tell you when to get back in touch.

- [Premium costs $12 a month billed yearly, or $20 monthly](https://getdex.com/pricing), and syncs up to 2,500 LinkedIn connections.
- Professional costs $20, or $34 monthly, syncs up to 9,000 connections and adds mail merge and the Outlook integration.
- Team costs $40 per user a month billed monthly, and adds CRM integrations and SAML SSO.
- The trial on Premium and Professional lasts 6 days, and Dex asks for a card up front.

Personally, we'd point a founder to Dex when folk was mostly a place to remember investors and advisors. For a pipeline with deals and stages, one of the CRMs above will fit better.

## How to move from folk to another CRM

folk's [export help article](https://help.folk.app/en/articles/5007388-export-data) says you export one group at a time, with "Export view as CSV" under the three dots by the view name. Each export is a zip with a contacts file and a notes file. Interactions, tasks and a few fields stay behind, so read the article's list before you start.

Here's the order we'd follow, with Omniscient as the example:

```steps
1. Show every column first: folk's export follows the columns and filters in the view, so display every field you want before you export each group.
2. Connect your inbox and calendar: Add Gmail or Outlook to the new CRM while folk keeps running, so the timeline starts filling.
3. Import companies first: Bring them in with their domains, so Omniscient can put each imported person on the company that matches their email domain.
4. Import people: Match each CSV column to a field, and keep in mind that rows without a valid email address are skipped.
5. Rebuild open deals: Recreate them as opportunities, and keep folk's notes file next to them for reference.
6. Give LinkedIn a slow start: A new Omniscient sender begins at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day, and a request waits up to 14 days for acceptance by default, so give your first campaign two weeks before you judge it.
```

Omniscient's CSV import takes people or companies, where people need an email address and companies need a domain. Fortunately, a record already in your workspace is matched on those and filled in, without overwriting anything your team set.
