# Best CRMs for LinkedIn Prospecting in 2026

When your deals start with a LinkedIn invitation, the most useful part of each conversation happens on a site your CRM can't see. Every CRM on this list closes that gap in its own way, and that choice decides how much your team still copies by hand.

We build Omniscient, a CRM with LinkedIn outreach inside it, and wiring our sending engine to the CRM showed us where these connections tend to break. This guide compares seven CRMs on how each one connects to LinkedIn, what lands on the record and what it costs in November 2026.

## What a CRM for LinkedIn prospecting needs to do

LinkedIn prospecting runs in a loop: you find someone, reach out, wait for an answer and follow up. A good CRM puts each turn of that loop on one record without anyone pasting it in, so these are the checks we'd run first:

- Saving a profile adds one person, matched to anyone already in your CRM.
- A LinkedIn reply shows up on that person's record, or right next to it.
- A reply stops any follow up still scheduled for that person.
- Email and meetings join the same record once the buyer moves off LinkedIn.
- Someone on your team decides what goes out from their LinkedIn account.

Honestly, vendors' pages say a lot about the first check and little about the rest, so we sorted this list by how each CRM connects to LinkedIn.

## Four ways a CRM connects to LinkedIn

Reading the vendors' pages side by side, we found four kinds of connection, and a few CRMs use more than one.

![Four ways a CRM connects to LinkedIn: Sales Navigator inside the CRM, sold by LinkedIn on Advanced Plus, used by HubSpot, Salesforce and Zoho CRM; a browser extension that saves the profile you have open, used by folk and Salesflare; a connected LinkedIn account where new messages land on the matching CRM person, used by Breakcold and Omniscient; and outreach inside the CRM that sends what your team approves, used by Omniscient](/assets/blog/best-crm-for-linkedin/four-ways.svg)

- LinkedIn sells Sales Navigator CRM Sync itself on the Advanced Plus plan, and its [plan guide](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a104003) says it logs "InMails, messages, notes, and Smart Links back to CRM."
- A browser extension saves the LinkedIn profile you have open to the CRM, which folk and Salesflare both offer.
- A connected LinkedIn account sends new LinkedIn messages to the matching CRM person, as in Breakcold and Omniscient.
- With outreach inside the CRM, the CRM sends invitations and messages itself, which only Omniscient does here.

Only the first is an integration LinkedIn sells. Its [prohibited software page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387) lists "browser extensions that scrape, modify the appearance of, or automate activity on LinkedIn's website" among the tools it doesn't permit. Members who use them, it says, "risk having their accounts restricted or shut down."

LinkedIn doesn't name products, and vendors design their tools to lower the risk. It still applies to the last three groups, Omniscient included, so weigh it against the hours each tool saves your team.

## The 7 best CRMs for LinkedIn prospecting in 2026

### 1. Omniscient

Omniscient is a CRM software that runs LinkedIn outreach your team approves and fills in its records from your email and calendar. Otto, our AI agent, writes each person's messages and drafts your replies.

We built the outreach and the CRM as one loop, so a lead, their reply and the deal stay on the same person:

- Scout turns a sentence into a lead list and scores each lead out of 100, with up to 60 points for fit and 40 for signals like hiring.
- You approve messages one person at a time or all at once at launch, and editing a message after approval sends it back for approval.
- Replies from every connected sender land in the Inbox, matched to the CRM person by LinkedIn member ID or profile address.
- A reply stops that person's outreach in every campaign, and you can create an opportunity from the thread at the stage you pick.

![Omniscient Inbox with a LinkedIn thread from Amara Kofi tagged Interested: her reply to a campaign message, a reply drafted by Otto waiting in the composer, and a side panel with her company, the Scout search and post comment that found her, her campaign marked replied with outreach stopped, and a Create opportunity button](/assets/blog/best-crm-for-linkedin/inbox.shot.html)

Sending follows the pacing we describe in [how to automate LinkedIn outreach without getting restricted](/blog/automate-linkedin-outreach-without-getting-restricted/):

- Each sender keeps working hours in its own time zone, with a lunch break, and waits 3 to 12 minutes between actions at normal pace.
- By default, a profile view comes 1 to 4 hours before each invitation.
- A new account starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day, and each day's total lands between 80% and 100% of your limit.
- Acceptance under 1 in 5 in a week, or a LinkedIn rate limit, halves the pace for 3 days before it eases back over the next 3.

LinkedIn's [help page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550555) doesn't publish an invitation limit, and our guide to the [LinkedIn connection request limit](/blog/linkedin-connection-request-limit/) explains the defaults we chose.

On the CRM side, Omniscient builds people, companies and meetings from Gmail or Outlook and your calendar, and Otto writes meeting notes from your call transcripts. The [CRM page](/product/crm/) and the [LinkedIn Outbound page](/product/linkedin-outbound/) show both halves.

Paid seats start at [$19 per seat a month, billed yearly](/#pricing), and Pro adds the pipeline board and forecast at $49. Monthly billing costs 20% more, and the 14 day trial needs no credit card.

Admittedly, Omniscient isn't on LinkedIn's CRM Sync list and has no browser extension for saving profiles as you browse. Teams that live in Sales Navigator Advanced Plus will get more from HubSpot or Salesforce, and teams that follow up with email sequences will prefer Salesflare.

### 2. HubSpot

HubSpot is a customer platform software that runs marketing, sales and service from one CRM, and it's one of the four CRMs on LinkedIn's CRM Sync list.

- [The free CRM covers up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm).
- [Sales Hub Starter shows $20 per seat a month, with a lower $7 starting price for new customers](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/sales).
- Professional starts at $90 per seat a month, plus a required one time onboarding fee of $1,500.
- The [LinkedIn CRM Sync app](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/integrations/connect-hubspot-and-linkedin-crm-sync) needs a Sales Hub Professional or Enterprise seat and Sales Navigator Advanced Plus.

With the app, you can create HubSpot contacts from Sales Navigator, and Sales Navigator activity logs to HubSpot on its own.

LinkedIn steps in HubSpot sequences are reminders, since the [sequences guide](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/sequences/create-and-edit-sequences) describes tasks to send a connection request or InMail manually. HubSpot fits a team that already pays for Advanced Plus and runs marketing in the same system.

### 3. Salesforce

Salesforce is a CRM platform software that scales from a free two user suite to large enterprise editions, and it's on LinkedIn's CRM Sync list.

- [Free Suite covers up to 2 users](https://www.salesforce.com/crm/free-crm/) at no cost, as long as you log in at least once every 60 days.
- [Starter Suite costs $25 per user a month](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/pricing/), Pro Suite costs $100 billed yearly, and Core costs $195 billed yearly.

The same guide says a LinkedIn account representative turns CRM Sync on, and a Salesforce administrator installs the app. Salesforce now sells suites while LinkedIn's guide names editions, so ask Salesforce which suite qualifies before you buy.

Personally, we'd pick Salesforce for LinkedIn prospecting only when an admin can own both sides of the sync.

### 4. Zoho CRM

Zoho CRM is a sales CRM software that helps teams run their pipeline, with a free plan for 3 users and an extension for LinkedIn Sales Navigator.

Once it's installed, leads, contacts, accounts and deals show a Sales Navigator section. You can send an invitation or start an InMail from the record, one person at a time.

The help page doesn't name the Zoho CRM edition the extension needs, so confirm that with Zoho during your trial.

## Sales Navigator and your CRM

Sales Navigator is LinkedIn's paid prospecting tool, and only its top plan connects to a CRM. LinkedIn's [help center](https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a8037044) puts it directly: "Integration between Sales Navigator and your CRM is only available for Sales Navigator Advanced Plus users."

Here's what each plan costs per license on LinkedIn's [compare plans page](https://business.linkedin.com/sell/sales-navigator/compare-plans):

- Core costs US$119.99 a month or US$1,079.88 a year, for one seller who needs search and alerts.

Advanced Plus pays off when your team wants Sales Navigator to mark which leads are already accounts or open deals in your CRM. If you only need search, Core or Advanced next to a CRM with an extension or a connected account can cover the rest.

## What we learned joining LinkedIn to a CRM

Building Omniscient's sending engine, we spent more time on records than on messages. A message goes out once, and a wrong record stays in front of your team until someone fixes it.

Each rule we settled on makes a fair question for any CRM you try:

- A search result stays a search result until you save it to a list or a campaign, and results left unsaved are deleted after 30 days.
- A person is matched first by LinkedIn member ID and then by profile address, because two people can share a name.
- A company is created only when its web domain is known, and it's never matched by name.
- New data fills empty fields and leaves anything a teammate typed exactly as it was.
- LinkedIn photo links expire, so we keep our own copy of each photo, with initials as the fallback.
- A send that times out is marked uncertain and never retried on its own, since we'd rather let the thread settle it than invite someone twice.

LinkedIn's CRM Sync makes a similar call. Its Salesforce guide says each LinkedIn profile links to a single CRM record, so when you keep duplicates, it picks the best scoring match. Frankly, that's a good reason to merge duplicates before you turn on any LinkedIn connection.

## How to choose a CRM for LinkedIn prospecting

Start from how your team prospects today, then use the trial to test where LinkedIn meets the record.

![Which CRM for LinkedIn prospecting to try first: a team that wants LinkedIn outreach it approves inside the CRM starts with Omniscient; one that already pays for Sales Navigator Advanced Plus starts with HubSpot or Salesforce; one that wants a free plan for up to 3 users starts with Zoho CRM; one that saves profiles by hand while browsing LinkedIn starts with folk or Salesflare; one that writes every LinkedIn message by hand starts with Breakcold](/assets/blog/best-crm-for-linkedin/pick-a-crm.svg)

```steps
1. List your senders: Write down who sends LinkedIn invitations today and whether each person will connect their own account.
2. Decide on Sales Navigator: Check whether you'll pay for Advanced Plus, since that decides whether CRM Sync is an option at all.
3. Save ten real profiles: Include people already in your CRM and count how many duplicates appear.
4. Reply from a second account: Answer one message and confirm the reply reaches the right record and stops any follow up.
5. Move one lead to email: Send an email and book a call, then check that both sit next to the LinkedIn history.
```
