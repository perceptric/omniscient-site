## What Pipedrive does well

Before you switch, list what Pipedrive does for you today, since you'd be giving it up. Its [homepage](https://www.pipedrive.com/en) puts these parts up front:

- Nova, its meeting assistant, writes a brief before each call, records and transcribes it, then drafts CRM updates you approve in one click.
- Nova is included in all plans, "at no additional cost."
- The AI Sales Assistant predicts which deals you're most likely to win or lose.
- The free trial lasts 14 days with no credit card.

Honestly, if your team plans its week around a pipeline board and sells mostly by email, Pipedrive is a calm, simple place to work.

## Why teams look for a Pipedrive alternative

Pipedrive's plans are now Lite, Growth, Premium and Ultimate, and its [help center article on the new plans](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/new-pipedrive-plans), updated September 3, 2026, lists these limits worth checking:

- Lite allows 2,500 leads and deals per user, and Growth allows 5,000.
- Sequences and the forecast view start on Growth, so a Lite team has neither.
- [LeadBooster](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/leadbooster-add-on), with a chatbot, live chat, Prospector and web forms, comes with Premium and costs extra on Lite and Growth.
- Premium allows up to 25 sequences, and Ultimate caps custom fields at 500, where the old Enterprise plan had no cap.
- Changing plans moves your account onto Pipedrive's latest list pricing, which matters if you signed up at an older price.

LinkedIn doesn't come up in the text of Pipedrive's homepage or its plans article. Teams that prospect there add a Marketplace app such as [LinkMatch](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/marketplace/app/link-match-for-pipedrive/6ceb1e3f05f1745a), a Chrome extension from Logical Pure Minds that imports LinkedIn profiles and syncs LinkedIn messages into Pipedrive.

## The 13 best Pipedrive alternatives in 2026

### 1. Omniscient

Omniscient is a CRM and LinkedIn outreach software that fills in your records from email, calendar and call transcripts, and sends only the LinkedIn messages your team approves. Otto, our AI agent, drafts those messages and your follow up emails after a call, and he suggests deal updates with the quote behind each one.

Our pipeline board comes with Pro, at $49 per seat a month billed yearly. Interestingly, Pipedrive's Nova also drafts CRM updates after a call for you to approve, so the idea won't be new to your team.

![Omniscient's pipeline board after a Pipedrive import, with opportunities in the stages brought over from Pipedrive: Qualified, Contact made, Demo scheduled and Proposal made. Each card shows its amount, owner and next step, and a suggested update from a call raises Pallet Cloud's amount from $18,000 to $30,000, waiting to be accepted or skipped.](/assets/blog/pipedrive-alternatives/pipeline.shot.html)

On the LinkedIn side, Scout finds leads from a sentence describing who you want. It scores each one out of 100, with up to 60 points for fit and 40 for timing signals like hiring. LinkedIn's [invitation limit page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550555) gives no exact number, so when we built our sending engine this year, we made each sender pace itself:

- A new sender starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day until it reaches the limit you set.
- Each day's total lands between 80% and 100% of that limit, with random gaps of 3 to 12 minutes inside working hours.
- When LinkedIn rate limits the account, or fewer than 1 in 5 of the last week's invitations are accepted, the sender drops to half pace for 3 days, then eases back to full over the next 3.

Replies land in one Inbox next to the person's CRM record, and a reply stops that person's outreach in every campaign.

Admittedly, we have no mobile app and no dialer, and we don't send email sequences, since each email Otto drafts goes out only when you send it. A team that runs email cadences or sells by phone will do better with Pipedrive Growth or Close.

Teammates who only view or log data are free, paid seats start at [$19 per seat a month billed yearly](/#pricing), and the 14 day trial needs no credit card. The [CRM page](/product/crm/) and the [Outbound LinkedIn page](/product/linkedin-outbound/) cover the rest.

### 2. HubSpot

HubSpot is a customer platform software that puts marketing, sales and service tools on one CRM. Its [free CRM](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm) is "100% free with no expiration date" for up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts. It includes Breeze Assistant, which researches companies and helps you prepare for sales calls.

- [Sales Hub Starter](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/sales) shows $7 per seat a month as its "starts at" price, with $20 beside it, and the page says discounts are for new customers only.
- The plan comparison lists sequences under Professional and Enterprise.
- Professional starts at $90 per seat a month, plus a $1,500 onboarding fee you pay once.

Thankfully, the free plan has no end date, so you can rebuild a small pipeline there and test it before you pay. For LinkedIn, HubSpot's [Sales Navigator sync](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/integrations/connect-hubspot-and-linkedin-crm-sync) needs a Sales Hub Professional or Enterprise seat and LinkedIn's Sales Navigator Advanced Plus plan.

HubSpot fits a team that wants marketing and sales on one record and can grow into Professional. Our [HubSpot CRM alternatives](/blog/hubspot-crm-alternatives/) guide covers what happens when that bill arrives.

### 3. Zoho CRM

Zoho CRM is a cloud CRM software that helps sales teams respond to leads and run their pipeline, with AI agents from its Zia Agent Store. Its [edition comparison](https://www.zoho.com/crm/comparison.html) lists these limits:

- The Free edition is free for 3 users, with one standard pipeline.
- Standard adds multiple pipelines, 5 forecasts and cadences, Zoho's name for sequences, with up to 10 active at a time per module.
- Process management, which Zoho calls Blueprint, starts on Professional.
- The pages showed us prices in Indian rupees, so [check the price in your currency](https://www.zoho.com/crm/zohocrm-pricing.html).

Luckily for a team of three, the free edition lets you rebuild one Pipedrive pipeline at no cost. Once you need a second pipeline or cadences, plan on Standard.

Zoho CRM suits a small team that wants to start free and keep growing with the same vendor.

### 4. Close

Close is a sales CRM software that puts calling and SMS next to email, built for teams that sell by phone. Its AI assistant, Chloe, answers questions about your pipeline, meetings and calls from your own data.

- [Solo shows $9 or $19 a month](https://www.close.com/pricing) and is limited to 1 user, and Essentials shows $35 or $49 per user, without saying which price is yearly.
- Workflows and the Power Dialer start on Growth, which shows $99 or $109 per user.
- The trial lasts 14 days with no credit card and includes $5 of calling and enrichment credits.

Frankly, Close is the CRM we'd hand a team that lives on the phone, and we say that as builders of a CRM with no dialer. Once you hit dial on a list, its homepage says, "Close moves through the list on its own, connecting you the moment someone picks up."

The catch is the jump to Growth, since the dialer that makes Close worth it sits there. Close fits a team that makes dozens of calls a day.

### 5. Freshsales

Freshsales is an AI sales CRM software from Freshworks that helps teams capture and qualify leads with Freddy AI. Freddy scores each lead's intent so your reps know whom to call first, and it makes recommendations on deals.

- [Growth costs $9 per user a month, billed annually](https://www.freshworks.com/crm/pricing/), with a Kanban view, chat, email and phone, and basic workflows.
- Sales sequences, contact scoring and deal insights start on Pro, at $39.
- Forecasting insights start on Enterprise, at $59.
- The Freddy AI Agent is sold separately at $49 per 100 sessions, and paid plans get 500 trial sessions once.
- The trial lasts 21 days with no credit card.

Freshsales suits a small team that wants calls and email in one CRM. If AI is part of why you're switching, budget for the Freddy AI Agent too.

### 6. monday CRM

monday CRM is a CRM software built on monday.com's boards that helps revenue teams manage their pipelines. Its homepage says calls, emails and meetings fill the right CRM fields automatically, and it names agents for lead sourcing, calling, pipeline monitoring and meeting prep.

- [Basic shows $12 per seat a month](https://monday.com/crm/pricing), next to a crossed out $18, with 1,000 active contacts and deals.

- AI agents come with separate AI plans that include AI credits.

Happily, a team that already runs projects or marketing in monday keeps its deals on the same boards. A sales team on its own should price in the 3 seat minimum and the separate AI plans.

### 7. folk

folk is an AI CRM software that helps small sales teams and agencies keep their contacts and deals in shared pipelines. Its Chrome extension, folkX, imports people and lists from LinkedIn and Sales Navigator, and its homepage names Pipedrive among the tools its pipelines replace.

- [Standard costs $24 per member a month billed yearly, or $30 monthly](https://www.folk.app/pricing), with pipelines, email campaigns and folkX.

folk fits founders whose pipeline is mostly people they already know or meet on LinkedIn. Our list of the [best CRM for LinkedIn](/blog/best-crm-for-linkedin/) compares how folk and the others connect to LinkedIn.

### 8. Attio

Attio is a CRM software that sets itself up around your business once you connect your inbox and calendar, with a data model you can reshape as you grow. Its homepage opens with "Welcome to agentic revenue" and describes agents that pick up leads at 2am and catch renewals before they slip.

- [Free covers up to 3 seats and 3 objects](https://attio.com/pricing), and Plus costs $35 per user a month billed yearly, or $44 monthly, for up to 10 seats.

Attio fits when your records have outgrown deals and stages, like partners or product usage tracked next to the pipeline. Our [Attio alternatives](/blog/attio-alternatives/) guide covers the trip in the other direction.

### 9. Nutshell

Nutshell is a sales CRM software that helps small businesses run their pipeline, with email marketing sold as an add on. Its homepage says it "automatically transcribes, summarizes, and logs every call and meeting," so your call notes reach the CRM without typing.

- [Foundation costs $13 per user a month](https://www.nutshell.com/pricing), with unlimited contacts, email and calendar sync, and up to 100 open leads.

- Pro costs $42 and adds 5 custom pipelines, sales automation and AI recaps with next steps for each lead.

Fortunately, Nutshell moves your data for free during the trial, which covers the hardest part of a switch. Watch the Foundation limit, though: Pipedrive Lite counts 2,500 leads and deals per user, while Nutshell Foundation stops at 100 open leads. Its pricing page doesn't name sequences or forecasting on any plan, so ask about both before you commit.

Nutshell fits a small business that wants its pipeline and email marketing from one vendor.

### 10. Salesflare

Salesflare is a B2B CRM software that fills in contacts and deals from your inbox, email signatures, calendar and company websites. Of every tool here, Salesflare's pitch sits closest to ours. When we built our own inbox sync, we learned to skip noreply senders and the bots that meeting notetakers send as guests. Check who Salesflare turns into a contact from a busy inbox.

Its pricing page doesn't list AI features on any plan. Salesflare suits a small B2B team that wants automatic capture plus email campaigns, which Omniscient doesn't send.

Copper deserves a trial if you sell services and your day already happens in Gmail.

### 12. Streak

Streak is a Gmail CRM software that keeps your pipelines inside your inbox and ties each email thread to its deal and stage. Its homepage calls it "The Gmail CRM for high-performing teams," and its AI fills in deal records from inbox activity and answers questions about your deal history.

Surprisingly for a Gmail add on, Streak has pages for fundraising and hiring teams, so a founder can track investors next to deals. Its first paid plan costs more than any other starting price we could confirm here, and automations wait for Pro+.

Streak fits a founder or a small team that works out of Gmail all day and would rather keep the pipeline there than in another tab.

### 13. Less Annoying CRM

Less Annoying CRM is a simple CRM software that helps small businesses track contacts, leads and follow ups for one flat price. Its homepage calls it "More than a spreadsheet, less than a CRM."

Refreshingly, there's one plan and nothing to upgrade to, the opposite of Pipedrive's four tiers with a limit at each step. The pricing page suggests staying under about 50,000 contacts, and neither it nor the homepage mentions AI, email sequences or LinkedIn.

Less Annoying CRM fits a small business that finds Pipedrive's plans more than it needs and mostly wants a tidy contact list with reminders to follow up.

## How to move from Pipedrive to another CRM

We'd run both CRMs side by side for the length of a trial, about two weeks, in this order:

```steps
1. Connect your inbox first: Add Gmail or Outlook and your calendar to the new CRM while Pipedrive keeps running.
2. Add missing email addresses: Our importer skips people without one and matches everyone else by email, so give an address to each person you plan to keep.
3. Export what won't come over: Save anything the new importer skips as spreadsheets. Ours leaves activities, products, Leads Inbox items and custom fields behind.
4. Import, then spot check: Move organizations, people, deals and notes, and open a few deals to confirm each kept its stage, amount and owner.
5. Rebuild automations last: Recreate workflows and sequences once the data settles, so nothing fires on a half imported deal.
```

In Omniscient, each imported pipeline keeps its stages and their win probabilities, and Won and Lost stages are added where they're missing. A few more rules decide how your records land:

- Won and lost deals keep their status, and each owner is matched to a teammate by email.
- People already in your workspace are matched by email, and running the import again matches records by their Pipedrive ID, so nothing doubles up.
- Pipedrive organizations come over without a website, so each company takes its domain from its people's work emails, skipping personal addresses like Gmail.

![How Omniscient imports Pipedrive: organizations become companies, with the website taken from people's work emails; persons become people, matched by email with no duplicates; pipelines become pipelines and stages, keeping win odds and adding Won and Lost; deals become opportunities, and won and lost deals keep their status; notes stay linked to the same records. People without an email are skipped, and activities, leads, products and custom fields stay behind, so export them first.](/assets/blog/pipedrive-alternatives/import.svg)

Omniscient has no task list, so write each open deal's next activity into its Next step field once the import finishes. From then on, Otto suggests updates to your deals from meetings and emails. The hardest call we made was deciding when he may change a deal without asking you, and we settled on these rules:

- You set every field to off, suggest or auto.
- A value with no quote behind it, or one Otto is less than 60% sure of, is dropped before you see it.
- An auto field fills itself only when it's empty and Otto is at least 85% sure.
- A value someone on your team typed is never changed.
- Everything else waits for you to accept or skip, like an amount that moved on a call.

Our guide to the [CRM with automatic data entry](/blog/crm-with-automatic-data-entry/) shows how other CRMs handle the same choice. If you'd rather not run the import yourself, send us an export and we map your fields, move your companies, contacts, deals and notes, and check the result with you.
