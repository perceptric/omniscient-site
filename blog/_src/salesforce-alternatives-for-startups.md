## Why startups look for a Salesforce alternative

Salesforce sells six Sales Cloud editions, and here's what its [Sales Cloud pricing page](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/pricing/) and [free CRM page](https://www.salesforce.com/crm/free-crm/) say today:

- The Free Suite covers up to 2 users with no end date, as long as someone logs in at least once every 60 days, and it sends up to 100 emails a month.
- Starter Suite costs $25 per user a month, and it's the only edition you can pay for month by month.
- Pro Suite costs $100 per user a month, billed yearly, and it's where sales quoting and forecasting start.
- Core, Advanced and Max cost $195, $395 and $550 per user a month, all billed yearly.
- Agentforce for Sales is an add on from $125 per user a month, and the page says AI can be added to Core and above.

![Price per user a month for each Salesforce Sales Cloud edition, from Salesforce's pricing page on November 3, 2026. Free Suite: $0, for up to 2 users. Starter Suite: $25, billed monthly or yearly. Pro Suite: $100, billed yearly, and the first edition with quoting and forecasting. Core: $195, Advanced: $395 and Max: $550, each billed yearly. Agentforce for Sales is an add on from $125 per user a month.](/assets/blog/salesforce-alternatives-for-startups/salesforce-editions.svg)

Honestly, Starter Suite at $25 is a fair price for a small team that only needs leads, accounts and a pipeline. The bigger step is Pro Suite, where forecasting and quotes start and monthly billing is no longer on offer.

Building our importer, we also saw how Salesforce spreads a startup's history across separate objects. Leads and contacts live apart, and every custom field someone added is one more thing to carry over or leave behind.

If you're venture backed, ask about [Salesforce Launchpad](https://www.salesforce.com/solutions/small-business-solutions/salesforce-for-startups/) before you leave. Salesforce says it adds discounts and implementation support to Starter and Pro Suite, but the page doesn't say how much, so ask for the terms.

## When staying on Salesforce makes sense

A startup whose Salesforce setup already works has less to gain from a move. We'd stay on Salesforce and revisit it at renewal in these cases:

- You sell to large companies with several sales processes, territories or approval steps.
- Someone on your team already runs Salesforce well, so the setup work is done.
- A Launchpad discount covers the next year or two at a price you're happy with.
- Your team uses custom objects and automations you built in Salesforce every day.

## The 13 best Salesforce alternatives for startups

### 1. Omniscient

Omniscient is an AI CRM that fills in its records from the email, calendar and call transcripts you connect, with a Deal Room for proposals and Contract Management in the same app. Otto, our AI agent, writes your meeting notes and proposes field values, each with the quote that backs it.

Coming from Salesforce, the big change is who does the typing. Each email and meeting lands on the right company and person, so records stay current in your busiest weeks.

- [Starter](/#pricing) costs $19 per seat a month billed yearly, with record pages, meeting notes from transcripts and follow up email drafts.
- Pro costs $49 and adds the pipeline board and forecast, custom fields and fields filled with proof.
- Business costs $99 and adds a manager view with call coaching, plus MEDDICC and BANT from calls.
- Teammates who only view or log data are free, monthly billing costs 20% more, and the 14 day trial needs no card.

Otto fills a field only when it's empty, he's at least 85% sure and he can show the quote. He never changes a value your team typed, because a wrong amount in a forecast costs more than an empty one.

Salesforce starts quoting on Pro Suite at $100. In Omniscient, the [Deal Room](/product/deal-room/) builds a proposal page for each deal from the deal, your calls and your Library, shows who viewed each part, and lets the buyer approve on the page. A full proposal uses 40 of the proposal credits each paid seat adds every month.

[Contract Management](/product/contract-management/) comes with the seat. It drafts the contract from the approved proposal and your template, keeps every redline, sends a discount or a changed term to an approver and collects e-signatures with a link for each signer. It also reads each contract's end date and notice period, and opens a renewal opportunity 90 days before the end date.

Admittedly, Omniscient covers less ground than Salesforce in places. We don't send marketing emails or email sequences, there's no dialer, and our importer leaves custom objects and custom fields behind. Everything else is on the [CRM page](/product/crm/).

### 2. HubSpot

HubSpot is a customer platform that runs marketing, sales and service teams from one shared CRM, with Breeze Assistant as its AI helper.

- [The free CRM covers up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts](https://www.hubspot.com/products/crm), with no expiration date.
- [Sales Hub Starter shows $20 per seat a month](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/sales) next to a "starts at" price of $7, which HubSpot marks as a discount for new customers only.
- Sequences and forecasting start on Professional, from $90 per seat a month, plus a required one time onboarding fee of $1,500.
- [HubSpot for Startups](https://www.hubspot.com/startups) lists 90% off year one, 50% off year two and 25% off year three on new Professional or Enterprise products, for startups funded up to Series A.

HubSpot fits a startup that wants marketing and sales to share one system. Luckily for two founders, the free CRM has no end date, so you can wait to pay until your first hire.

HubSpot's startup FAQ calls the discount period "typically two years" and asks for a yearly commitment, so confirm the third year before you plan around it. Our list of [HubSpot CRM alternatives](/blog/hubspot-crm-alternatives/) covers where its plans step up.

### 3. Attio

Attio is a CRM that builds pipeline and advances deals with AI agents, on objects and fields you shape around your business. Its homepage lists custom agents, plus a Web Agent that researches the web for you.

- [The Free plan covers up to 3 seats](https://attio.com/pricing), 3 objects and 50,000 records.
- Plus costs $35 per seat a month billed yearly, or $44 monthly, for up to 10 seats and 5 objects.
- Pro costs $79 billed yearly, or $99 monthly, and adds sequences, Call Intelligence and up to 12 objects.
- [Attio for Startups](https://attio.com/startups) takes 80% off the yearly Pro plan and credits in year one, and 30% in year two, for venture backed workspaces of up to 10 seats.
- The FAQ lists a 14 day trial of Pro, and you can start without a credit card.

If your team relied on Salesforce's custom objects, Attio is the one we'd look at first. Its site also links to a guide for moving data from Salesforce.

Apply for the startup offer early, since its 90 day window opens the day you create the workspace. Our [Omniscient vs Attio](/blog/omniscient-vs-attio/) comparison puts the two side by side.

### 4. Pipedrive

Pipedrive is a sales pipeline CRM that tracks every deal by stage and helps you manage leads. Its AI Sales Assistant predicts which deals you're most likely to win or lose.

- Nova, its meeting intelligence, writes a brief before each call, records and transcribes it, and drafts CRM updates for you to approve.
- The [homepage](https://www.pipedrive.com/en) says Nova comes with all plans at no extra cost, and Pipedrive offers a 14 day free trial with no credit card.
- Its [help center](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/how-does-pricing-work-in-pipedrive) lists four plans, Lite, Growth, Premium and Ultimate, and bills every seat whether or not anyone is assigned to it.
- Its [pricing page](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/pricing) didn't load for us on November 3, so check prices there.

Pipedrive suits a startup whose first sales hire plans the week by pipeline stage. Since empty seats are billed too, remove leftover seats before each renewal.

### 5. Zoho CRM

Zoho CRM is a sales CRM that helps teams answer leads faster and run leaner sales operations, with AI agents from its assistant, Zia.

- [The Free edition is free forever for 3 users](https://www.zoho.com/crm/zohocrm-pricing.html), with contact management, workflow automation and up to 10 email templates.
- Standard costs $14 per user a month billed yearly, or $20 monthly, and includes sales forecasting, custom modules and several pipelines.
- Professional costs $23 billed yearly, or $35 monthly, and adds AI agents and quoting tools (CPQ).
- For teams switching from Salesforce or HubSpot, the [homepage](https://www.zoho.com/crm/) offers "free migration assistance, and if you're locked mid-contract, Zoho CRM is on us for 6 months."

Interestingly, Zoho includes sales forecasting on Standard, while Salesforce keeps it for Pro Suite. If you're partway through a Salesforce contract, read the terms of Zoho's six month offer before you sign anything.

### 6. folk

folk is an AI CRM that helps teams close deals and find new clients, with a Chrome extension that adds people from LinkedIn and Sales Navigator. Its assistants score leads against your ideal customer and add company details from the web.

- [Standard costs $24 per member a month billed yearly, or $30 monthly](https://www.folk.app/pricing), with the LinkedIn extension, email campaigns and AI magic fields.
- Premium costs $48, or $60 monthly, and adds email sequences, custom objects, dashboards and API access.
- The 2 week trial includes every premium feature and needs no credit card.

folk fits a founder whose deals start on LinkedIn or through warm introductions, where a tidy contact list matters more than stages and forecasts. If you used custom objects in Salesforce, price Premium, since that's where folk's custom objects start.

### 7. Close

Close is a sales CRM that lets teams call, text and email their leads from one place, with automation and AI built in. Chloe, its AI assistant, answers questions about your pipeline, meetings and calls from your own data.

- [Solo shows $9 or $19 a month for one user](https://www.close.com/pricing), depending on billing, with up to 10,000 leads.
- Essentials shows $35 or $49 per user with unlimited contacts, and every plan includes calling and SMS.
- Growth shows $99 or $109 per user and adds workflows and the Power Dialer.
- The 14 day trial needs no credit card and includes $5 of calling and enrichment credits, and paid plans come with a 30 day money back guarantee.

Happily, a founder who sells by phone can start on Solo and move to Essentials once the first rep joins. If your reps spend most of the day on calls, Close is the one we'd send you to, since Omniscient has no dialer.

### 8. Salesflare

Salesflare is a B2B CRM that fills in contacts and companies from your emails, calendar and email signatures. Its homepage headline is "The CRM that keeps itself up to date," and the same page says it fills the CRM "semi-automatically."

- [Growth costs $29 per user a month billed yearly, or $39 monthly](https://salesflare.com/pricing), with automatic data capture, personalized email campaigns and a LinkedIn sidebar.
- Pro costs $49 yearly, or $64 monthly, and adds email workflows of several steps, user permissions, custom dashboards and 100 lead credits.
- Enterprise costs $99 yearly, or $124 monthly, needs at least 5 users and includes data migration and training.
- The trial runs 30 days with no credit card.

Salesflare's pitch sits closest to ours. When we built inbox capture, we had to skip noreply addresses, newsletter tools and the bots that meeting notetakers send as guests, so the CRM doesn't fill up with contacts you'll never sell to. Run Salesflare on your busiest inbox during the trial and look at who it adds.

It suits a small B2B team that wants automatic capture plus email campaigns, which Omniscient doesn't send. Our guide to [CRMs with automatic data entry](/blog/crm-with-automatic-data-entry/) compares more of them.

### 9. Freshsales

Freshsales is a sales CRM from Freshworks that helps teams capture and qualify their leads. On its [sales page](https://www.freshworks.com/crm/sales/), Freddy AI scores each lead's intent and makes deal recommendations.

- [Growth costs $9 per user a month, billed annually](https://www.freshworks.com/crm/pricing/), with a kanban pipeline, email, phone and chat, custom fields and basic workflows.
- Pro costs $39 and adds sales sequences, contact scoring, deal insights and territory management.
- Enterprise costs $59 and adds forecasting insights, custom modules, a sandbox and audit logs.
- The Freddy AI Agent chatbot is an add on at $49 per 100 sessions, and the trial lasts 21 days with no credit card.

Freshsales keeps much of Salesforce's shape at a lower seat price. Sequences arrive on Pro, and forecasting insights wait for Enterprise at $59, where Salesforce asks $100 a seat for Pro Suite.

The pricing page lists only annual prices, so ask about monthly billing before you commit. Freshsales suits a startup that wants calling and sequences in one CRM without Salesforce's setup work.

### 10. monday CRM

monday CRM is a CRM built on monday.com's boards that puts your sales pipeline next to the rest of your team's work. Its page says "every call, email, and meeting auto-populates to the right CRM data fields," and names agents for lead sourcing, calling, pipeline monitoring and meeting prep.

- [Basic costs $12 per seat a month billed yearly, or $18 monthly](https://monday.com/crm/pricing), for up to 1,000 active contacts and deals.
- Standard costs $17 yearly, or $25 monthly, raises the limit to 10,000, and its AI version adds a lead sourcer, an outreach caller and an AI notetaker.
- Pro costs $28 yearly, or $41 monthly, allows 100,000 contacts and deals, and adds email sequences and mass email.
- Every plan starts at 3 seats, there's no free plan, and the trial lasts 14 days.

Frankly, the 3 seat minimum is the first thing a two founder team will notice, since you pay for a third seat you don't use yet. Basic also allows 5 columns per board, so a team that tracks more than a handful of deal fields will likely need Standard, which allows 15.

monday CRM makes the most sense when your startup already plans its work on monday.com and wants sales on the same boards.

### 11. Copper

Copper is a CRM for Google Workspace that runs inside Gmail and Google Calendar and logs your emails and meetings as you work. Its homepage describes it as a CRM for "relationship-focused service businesses."

- [Basic costs $23 per seat a month billed yearly, or $29 monthly](https://www.copper.com/pricing), with pipelines, task automation and up to 2,500 contacts.
- Professional costs $59 yearly, or $69 monthly, and adds workflow automation, bulk email, reporting and up to 15,000 contacts.
- Business costs $99 yearly, or $134 monthly, and adds email series, custom reports and unlimited contacts.
- You can start free with no credit card, though the page doesn't say how long the trial lasts.

For a startup leaving Salesforce, Copper's draw is that you update deals without leaving Gmail. Email series only arrive on Business at $99 a seat, though. Its homepage and pricing page name only Google tools, so a team on Microsoft 365 should ask Copper what works before it buys.

Copper deserves a trial if you sell services and your day already happens in Gmail.

### 12. Nutshell

Nutshell is a sales CRM that helps small businesses run their pipeline, with email marketing sold as an add on. Its homepage says it "automatically transcribes, summarizes, and logs every call and meeting."

- [Foundation costs $13 per user a month](https://www.nutshell.com/pricing), with unlimited contacts, email and calendar sync and up to 100 open leads, and the page says yearly billing saves $6 a month.
- Growth costs $25 and adds activity reports, sales quotas and rules for assigning leads.
- Pro costs $42 and adds 5 pipelines, sales automation, advanced reporting and AI recaps for each lead.
- The Marketing add on costs $49 a month, and Engagement, with two way SMS and WhatsApp, costs $16 per user.
- The 14 day trial needs no credit card.

Refreshingly, Nutshell offers free support and data migration during the trial, which helps when your history sits in Salesforce. The 100 open lead cap on Foundation will pinch a startup doing steady outbound, and no plan lists forecasting, so ask about it before you move.

Nutshell fits a small business that wants its pipeline and email marketing from one vendor.

### 13. Twenty

Twenty is an open source CRM that you can host on your own servers or run on its managed cloud. Its homepage calls it "the #1 Open Source CRM on GitHub," and every cloud workspace comes with an MCP server for AI tools.

- [Cloud Pro costs $9 per user a month billed yearly](https://twenty.com/pricing), with email and calendar sync, a custom data model and workflows.
- Organization costs $39 billed yearly and adds custom roles, SSO and audit logs.
- The cloud trial lasts 30 days with no credit card.
- To move off Salesforce, the [homepage](https://twenty.com/) points to CSV import, its API for larger imports, or partners who run the whole migration.

We'd pick Twenty for a startup with engineers who want to own the CRM's code and data. Fortunately, the open source core itself is free, though you then pay for your own servers and someone has to look after the upgrades.

## How to move from Salesforce to another CRM

We built Omniscient's Salesforce importer ourselves, and most of what we learned applies whichever CRM you pick. You sign in with Salesforce, the importer only reads, and it deletes its saved Salesforce login once the import finishes.

![What Omniscient's Salesforce importer brings over and where each record lands. Accounts become companies, matched by domain. Contacts with an email become people, linked to their company. Leads not yet converted become people, with a company from their domain. Active opportunity stages become one pipeline, with won and lost stages kept. Opportunities with amount, close date and owner become opportunities, with the owner matched by email. Classic notes on accounts, contacts and opportunities land on the same record. Tasks, events, cases, files, enhanced notes, custom objects and custom fields stay in Salesforce, and contacts and leads with no email are skipped.](/assets/blog/salesforce-alternatives-for-startups/import-map.svg)

These details shaped our importer, and they're worth checking in any CRM's import:

- Leads and contacts are separate in Salesforce, so check that unconverted leads come over too. Ours brings them in as people and links each one to a company when its website or work email gives a domain.
- Contacts and leads without an email address are skipped, so export them first if you want to keep them.
- Every active opportunity stage lands in one pipeline called Sales, so split it into pipelines afterwards if you run more than one sales process.
- Salesforce has two note tools, and [its help article](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=000384089&language=en_US&type=1) says a note opens only in the tool that made it. Ours reads classic notes, so check which kind your team writes.
- Opportunity owners are matched to teammates by email, and running the import again updates each opportunity's stage, amount and close date.

Thankfully, a read only import leaves Salesforce as it was, so you can run both side by side until the numbers match. We'd switch in this order:

```steps
1. Check your renewal date: Pro Suite and the editions above it are billed yearly, so time the switch to the end of your contract.
2. Save what won't move: Export tasks, cases, files and custom objects, and write down your flows and alerts.
3. Connect your inbox and calendar: Add Gmail or Outlook first, so new activity lands in the new CRM.
4. Invite your team: Use the email addresses they use in Salesforce, so opportunity owners match.
5. Run the import: Bring everything over, then check five opportunities by hand for stage, amount and owner.
6. Rebuild automations last: Recreate flows once the data settles, so nothing fires on a half imported record.
```
