Salesforce shows a startup every account, lead and opportunity it records. What it hides is the work behind them: someone still logs each email, call and next step, and forecasting starts on Pro Suite at $100 per user a month.

Salesforce alternatives for startups are CRMs that keep your pipeline current with less setup and a smaller bill. Many now capture emails, calls and meetings on their own and work on day one, which suits founders, SDRs, account executives and agencies at an early stage.

The result is a CRM your team keeps up to date, at a price that fits a seed budget. 🚀

What Is a Salesforce Alternative?

A Salesforce alternative is a CRM that tracks your accounts, contacts and deals without Salesforce's setup work or edition pricing. It connects to your inbox and calendar, then logs each conversation on the right company, person and deal. The newer ones add an AI agent that fills in fields, drafts follow-ups and answers questions about your pipeline.

Most run as a web app with a mobile app, a Chrome extension or a Gmail sidebar, so reps update deals where they already work.

Most tools include:

  • ✅ Email and calendar sync
  • ✅ A visual deal pipeline
  • ✅ Custom fields and objects
  • ✅ Imports from Salesforce

The payoff is a CRM that a team of three can run without an admin.

Why Do You Need a Salesforce Alternative for Your Startup?

Salesforce's Free Suite covers up to 2 users, so two founders can start at no cost. Starter Suite costs $25 per user a month, and quoting and forecasting start on Pro Suite at $100, billed yearly. A Salesforce alternative solves this.

  • Room to hire: A third or fourth seat costs less, and several tools have a free plan.
  • Less setup: Pipelines, fields and imports work on day one, with no admin needed.
  • A forecast sooner: Several alternatives include forecasting on a lower tier than Pro Suite.
  • Less manual logging: An agentic CRM captures every email, call and meeting, then keeps each deal current for you.

Where Salesforce Shines

Salesforce is a strong CRM, and if your setup already works, it can be a good place to stay. Here is what it does well for a startup:

  • A free start. The Free Suite covers up to 2 users, so two founders can begin at no cost.
  • Room to grow. Six Sales Cloud editions take a team from Starter Suite at $25 per user a month to Core, Advanced and Max.
  • Deep custom setup. Custom objects, territories and approval steps suit startups that sell to large companies with complex buying processes.
  • A huge ecosystem. Many partners, consultants and apps already work with Salesforce.

We'd stay on Salesforce and revisit it at renewal in these cases:

  • You sell to large companies with several sales processes, territories or approval steps.
  • Someone on your team already runs Salesforce well, so the setup work is done.
  • Your team uses custom objects and automations you built in Salesforce every day.

How to Choose a Salesforce Alternative

Start from the way your startup sells, then test the shortlist on real deals. These steps keep the choice simple:

  1. Name your main job. Pick the one thing the CRM must do best, such as finding leads, logging calls or running marketing.
  2. Count your seats for the next year. Price each tool for the team you'll have at renewal, on yearly billing.
  3. Check where AI is priced. Some CRMs include AI in the seat, and others sell it as an add-on or with credits.
  4. Look at the import. Make sure the tool can bring over your accounts, contacts, leads and opportunities from Salesforce.
  5. Run a free trial on live deals. Connect your inbox and calendar, and see how much the CRM fills in on its own in a week.

How to Move from Salesforce to Another CRM

Most of what matters in a Salesforce move applies whichever CRM you pick. These details are worth checking:

  • Leads and contacts. Salesforce keeps them apart, so check that unconverted leads come over too.
  • Records without an email. Some importers skip contacts and leads with no email, so export them first if you want to keep them.
  • Pipelines. Check how your opportunity stages map, and split them into more than one pipeline if you run several sales processes.
  • Notes. Salesforce has two note tools, and each note opens in the tool that made it, so check which kind your team writes.
  • Owners. Opportunity owners usually match by email, so invite teammates with the same addresses they use in Salesforce.

Omniscient imports from Salesforce, HubSpot, Attio and Pipedrive. We'd switch in this order:

  1. 01
    Check your renewal date

    Pro Suite and the editions above it are billed yearly, so time the switch to the end of your billing year.

  2. 02
    Save what won't move

    Export files and custom objects, and write down your flows and alerts.

  3. 03
    Connect your inbox and calendar

    Add Gmail or Outlook first, so new activity lands in the new CRM.

  4. 04
    Invite your team

    Use the email addresses they use in Salesforce, so opportunity owners match.

  5. 05
    Run the import

    Bring everything over, then check five opportunities by hand for stage, amount and owner.

  6. 06
    Rebuild automations last

    Recreate flows once the data settles, so nothing fires on a half-imported record.

Run both CRMs side by side until the numbers match, then switch. Our guide to the best CRM for startups compares a few more options.
