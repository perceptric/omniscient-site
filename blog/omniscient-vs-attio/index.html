If you're weighing Omniscient vs Attio, you probably want a CRM that stays current without extra typing. Both read your email and calendar, and they part ways once a deal needs a proposal and a contract.

We build Omniscient, so read this knowing which side we sit on. We read Attio's pricing page and help center again on November 3, 2026. We haven't run Attio with a live sales team, so what we say about it comes from what Attio and the app makers publish.

Our short answer depends mostly on how your deals close:

  • Pick Attio if your team works mostly in email and wants to shape the CRM around custom objects of its own.
  • Pick Omniscient if your deals close on a proposal and a contract, and you want both on a record that fills itself in from your email and calls.
  • Run both for a few weeks if you're unsure, since Omniscient works beside your current CRM.

What Attio does well

Attio is a flexible CRM that builds records from your email and calendar and lets you shape the data model around how your business works.

These stood out to us on Attio's pricing page:

  • Email and calendar sync comes with every plan, Free included.
  • Objects grow with the plan, from 3 on Free to 5 on Plus and 12 on Pro, and custom objects and formula attributes start on Pro.
  • Call Intelligence records and transcribes Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams calls, and it starts on Pro.
  • The API, webhooks, an MCP server and an App SDK come with every plan, and Attio has iOS and Android apps.

Honestly, Attio is where we'd point a team whose process doesn't fit a standard CRM, like deal flow for an investor. Its startup program also takes up to 80% off for eligible startups, so check if you qualify before you compare prices.

What Omniscient does well

Omniscient is a CRM with a Deal Room for proposals and Contract Management in the same app. The CRM fills itself in from your email, calendar and call transcripts, and on Pro it shows the quote behind every value it writes.

One app covers the path from a first meeting to a signed contract:

  • Connect Gmail or Outlook and your calendar, and each email and meeting lands on the right person, company and deal.
  • Leads from forms, events and imports merge into one record per person, matched by email, with companies matched by domain.
  • The Deal Room builds a proposal for each deal from the deal, your calls and your Library, in your brand, and shows you who viewed each part.
  • Contract Management keeps every version of the contracts you upload, gets your team's approval and collects signatures on the page.

Admittedly, Omniscient is narrower than Attio in a few places. We don't offer bulk sends or a mobile app, and we don't search for new leads, so yours come from your own forms, events and lists.

How each CRM keeps records up to date

Attio fills in what it calls communication intelligence, such as first and last interaction and connection strength. From Plus up, it writes summaries and labels for emails, and on Pro, Call Intelligence turns calls into summaries linked to the right records.

Omniscient saves each email, meeting and transcript to the record, and on Pro it suggests field changes with the quote they came from. You accept or skip each one, and a value someone on your team typed is never changed.

We made suggestions wait for you on purpose. One wrong amount that a manager trusts in a forecast costs more than an empty field, so a value only writes itself when that field's rule allows it.

Proposals and contracts in Attio and Omniscient

This is the widest gap. Attio's pricing page lists no proposal builder or e-signature, so an Attio team writes proposals in a doc or deck tool and sends contracts through a separate signing app.

Whichever tools you pick, two things follow:

  • You pay for each one on top of Attio, and each keeps its own copy of the deal.
  • The deal in Attio updates only as far as each integration carries it, or as far as someone updates it by hand.

In Omniscient, the proposal and the contract read from the same record as the CRM. The Deal Room builds a proposal page for one deal from the deal, your calls and your Library of case studies, decks and videos. The top shows the buyer's goals, your plan and the price, and below it are rows for each person on the buying team.

You see who viewed which part, and when the buyer approves on the page, your team gets an alert.

You can also ask questions across all your contracts, such as which ones renew next quarter, and upload the contracts you already signed so Otto reads their terms for you to confirm. The Contract Management page shows the flow screen by screen.

Email sending: where Attio is ahead

Attio sends email from inside the CRM, one at a time or in bulk, with these limits on its pricing page:

Omniscient reads your mailbox and never deletes or changes anything in it. Otto drafts the next email from the thread, and it goes out from your own account when you click Send.

Email sequences run as Automation workflows on every plan. Each email in a workflow needs a person's approval before Publish, it goes from the owner's own mailbox at a steady pace with a daily cap, and a reply stops the run. Frankly, there's no bulk send, so Attio covers one-off email blasts to a list better.

AI in Attio and Omniscient

Attio prices its AI with credits, and its pricing FAQ says workspace credits pay for both the AI features and workflows. The allowances look like this:

  • Seat credits for Ask Attio come to 100, 500, 1,000 or 2,500 per user a month, from Free to Enterprise.
  • Workspace credits come to 250, 1,500 or 10,000 a month on Free, Plus and Pro, and Enterprise is custom.
  • Extra workspace credits come in packs, starting at 5,000 a month for $70 billed yearly, or $85 billed monthly.

Omniscient puts the CRM's AI in the seat price. Ask Omniscient answers questions about any account in plain English and links each answer to the records it used, and questions across your contracts come in the seat price too.

The one place we use credits is the Deal Room, where each paid seat adds monthly proposal credits and a full proposal uses 40.

Interestingly, each model suits a different team. Credits show what AI costs as usage grows, and AI in the seat leaves one number to plan around.

Which one should you choose

Luckily, the choice mostly comes down to two questions: how your deals close, and how far you need to bend the CRM.

Choose Attio if:

  • Your team sends one-off emails in bulk to lists from inside the CRM.
  • You need custom objects, or a process like deal flow that a standard CRM doesn't fit.
  • Your reps want mobile apps for days away from the desk.

Choose Omniscient if:

  • You send a proposal and a contract for most deals and want both on the CRM record.
  • You want each contract's end date and notice period tracked, with alerts before the notice date.
  • You want records that fill themselves in, with the quote behind each value.
  • You'd rather have the CRM's AI in the seat price than a credit balance to watch.

FAQs

Can Attio build proposals or contracts?

Attio's pricing page lists no proposal builder or e-signature, so Attio teams add other tools for those steps. Omniscient builds proposals in its Deal Room, and Contract Management keeps every version of the contracts you upload and collects signatures on the page.

Is Omniscient cheaper than Attio?

Paid seats start at $19 a month on Omniscient and $35 on Attio, billed yearly. Compare the plan you'd really use: Attio's sequences and call recording start on Pro at $79, and Omniscient's forecast starts on Pro at $49.

Can I move my data from Attio to Omniscient?

Yes. Our importer reads companies, people, deals with their stages, and notes from Attio, or our team migrates you from an export. You recreate custom objects in Omniscient, or send them to us as a spreadsheet.

Can Omniscient read contracts we already signed?

Yes. Upload them as already signed, and Otto reads their terms, including the end date, the notice period and auto-renewal, each with a quote for you to confirm. The owner then gets alerts 90, 60 and 30 days before each notice date.
