What Waalaxy does well

Waalaxy is a LinkedIn outreach tool built for founders and small teams who are new to prospecting. Since July 2026 it runs fully in the cloud, so there's nothing to install and campaigns keep going with your browser closed.

Here's how the three plans compare, per user and per LinkedIn account:

Waalaxy planMonthlyBilled yearlyInvitationsWhat it adds
Pro$19$16300 a month, 11 a dayUnlimited campaigns, sequence templates, CRM sync and 25 email finder credits a month
Advanced$39$32800 a month, up to 100 a day and 200 a weekAPI access, Make, Zapier and n8n modules, and live chat
Business$69$55The same as AdvancedCold email sequences, LinkedIn plus email campaigns and 500 email finder credits a month

The product is shaped around people running outreach for the first time:

  • Quotas set a modest pace for you, and each day's total lands at a random point between 80% and 100% of the maximum.
  • Every plan includes ready sequence templates, automated follow ups, unlimited campaigns and CSV imports and exports.
  • You can import leads from LinkedIn search, Sales Navigator and Recruiter Lite, or bring them in from a CSV.
  • Replies sync to HubSpot or Pipedrive, and Make, Zapier and n8n modules reach 2,000+ other apps.

Honestly, for a founder sending a few hundred invitations a month, that's a tidy package. If Pro's pace suits you and you don't need cold email, staying with Waalaxy is a sensible call.

Why people look for a Waalaxy alternative

Most of the reasons come down to plan limits and where your replies end up:

  • Pro stops at 300 invitations a month, about 11 a day, so any faster pace means moving up to Advanced.
  • Cold email sequences need Business, the most expensive of the three plans.
  • The LinkedIn Inbox, with reply templates, reminders and tags, is a paid add on at €20 a month once its 500 free conversations run out.
  • Replies reach your CRM through a sync, so the CRM stays a second tool with its own bill.
  • The card trial turns into a paid plan unless you cancel in time, and no reminder arrives before the first charge.

Waalaxy's invitation quotas next to our pacing

Waalaxy sets its daily quotas itself and keeps them separate from LinkedIn's weekly limit, so your actions stop when you hit either one. LinkedIn doesn't publish its own number. When you reach it, LinkedIn restricts your invitations for a period that "typically lasts one week."

Interestingly, the two products meet on the weekly ceiling: Waalaxy Advanced and our highest setting both stop at 200 invitations a week. They part ways on the daily peak, where Waalaxy allows up to 100 in one day and our engine stops at 40.

Under that ceiling, our engine shapes each day on its own:

  • Each day's total lands between 80% and 100% of your limit, the same range Waalaxy uses for its daily quotas.
  • A new account starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day, and the warmup starts again after a reconnect or a restriction.
  • Once 20 invitations have gone out in a week, an acceptance rate under 20% drops the account to half pace for 3 days, and a rate limit from LinkedIn does the same. It then climbs back to full over 3 more days.
  • A profile view goes out 1 to 4 hours before each invitation, unless you turn that setting off.
  • Actions keep a random gap of 3 to 12 minutes at the Normal pace, inside your working hours and around a lunch break.

We chose the lower daily peak on purpose. LinkedIn names sending "many invitations within a short amount of time" as one cause of invitation restrictions, so our engine spreads a week's invitations across your working days.

Our sender settings recommend 100 or fewer invitations a week, or up to 150 to 200 with Sales Navigator. Admittedly, the trade is speed. At the Normal pace of 80 a week, a list of 400 people takes at least five weeks to invite, while a tool with a higher ceiling sends faster and carries more of the risk that LinkedIn steps in.

Every tool on this list automates LinkedIn, ours included, and LinkedIn warns that members who use such tools "risk having their accounts restricted or shut down." Our guide to the LinkedIn connection request limit explains where the common weekly numbers come from.

  • Email from the first plan: Basic covers LinkedIn and email, with one sending mailbox per identity.
  • Free teammates: Members who answer replies or prepare campaigns don't need an identity, with 3 included on Basic and 25 on Pro.
  • A full trial: 14 days with no card, covering every feature apart from custom sequences.
  • Room for more email: Pro sends from up to 5 mailboxes per identity and Ultimate from up to 10, which spreads your email volume.

Cons

  • Channels gated by plan: Calls and the shared inbox start on Pro, and X and CRM sync need Ultimate.
  • Basic caps identities: Basic allows up to 3 identities, so a growing team moves up to Pro.
  • One identity in the trial: You can test a single identity until you upgrade.

How to choose a Waalaxy alternative

Start with whatever pushed you away from Waalaxy, then trial the two or three tools that fix it. These questions settle most choices:

  • Count your senders. Per seat pricing suits a team of three, while 25 or more client accounts make flat plans like HeyReach Agency or Skylead Agency cheaper.
  • Decide where email lives. If cold email matters as much as LinkedIn, keep both in one sequence with La Growth Machine, Dripify, Skylead or lemlist.

  • Read the safety defaults. Look for a warmup that runs on its own, a daily ceiling you'd be comfortable hitting and a pace that changes from day to day.
  • Use the trial on a real list. A 7 day trial leaves little time to see who accepts, so launch your test campaign on day one.

FAQs

What is the best alternative to Waalaxy?

Pick by the reason you're leaving. Omniscient fits teams that want outreach and a CRM together, La Growth Machine and lemlist fit people who also email and call, and Meet Alfred is the closest cloud tool with X steps. HeyReach and GetSales fit agencies, and Linked Helper costs the least per account.

Is there a cheaper alternative to Waalaxy?

Waalaxy Pro costs $19 per user a month, or $16 billed yearly. Linked Helper starts at $15 per LinkedIn account a month, Kanbox Starter costs $20 a month or $12 billed yearly, and Omniscient starts at $19 per seat a month billed yearly.

Which Waalaxy alternatives include cold email?

La Growth Machine, Dripify and Skylead put email steps on every plan, and Expandi includes email follow ups in its Business plan. lemlist needs Multichannel for LinkedIn steps, Meet Alfred needs Multi-Channel for email, SalesRobot charges $15 per email account and GetSales sells email as its own module. Omniscient, Kanbox, Dux-Soup and Linked Helper stay on LinkedIn.

Is Waalaxy still a Chrome extension?

No. Since July 1, 2026, Waalaxy runs 100% in the cloud from its web app, and its Chrome extension has left the Chrome Web Store. An optional extension called Alien Copilot speeds up imports.

How many invitations can Waalaxy send per week?

Pro sends up to 11 invitations a day, about 300 a month. Advanced and Business allow up to 100 a day and at most 200 a week, or 800 a month. LinkedIn keeps its own limit private.

Does Waalaxy have a free trial?

Yes. Every plan comes with a 14 day free trial. It needs a credit card and becomes a paid plan when it ends unless you cancel first.
