There's no published LinkedIn connection request limit, so most outreach tools work from an estimate of about 100 invitations a week over a rolling seven days. LinkedIn's help pages do confirm that every member has a limit, Premium members included.

We still had to pick real numbers when we built the LinkedIn sending engine inside Omniscient. You'll find LinkedIn's own wording first, then vendor estimates, then the daily and weekly caps we chose and why.

Plan on 100 invitations a week or fewer, spread over your working days at about 15 to 20 a day. Any figure more exact than that is an estimate, ours included.

What is the LinkedIn connection request limit?

The connection request limit is the number of invitations to connect that one account can send before LinkedIn stops it for a while. LinkedIn's Invitation limit reached page says reaching it may restrict your invitations, and that the restriction typically lasts one week.

The same page says "all LinkedIn members, including those with Basic and Premium accounts, are subject to these limits." It never gives the number and only explains that LinkedIn sets the limits "to protect the overall member experience and ensure that connection requests remain relevant."

Its page on personalizing invitations allows notes on three requests a month without Premium, while its invitation limit page and its InMail comparison page both say five. The hint in our own settings uses three, the lower number, to stay on the safe side.

How many LinkedIn connection requests you can send per week

The weekly figure most guides use is about 100, and several, including FirstSales and Overloop, describe it as a rolling window. In that model, an invitation stops counting seven days after you send it, so nothing resets on Monday morning.

Omniscient counts the same way: it adds up every invitation a sender made in the last seven days and treats your weekly setting as a hard cap.

Keep in mind that our counter only sees the invitations Omniscient sends. If you also invite people by hand in LinkedIn, those count toward LinkedIn's limit too, so lower your weekly setting to leave room for them.

How many connection requests per day on LinkedIn

LinkedIn doesn't publish a daily number either, but its restrictions page names sending "many invitations within a short amount of time" as a cause. So the shape of your day matters as much as the weekly total.

Two accounts can send the same 70 invitations in a week and look nothing alike. One sends all 70 on Monday morning, while the other sends 14 on each weekday between meetings, which looks far more like a person working through a normal week.

Here's how our engine spreads a week at Normal pace:

  • Each day's cap is drawn once, between 80% and 100% of your setting, so a setting of 15 gives a cap of 12 to 14.
  • Invitations go out only in working hours, in the sender's own time zone. The start and end of each day move by up to 20 minutes, and a 30 to 60 minute lunch break falls near the middle.
  • Every action waits a random 3 to 12 minutes after the one before it.
  • A new account warms up. Its daily setting starts at 5 and rises by 2 a day until it reaches yours, with the same 80% to 100% draw on top.

Admittedly, the randomness costs you some volume, since five working days at Normal pace allow only 60 to 70 invitations under the 80 cap. We accepted that gap because the same 15 invitations at the same hour every day doesn't look like anyone's real working week.

LinkedIn connection limits by account type

Paying for LinkedIn changes less than you might hope, since LinkedIn's help pages say Premium members are subject to the invitation limits too. The paid extras they list are about notes and InMail:

LinkedIn Premium is a paid LinkedIn subscription that adds InMail credits and a note on every invitation. Its Premium page offers Career, Business and All-in-One plans, plus a Premium Company Page, with a free trial for eligible members. The page itself lists no prices.

LinkedIn Sales Navigator is a LinkedIn sales software that helps sellers find the right buyers and build relationships with them. Its plans page lists Core at US$119.99 a month, or US$1,079.88 a year, with 50 InMails a month on every plan.

Vendor guides report 150 to 200 invitations a week on older Sales Navigator accounts. LinkedIn hasn't confirmed any figure, so we'd treat that range as an upper bound you work up to.

Interestingly, LinkedIn's comparison page treats InMail as its own message type, apart from invitations. We read that as a way to reach a few important people while your invitations are paused, as long as your plan includes credits.

Other LinkedIn invitation limits to know

Beyond the weekly number, a few other LinkedIn rules decide how many invitations you can really send:

  • LinkedIn's restrictions page says standard and personalized invitation limits are separate, so running out of notes doesn't stop plain invitations.
  • Too many unanswered invitations can restrict you for up to a month, according to the same page.
  • After you withdraw an invitation, you can't invite that person again for up to three weeks, and withdrawing doesn't lift a restriction you already have.
  • Sending too many invitations to people you don't know is the most common reason for a restriction, says LinkedIn's best practices page. Repeated restrictions can leave you "permanently required to enter the recipient's email address for all future invitations."
  • Your whole network tops out at 30,000 first degree connections.

Thankfully, the pending pile is the easiest of these to control, because you decide how long an unanswered invitation stays out. In Omniscient, a sequence waits 14 days for an acceptance by default, and you can set anything up to 30. When that time runs out, it can withdraw the request it sent.

We also check the relationship on LinkedIn right before every invitation, so none goes to someone who's already connected or already invited.

On the LinkedIn senders page in Omniscient's settings, your pending count turns amber above 400, a line we picked as an early warning. LinkedIn publishes no pending number, and 400 sits well below the 1,500 that several vendor guides quote.

If you'd rather clear old invitations yourself, LinkedIn's withdraw an invitation page gives these steps on desktop:

  1. 01
    Open My Network

    Click the My Network icon at the top of your LinkedIn homepage.

  2. 02
    Find your invitations

    In the Invitations section, click Show all if you have invitations to answer, or Manage if you don't.

  3. 03
    Open the Sent tab

    Click Sent under Manage invitations to see the requests you've sent.

  4. 04
    Withdraw

    Click Withdraw next to the invitation, then click Withdraw again to confirm.

The person isn't told when you withdraw, and LinkedIn has no bulk option, so a big backlog takes a while to clear by hand.

What happens when you hit the LinkedIn invitation limit

LinkedIn's restrictions page lists several kinds of restriction, and how long you wait depends on which one you get:

Luckily, LinkedIn says most restrictions lift on their own within one week. These are the steps we'd follow if it happens to you:

  1. Stop all automated sending. In Omniscient, a sender pauses on its own when LinkedIn refuses its actions, and nothing retries around the pause.
  2. Wait it out. LinkedIn says it can't remove invitation restrictions on request, and you can't buy more invitations while one is active.
  3. Clear out old pending invitations once you're back, keeping the three week wait in mind for anyone you withdraw.
  4. Restart slowly. When a restricted sender comes back, Omniscient starts its warmup again from 5 invitations a day.
  5. Look at who you invited. If fewer than 1 in 5 people accepted, a smaller and better matched list will do more for you than a higher limit.

Our engine also checks acceptance every hour once a sender has made 20 or more invitations in the last seven days. If fewer than 20% of them were accepted, it drops to half pace for 3 days, then climbs back to full pace over the next 3. A rate limit from LinkedIn sets off the same slowdown.

Where the vendor LinkedIn limit estimates come from

Four companies that sell outreach software wrote the guides behind our vendor column. Each guide puts the weekly limit at about 100, which LinkedIn hasn't confirmed.

La Growth Machine is a multichannel outreach software that runs sequences on LinkedIn and email, with calls and X on its higher plans. Its Basic plan costs €60 per identity a month, and its limits guide calls 100 a week LinkedIn's official limit, though LinkedIn publishes no such number.

Overloop is an AI sales prospecting software that finds prospects who fit your business and contacts them with email and LinkedIn in one sequence. Starter costs $69 per user a month after a 7-day free trial. Its limits guide is careful to say LinkedIn doesn't publish the number.

FirstSales is a cold email software whose AI agents write each email and follow up until people reply. LinkedIn only appears in Signals, which watches LinkedIn posts to find leads. Starter costs $29 a month with unlimited team members, and its guide explains the rolling seven-day window well.

Topo is an outbound sales software with an AI agent that works beside your reps to find leads and send email and LinkedIn sequences. Its Solo plan costs $89 per seat a month, and we cite its invitation limit guide in the vendor column too.

We'd pick Overloop, La Growth Machine or Topo over Omniscient if you want email and LinkedIn in one sequence. Our default of 80 a week stays below the guides' shared 100 because none of them speaks for LinkedIn.

FAQs

What is the LinkedIn connection request limit per week?

LinkedIn doesn't publish a weekly number for invitations. The figure most outreach vendors report is about 100 invitations a week, counted over a rolling seven days, and our default is 80.

When does the LinkedIn invitation limit reset?

Vendors report a rolling window, so each invitation stops counting seven days after you send it. If you're restricted for reaching the limit, LinkedIn says that restriction typically lasts one week.

Does LinkedIn Premium increase the connection request limit?

LinkedIn's help pages say Premium members are subject to the invitation limits. Premium removes the monthly cap on invitation notes, and one help page allows 300 characters per note. Vendors report higher weekly figures for older Premium and Sales Navigator accounts, which LinkedIn hasn't confirmed.

How many connection requests can I send per day on LinkedIn?

LinkedIn doesn't publish a daily number, and it lists many invitations in a short time as a reason for restrictions. Our default setting is 15 a day, spread across working hours, and vendor guides suggest 10 to 25.

How many pending LinkedIn invitations is too many?

LinkedIn doesn't give a number, only that too many can restrict you for up to a month. Several vendor guides quote a ceiling of about 1,500, and Omniscient flags a sender above 400.
