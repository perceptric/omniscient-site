What Dripify does well

Dripify keeps its plans short and puts a lot into the first one:

  • Pro costs $79 per user a month, or $59 billed yearly, with unlimited drip campaigns, LinkedIn and email steps in one sequence, a dedicated inbox and a native HubSpot integration.
  • Advanced costs $99 per user a month, or $79 billed yearly, and adds team management, A/B tests, step analytics, lead tagging, evergreen leads and advanced LinkedIn protection.
  • Campaigns run on Dripify's servers from a dedicated IP in your location, so your laptop can stay closed.
  • Free LinkedIn accounts work fine, so you don't need Premium or Sales Navigator to start.
  • The 7 day trial gives you Advanced features with no card, and nothing charges when it ends.

Users rate it 4.7 out of 5 across 477 reviews on Capterra. Honestly, email steps on the entry plan are a real plus, since our own tool has none and Waalaxy saves cold email for its top plan. If each rep runs LinkedIn and email from one account, staying with Dripify is a fair call.

Why teams look for a Dripify alternative

Most of the reasons come down to how Dripify bills seats and where your replies end up:

  • Each seat connects one LinkedIn account, so a team of five reps pays for five seats.
  • Team management, A/B tests and advanced LinkedIn protection come only with Advanced, so a growing team ends up on the higher price.
  • HubSpot is the one native CRM, and that sync runs one way, from Dripify to HubSpot. Salesforce, Pipedrive and the rest connect through Zapier, Make or webhooks.
  • Sequences mix LinkedIn and email steps, so a rep who also calls or reaches buyers on X keeps those in another tool.
  • Its ceiling of 75 connection requests a day sits well above the 40 our own engine allows at its highest setting.

This tree walks each reason to the tools we'd look at first:

Dripify's daily limits next to ours

Dripify publishes its daily limits for each LinkedIn account type, and you move them with sliders. The table puts them beside our engine's settings:

LinkedIn keeps its invitation limit private. Reaching it can restrict your invitations, and LinkedIn says that restriction "typically lasts one week." Every number above is a ceiling a vendor picked for itself, ours included.

That week is why we default to 15 a day. A restriction stops every invitation until it lifts, and a slower day costs you far less than a lost week. We also capped our sliders at 40 a day and 200 a week, the top of the range our app suggests for Sales Navigator accounts.

Thankfully, Dripify's sliders go down as easily as they go up. If you stay, these are the changes we'd make first:

  • Move the connection request slider well below 75, closer to a pace you'd keep by hand.
  • Set lower limits yourself during the 7 day trial, since Dripify says the trial "doesn't have any restrictions on daily processing limits."
  • Turn on Activity Control, which Dripify recommends "for the best account safety."
  • Keep pending invitations under 800, as Dripify's weekly limit article advises.

01Omniscient

Omniscient is a CRM with LinkedIn outreach built for small B2B sales teams that want prospecting and deals in one place. Otto, our agent, drafts each message from your own pitch, and nothing goes out until your team approves it. Where Dripify pushes contacts one way into HubSpot, Omniscient is the CRM, and it fills itself in from your email and calendar.

Try Omniscient free →

Key features

  • Your pitch in versions: Otto writes up to three versions of your own pitch for each kind of signal, such as a new role or a job opening, where Dripify fills in profile variables.
  • Held when it adds facts: A version that adds a number, a name, customers or a result you didn't write waits until someone on your team edits or approves it.
  • A preview before go live: Before a campaign starts, you see who it reaches, who's left out and the reason for each one.
  • Automatic warmup: A new account starts at 5 invitations a day and adds 2 a day, and the warmup starts again after a reconnect or a restriction.
  • A profile view first: A profile view goes out 1 to 4 hours before each invitation, unless you turn that setting off.
  • Automatic slowdown: When acceptance drops under 20% or LinkedIn rate limits the account, sending falls to half pace for 3 days.
  • Old requests withdrawn: If an invitation isn't accepted after the days you set, the sequence can withdraw it, which keeps your pending list short.
  • Inbox on the CRM record: Replies from every teammate's sender land in one Inbox, right next to the person's CRM record.

Pros

  • Outreach and CRM together: Replies and deals share one record, so your team never copies a LinkedIn thread into a second tool.
  • A lower ceiling: The Normal pace sends 15 invitations a day and the top setting stops at 40, against Dripify's 75.
  • Approval tied to the text: Editing a message after approval clears that approval, so what sends is what someone read.
  • No double outreach: A person sits in one live campaign at a time, and a reply stops their sequence.

Cons

  • LinkedIn only: Campaigns have no email steps, while Dripify includes email on Pro.
  • No dedicated IP: Omniscient doesn't give each sender its own IP address, which every Dripify account gets.
  • One sender per campaign: Each campaign sends from one LinkedIn account and there's no white label, so an agency rotating dozens of client senders will do better with HeyReach.
  • Newer product: Our LinkedIn outreach is younger than most tools here and has a shorter track record with large sales teams.

Price and plans

You can try Omniscient free for 14 days with no credit card. Prices are per seat, per month, billed yearly:

  • Free: $0 for teammates who only view or log data.
  • Starter: $19 per seat, per month.
  • Pro: $49 per seat, per month.
  • Business: $99 per seat, per month.

Monthly billing costs 20% more. The LinkedIn Outbound page and the CRM page show the rest.

03HeyReach

HeyReach is a LinkedIn automation tool built for agencies and sales teams that run outreach from many LinkedIn accounts at once. It rotates sending between your senders and puts every reply in one unified inbox. Growth costs the same per sender as Dripify Pro on monthly billing, while the Agency plan covers 25 senders for one flat price.

Key features

  • Sender rotation: Connect many LinkedIn accounts and spread one campaign's sending across them.
  • Unified Inbox: Answer every sender's LinkedIn messages in one place, without logging in and out of accounts.
  • LinkedIn steps with logic: Connection requests, messages, voice notes and profile views run in sequences with an "If Connected" condition.
  • Email through partners: Native Instantly, Smartlead and EmailBison integrations add email, and HeyReach finds verified addresses for you.
  • Workspaces and permissions: Keep each client's senders, leads and campaigns apart under a single login.
  • API, webhooks and MCP: Build your own workflows, or let an AI agent build campaigns and tag replies through the MCP server.

Pros

  • Users cost nothing: Teammates, clients and VAs join free, because HeyReach bills only for senders.
  • Flat agency pricing: $999 a month covers 25 senders, with white label, done for you onboarding and a dedicated Slack channel.
  • Proxies on Growth: Each Growth sender gets a dedicated residential proxy.
  • A trial with room to rotate: 14 days with no card and up to 3 LinkedIn accounts, enough to test sender rotation.

Cons

  • No email of its own: Email runs through Instantly, Smartlead or EmailBison, which means a second tool and a second bill.
  • Your own proxies on agency plans: Agency and Unlimited ask you to supply proxies, while Growth includes them.
  • Short list of CRMs: HubSpot, Attio and Breakcold are the direct CRM integrations, so a Salesforce or Pipedrive team would connect through Zapier, Make or the API.

Price and plans

You can try HeyReach free for 14 days with no credit card and up to 3 LinkedIn accounts.

  • Growth: $79 per sender, per month, or $64 billed yearly.
  • Agency: $999 a month for 25 senders, or $800 billed yearly, with a 50 sender option.
  • Unlimited: $2,999 a month, capped at 300 senders under a fair use policy.

How to choose a Dripify alternative

Start with whatever pushed you away from Dripify, then trial the two or three tools that fix it. These questions settle most choices:

  • Count your senders first: Per seat pricing suits a team of three, while 25 or more client accounts make flat plans like HeyReach Agency or Skylead Agency the better deal.
  • Decide where email lives: If you liked having email in the same sequence, Expandi, Skylead, La Growth Machine, lemlist and Closely keep it there.
  • Follow a reply: Ask where a LinkedIn reply lands and who copies it into your CRM, because a native sync or a CRM like Omniscient removes that step.
  • Read the safety defaults: Look for a warmup that runs on its own, daily invitations under 40 and a pace that changes from day to day.
  • Use the trial on a real list: A 7 day trial leaves little time to see who accepts, so launch your test campaign on day one.

Admittedly, no tool here fixes every reason at once, ours included, so rank your reasons before you start a trial.

How to move off Dripify without risking your account

A LinkedIn account carries its pending invitations and recent pace into the new tool, so the first week after a switch needs the most care. We'd make the move in this order:

  1. 01
    Pause every Dripify campaign

    Your plan stays active until the end of the period you've paid for, so stop campaigns yourself before you cancel from Billing.

  2. 02
    Save who you've contacted

    Keep a list of everyone in your Dripify campaigns, so the new tool can skip people who already heard from you.

  3. 03
    Load your do not contact list

    Add customers and anyone who asked not to hear from you before the first campaign.

  4. 04
    Withdraw only what you've given up on

    Dripify suggests keeping pending invitations under 800, and LinkedIn says a withdrawn invitation can't go to the same person again for up to three weeks.

  5. 05
    Restart slower than you ended

    Treat the first week like a warmup even on an old account, and raise limits only once acceptance holds above 20%.

  6. 06
    Test the reply path

    Ask a colleague to answer a test message and confirm that it lands on the right CRM record.

Fortunately, a careful tool covers part of step 2. Our engine confirms the relationship on LinkedIn before every invitation, so someone still holding your old Dripify request isn't sent a second one.

FAQs

What is the best alternative to Dripify?

It depends on why you're leaving. Omniscient fits small teams that want outreach and a CRM together, and SalesRobot is the closest match to Dripify in price and channels. HeyReach and GetSales suit agencies with many senders, La Growth Machine suits reps who also call, and Linked Helper costs the least per account.

Is there a cheaper alternative to Dripify?

Yes. Dripify Pro costs $79 per user a month, or $59 billed yearly. Linked Helper starts at $15 per LinkedIn account a month, Waalaxy at $19 per user a month, Omniscient at $19 per seat a month billed yearly and Kanbox at $20 a month, or $12 billed yearly.

How many connection requests does Dripify send per day?

Dripify allows up to 75 connection requests a day on every plan and LinkedIn account type, and a slider sets it lower. LinkedIn doesn't publish its own limit, and our engine's default is 15 a day.

Does Dripify integrate with Salesforce?

Only through Zapier, Make or webhooks. HubSpot is Dripify's one native CRM integration, and it syncs one way. Expandi, SalesRobot, Closely, Skylead, Dux-Soup and Linked Helper each sync with Salesforce natively.

Which Dripify alternatives include email steps?

Expandi, Skylead, La Growth Machine and Closely include email on their first plan. lemlist adds LinkedIn to email on Multichannel, Meet Alfred adds email on Multi-Channel, Waalaxy on Business, and SalesRobot and GetSales sell email as an add on. HeyReach sends email through Instantly, Smartlead or EmailBison.

Can I run Dripify and another LinkedIn tool on the same account?

We'd avoid it, since two tools add up actions neither one can see. Dripify's help center also suggests using a single tool after a weekly limit notice.
