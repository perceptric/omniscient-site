What Is an Agentic CRM? Definition, Examples and Best Tools

An agentic CRM is a CRM with AI agents built in that do sales work for you, such as finding leads, logging calls, prepping meetings and updating deals. You stay in charge: the agent does the work, and you accept or skip what it suggests.

A classic CRM is a place where your team types in what happened. An agentic CRM reads what happened on its own and acts on it. This guide covers how that works, what the agents do day to day, how vendors charge for them and which tools to try.

Agentic CRM definition

Here is a short way to say it:

An agentic CRM is a CRM where AI agents read every customer conversation and do the busy work of the sale, while people make the calls that matter.

The word "agentic" means the software can take steps toward a goal, beyond answering one question at a time. A chatbot answers "What did Pallet Cloud say about pricing?" An agent can also prep tomorrow's meeting with Pallet Cloud, draft the follow-up and suggest a new close date after the call.

Most CRMs now add some AI. What sets an agentic CRM apart:

  • Agents are part of the core product. They come built in, ready on day one.
  • They work on your real records. Agents read and update the same records your team uses.
  • They show their work. Every change has a source you can check.
  • You stay in charge. Changes wait for you to accept them.

How an agentic CRM works

Every agentic CRM is built a little differently, but most share the same four layers:

  1. Memory: every email, call, meeting and note on the right record.
  2. Skills: fixed playbooks for each job.
  3. Agent: runs the skill on the memory and does the work.
  4. You: accept or skip each change before it lands.

A memory of every conversation

Agents are only as good as what they can read. So an agentic CRM syncs your email, calendar and call notes and puts each one on the right company, person and deal. This shared memory is what lets an agent answer "Where does this deal stand?" with facts instead of guesses.

Skills for each job

A skill is a fixed set of steps for one job, like researching an account or wrapping up a call. Skills keep the work consistent. When two reps ask for a meeting brief, they get the same kind of brief, built the same way.

Agents that do the work

The agent picks the right skill, reads the memory and does the job. It might build a lead list, fill in a deal field or write a follow-up draft.

You stay in charge

Good agentic CRMs show their work. Each change comes with the source behind it, such as a quote from a call, and nothing lands until you accept it. This matters most for numbers a manager trusts, like deal amounts in a forecast.

What an agentic CRM does: examples

The easiest way to understand an agentic CRM is to follow a deal from start to finish. Here is the busy work agents take on at each step.

  1. 01
    Find customers

    You describe your buyer in plain words, and the agent builds a list of matching companies and people with work emails.

  2. 02
    Capture everything

    Emails, meetings and calls land on the right record, and duplicate contacts merge with their history.

  3. 03
    Engage

    The agent drafts the next email from the thread in your voice, and you send it from your own inbox.

  4. 04
    Meet

    Before a call you get a brief on who's coming and what's still open. After it you get notes, action items and suggested deal updates.

  5. 05
    Manage the pipeline

    The agent fills fields like amount and close date with the quote behind each one, for you to accept.

  6. 06
    Review

    Each morning the agent flags deals that need attention and explains why.

None of these steps need a rep to stop and type. That is the real gain:

  • Reps spend more of the day talking to buyers.
  • Managers see a pipeline that stays current.
  • New teammates get the full history of every account on day one.
  • Forecasts rest on facts with a source behind each one.

Agentic CRM vs a classic CRM with AI

Many CRMs have added AI features over the past two years. Here is how an agentic CRM tends to differ:

Classic CRM with AIAgentic CRM
Where data comes fromMostly typed in by repsRead from email, calendar and calls
What the AI doesAnswers questions and writes summariesRuns whole jobs across the sale
How work stays consistentEach person prompts their own wayShared skills for each job
How changes landReps update fields by handAgent suggests changes with proof, you accept
How AI is pricedOften an add-on or creditsVaries: in the seat, per agent or on credits

The line between the two keeps moving, since most vendors are adding agents. When you compare tools, ask a simple question: how much of the sale does the agent run for you on day one?

How agentic CRMs charge for agents

Pricing is where agentic CRMs differ most. Here are the four models you'll see:

  • AI in the seat. Each paid seat includes the agent, so you have one number to plan around.
  • Per agent. You pay for each agent you deploy, and teammates who only look things up can cost nothing.
  • Per user or workspace. A seat or workspace price, with credits for heavier jobs like enrichment and sequences.
  • Credits. Agents and AI features draw from a pool of credits, so the bill tracks how much the agents do.

To pick a model:

  • Choose credits if you want the bill to track how much the agents do.
  • Choose AI in the seat if you want one number to budget.
  • Choose per agent if many teammates only look things up.
  • Run a real week of work in the trial either way, so you see what your team really uses.

FAQs

What is an agentic CRM?

An agentic CRM is a CRM with AI agents built in that do sales work for you, such as finding leads, logging calls, prepping meetings and updating deals. The agents read every customer conversation, and you accept or skip what they suggest.

How is an agentic CRM different from a CRM with AI?

A CRM with AI usually answers questions and writes summaries on top of data your team types in. An agentic CRM reads email, calendar and calls on its own and runs whole jobs across the sale, through shared skills, with each change waiting for you to accept.

What are examples of agentic CRMs?

Omniscient, Attio, Day AI and Lightfield are CRMs built around agents. HubSpot and Salesforce add agents to their platforms through Agent Hub and Agentforce.

Are agentic CRMs safe to use?

The good ones keep you in charge. Look for changes that wait for your approval, a source behind every fact, an agent log you can read and a way to undo any change.

How much does an agentic CRM cost?

It depends on the pricing model. Omniscient starts at $19 per seat a month, billed yearly, with AI in the seat. Day AI has a free plan and agents from $24 a month, Lightfield's Starter is $89 per user a month, and HubSpot, Attio and Salesforce run agents on credits.
