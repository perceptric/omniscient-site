A classic CRM shows what your team found time to type in. It hides most of what happened: the emails, calls and meetings that never reached the deal, and the busy work it takes to log them.

An agentic CRM has AI agents built in that read every customer conversation and do that busy work for you, from finding leads to updating deals. Founders, SDRs, account executives and sales managers use one to keep the pipeline current without the typing. This guide covers how it works, what the agents do, how vendors charge and which tools to try.

You stay in charge, and you get more of the day back for selling. ⚡

Agentic CRM definition

An agentic CRM is a CRM where AI agents read every customer conversation and do the busy work of the sale, while people make the calls that matter.

The word "agentic" means the software can take steps toward a goal, beyond answering one question at a time. A chatbot answers "What did Pallet Cloud say about pricing?" An agent can also prep tomorrow's meeting with Pallet Cloud, draft the follow-up and suggest a new close date after the call.

Most CRMs now add some AI. What sets an agentic CRM apart:

  • Agents are part of the core product. They are ready on day one.
  • They work on your real records. Agents read and update the same records your team uses.
  • They show their work. Every change has a source you can check.
  • You stay in charge. Changes wait for you to accept them.

How an agentic CRM works

Every agentic CRM is built differently, but most share the same four layers.

A memory of every conversation

Agents are only as good as what they can read. So an agentic CRM syncs your email, calendar and call notes and puts each one on the right company, person and deal, which lets an agent answer with facts instead of guesses.

Skills for each job

A skill is a fixed set of steps for one job, like researching an account or wrapping up a call. When two reps ask for a meeting brief, they get the same kind of brief.

Agents that do the work

The agent picks the right skill, reads the memory and does the job, such as a lead list or a follow-up draft.

You stay in charge

Each change comes with the source behind it, such as a quote from a call, and nothing lands until you accept it. This matters most for numbers a manager trusts, like deal amounts in a forecast.

What an agentic CRM does: examples

The easiest way to understand an agentic CRM is to follow one deal and the busy work agents take on at each step.

  1. 01
    Find customers

    You describe your buyer in plain words, and the agent builds a list of matching companies and people with work emails.

  2. 02
    Capture everything

    Emails, meetings and calls land on the right record, and duplicate contacts merge with their history.

  3. 03
    Engage

    The agent drafts the next email from the thread in your voice, and you send it from your own inbox.

  4. 04
    Meet

    Before a call you get a brief on who's coming and what's still open. After it you get notes, action items and suggested deal updates.

  5. 05
    Manage the pipeline

    The agent fills fields like amount and close date with the quote behind each one, for you to accept.

  6. 06
    Review

    Each morning the agent flags deals that need attention and explains why.

None of these steps need a rep to stop and type. Reps spend more of the day with buyers, and managers see a pipeline that stays current, with a source behind each fact.

Most vendors are adding agents, so ask one question when you compare: how much of the sale does the agent run on day one?

How agentic CRMs charge for agents

Pricing is where agentic CRMs differ most, with four models:

  • AI in the seat. Each paid seat includes the agent.
  • Per agent. You pay for each agent, and teammates who only look things up can cost nothing.
  • Per user or workspace. A seat or workspace price, with credits for heavier jobs.
  • Credits. Agents and AI features draw from a pool of credits.

Choose AI in the seat for one number to budget, per agent if many teammates only look things up, and credits if you want the bill to track agent work. Either way, run a real week of work in the trial.

FAQs

What is an agentic CRM?

An agentic CRM is a CRM with AI agents built in that do sales work for you, such as finding leads, logging calls, prepping meetings and updating deals. The agents read every customer conversation and work through shared skills, and you accept or skip each change they suggest, so people still make the calls that matter.

How is an agentic CRM different from a CRM with AI?

An agentic CRM runs whole jobs across the sale, while a CRM with AI mostly answers questions and writes summaries on top of data your team types in. The agentic CRM reads email, calendar and calls on its own, works through shared skills so every rep's work stays consistent, and waits for you to accept each change.

How much does an agentic CRM cost?

An agentic CRM's cost depends on its pricing model. Omniscient puts AI in the seat, from $19 per seat a month billed yearly. Day AI has a free plan and agents from $24 a month, and Lightfield's Starter is $89 per user a month, billed monthly. HubSpot, Attio and Salesforce run agents and AI features on credits.

What features should a modern agentic CRM include?

A modern agentic CRM should capture email, calendar and calls on the right records, run shared skills for each job, and have agents that find leads, prep meetings and update deals. Every change should show its source, wait for your approval and be easy to undo. Omniscient adds one shared memory your team and agents can ask anything.

Which agentic CRM is recommended for teams that need more than AI features?

For teams that need more than AI features, the agentic CRM we'd recommend is Omniscient. It is one agentic CRM for the whole sales motion, with Otto finding leads, capturing every conversation and keeping deals current, and AI in every paid seat. Large teams already on Salesforce may prefer Agentforce, which adds agents to the CRM they use.
